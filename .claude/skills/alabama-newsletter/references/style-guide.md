# Alabama Newsletter — Style Guide

## Voice (Morning Brew style)
- Smart friend over coffee: conversational, quick and a little witty, never
  smug.
- Short paragraphs (1–3 sentences), bold lead-ins, scannable sections.
- Translate jargon at once: "cloture (the vote to end debate)."
- Each policy item answers three questions: What happened? Why does it matter
  to Alabama? What can I do?
- Humor goes at situations and absurdities, never at readers or voters.

## Persuasion principles (bringing people toward the middle)
- Start from values the audience already holds: faith, family, hard work,
  local control, fiscal responsibility, fairness. Then show how a policy helps
  or hurts those values.
- Use concrete local stakes: "Rural hospitals in Greene and Pickens counties…"
  beats abstractions.
- Let the record speak. Quote the bill, show the vote and the dollar impact,
  and the reader reaches the conclusion.
- Hold everyone to one standard. Give credit where it's due, including to
  Republicans, and criticize Democrats where it's warranted.
- No labels as arguments. Never call a person or policy "socialist," "woke,"
  "radical," etc. as a put-down; say what the policy does.

## Length and format
- About a 5-minute read (900–1,300 words).
- Subject line under 50 characters, curiosity plus specificity; the preheader
  adds the second hook.
- Every item ends with a source link. Put the full source list at the bottom.

## Recurring section names (editable)
The Rundown · What Have You Done for Me Lately? · Word of the Week ·
Around the State · Opportunity Board · Quick Hits
