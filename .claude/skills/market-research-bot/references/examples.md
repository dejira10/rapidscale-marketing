## Example 1: Commercial roofing contractors

**User:** Commercial roofing contractors

### Opening insight

Commercial roofing contractors face a unique challenge: They can have millions of dollars in revenue, crews on payroll, and expensive equipment sitting in the yard — yet still struggle to generate predictable profits.

Unlike residential roofers, commercial contractors often pursue larger projects with longer sales cycles, multiple decision-makers, competitive bidding, and substantial upfront labor and material costs.

Their biggest desire isn't necessarily more leads.

They want more profitable commercial roofing projects, fewer wasted estimates, and a predictable pipeline that keeps their crews working without constantly chasing general contractors or waiting for the next storm.

Below is a detailed breakdown of their everyday problems, frustrations, buying motivations, and marketing opportunities.

These are industry-informed hypotheses and representative contractor language, not direct interview quotations or verified survey findings.

### 1A. Problems in their words (excerpt)

A. Payroll, overhead, and cash flow

"We're doing $5 million a year, but where's all the money?" Revenue looks impressive, but payroll, insurance, equipment, materials, fuel, and financing consume most of it.

"I've got $40,000 in payroll coming Friday, and we're still waiting on checks." Property owners or general contractors take weeks or months to pay, while employees expect payment every week.

"We're financing our customers' roofs." The contractor pays for materials and labor long before receiving final payment.

"One bad job can wipe out the profit from three good ones." Underestimated labor, hidden roof damage, change-order disputes, and callbacks destroy margins.

"Everybody gets paid before I do." The owner takes home less than expected despite carrying the financial risk of the entire company.

### 3. TIMER frustrations

TIME — "I'm spending more time chasing work than running jobs."

Estimators spend hours on proposals that never close. Owners drive between properties, follow up on bids, and chase decision-makers instead of managing operations.

IDENTITY — "I'm a roofing contractor, not a salesman."

They built their reputation by solving complicated roofing problems. They don't want to spend their days cold calling property managers or learning social media marketing.

MONEY — "We're winning jobs, but the margins aren't there."

Competitive bidding pressures pricing. Wasted estimating time, expensive leads, and idle crews erode profitability.

ENERGY — "I'm exhausted from not knowing what's coming next."

They move from overloaded schedules to scrambling for work. Constant uncertainty makes hiring, purchasing, and planning difficult.

REPUTATION — "I don't want to look desperate for work."

Contractors want to be respected as experienced commercial roofing specialists, not perceived as aggressive storm chasers or bargain-priced bidders.

### 4. Unasked questions (excerpt)

4. Questions they're thinking but rarely ask

These are excellent starting points for marketing hooks, lead magnets, and sales conversations.

"How do I get directly in front of commercial property owners instead of competing for every general contractor bid?"

"How do I find buildings that are actually due for a roof replacement?"

"How do I get more $100,000–$500,000 projects without constantly bidding against the cheapest guy?"

"How can I keep my crews working when storms aren't bringing in calls?"

"How do I know if a property manager actually has the budget to replace a roof?"

"Can I get more work from existing commercial customers without constantly following up myself?"

"How do I get into warehouses, distribution centers, manufacturing facilities, and large retail properties?"

"How do I build a pipeline of roof replacements six months before the work is needed?"

"How can I stop wasting estimator time on projects that will never happen?"

"Can marketing bring me actual building owners rather than residential leads?"

"How do I grow without hiring another full-time salesperson?"

"How do I avoid being dependent on two or three general contractors for most of my revenue?"

The deeper question: "How do I build a commercial roofing company that isn't constantly one slow month or one delayed project away from cash flow problems?"

## Example 2: Dentists

**User:** Dentists

### Opening insight

Target audience: Independent dental practice owners, general dentists, and multi-location dental groups in the United States.

The biggest opportunity when marketing to dentists is understanding one important distinction:

Most dentists don't simply want more patients. They want more of the right patients, for the right procedures, without increasing their stress, overhead, or working hours.

A dentist with a full schedule of low-reimbursement procedures can be less profitable than one with fewer appointments and a healthier mix of high-value treatments.

Below is a detailed breakdown of their everyday problems, how they currently attract patients, what frustrates them, and what they secretly want from a marketing company.

These are research-based working hypotheses and realistic examples of dentist language, not verified interview quotes. Individual practices will vary.

### 3. TIMER frustrations

3. Frustrations with their current marketing

Using the TIMER framework — Time, Identity, Money, Energy, Reputation — here is how these frustrations show up.

Time

"I don't have time to manage Google Ads, follow up with leads, and check whether my receptionist called everyone back. I'm already seeing patients all day."

Identity

"I went to dental school to treat patients, not become a salesperson or marketing manager."

Money

"My marketing company says we're getting leads, but I can't tell how many actually become paying patients."

Energy

"After eight hours of dentistry, the last thing I want is another meeting about keywords, clicks, and website traffic."

Reputation

"I don't want cheesy ads making my practice look like a discount dental clinic. I've spent years building my reputation."
