# Output Format (observed in both test runs)

> **Observation notes, not the format to follow.** The locked format is `template.md`. The GPT's two runs differed in their title ("Market Research: Commercial Roofing Contractors" vs "Dental Practice Market Research"), section 2 and 9 headings, table columns, the package title, and whether a core-offer block appeared. The template fixes one version.

The same structure appeared for commercial roofing contractors (run 1) and dentists (run 2). `[industry]` means the business type the user gave.

## Part A: Market research report

**Header:** "Market Research: [industry]", plus a one-line target-market label, e.g. "Target market analysis · B2B commercial roofing" or "Target audience: independent dental practice owners, general dentists, multi-location groups in the US."

**Opening insight (2–4 short paragraphs):** the one distinction that matters most about this market. Examples:

- "Their biggest desire isn't necessarily more leads. They want more profitable projects, fewer wasted estimates, and a predictable pipeline."
- "Most dentists don't simply want more patients. They want more of the right patients, for the right procedures, without more stress or overhead."

**Honesty line:** "These are industry-informed hypotheses and representative language, not direct interview quotations or verified survey findings."

| # | Section | What it contains |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Problems they face** | Grouped by theme (A, B, C…), e.g. cash flow; idle capacity; too many small or low-value jobs; inconsistent pipeline; tire kickers. Each problem is a **quote in their voice** plus a one-line explanation. Often a table of "unqualified prospect types → what happens." Ends with a **marketing insight** line |
| 2 | **How they currently get customers** | A table: channel → how it works (referrals, partners or GCs, bidding, cold calling, door knocking, events and associations, Google, past-customer databases, manufacturers, public bids…). Ends with the observation that they usually rely on a few relationships rather than a repeatable system |
| 3 | **What frustrates them about their current process (TIMER)** | **TIME / IDENTITY / MONEY / ENERGY / REPUTATION**, each with a one-line quote ("I'm a roofing contractor, not a salesman") and 1–2 sentences |
| 4 | **Questions they're thinking but rarely ask** | 10–12 questions in their voice, ending with "**The deeper question:** …" |
| 5 | **Competitors that concern them** | National or large players (only if confident they exist), local competitor *types*, and online platforms or channels. Note that bid and lead platforms create price pressure |
| 6 | **Their most desirable projects, services and customers** | What they'd love more of (job types, sizes, customer types). Numbers are labeled "illustrative, not verified averages" |
| 7 | **Fears about hiring a marketing agency** | Past bad experiences: junk leads, long contracts, no transparency, generic work, looking cheap |
| 8 | **The strongest marketing opportunities or angles** | 3+ numbered angles, each with a **target outcome** and **why it resonates** |
| 9 | **Messaging that speaks to their pain / what this means for your offer** | A table: "generic agency message → stronger [industry] message," then "**The positioning I'd test first:**" one sentence |

**Close of Part A:** "Next: turn this research into marketing that gets responses." It offers 3 handraisers, 1 Facebook ad and website copy, then asks **what service you offer [industry]**, with options (Google Ads / PPC, Facebook / Instagram ads, SEO / Google Maps, cold email / outbound, appointment setting, full-service, other).

## Part B: Marketing package (after the service is known)

**Header:** "[Industry] Marketing Package":

- "Your service: …"
- "Your strongest positioning: …" (e.g. "You don't sell roofing leads. You find opportunities, confirm the need, and introduce the contractor to the person responsible.")
- **Core offer:** a headline plus two sentences.
- **A note** that the copy avoids invented results and guaranteed timelines.

### 1. Three handraiser posts

Each has a **symptom label**, a short name and a "Recommended first test" tag on one of them. Structure:

1. **Audience call-out:** "Commercial roofing contractors…" / "Any dentists here doing implants…"
2. **A symptom question in their words:** "Got a crew wrapping up a job soon, but not enough work lined up behind it?"
3. **3–5 short lines** that show you understand: "The guys are ready to work. The trucks are sitting there…"
4. **"What if…"** the better situation.
5. **What we do,** in plain words (one or two sentences).
6. **"No [annoying thing]" lines:** "No driving around industrial parks. No cold calling property managers in the evening."
7. **"Just…"** the simple outcome.
8. **A one-word comment CTA:** "Comment ROOFS or send me a message." / "Comment IMPLANTS below."
9. **Target outcome:** one line on what a successful reply leads to.

Then: "**Which handraiser should you test first?**" A recommendation, plus why it's the most defensible promise.

### 2. Facebook ad

- **Angle line:** e.g. "Angle: stop chasing unqualified commercial roofing bids."
- **Body:**
  - a capitalized audience call-out;
  - a symptom story;
  - the cost of the problem;
  - "Here's a different way";
  - "We:" with 3–4 bullets;
  - **what the first conversation looks like** ("Here's the building. Here's the roofing issue. Here's the person who wants to talk.");
  - a removal line;
  - "You focus on… / We focus on…";
  - a CTA to a named review or consult ("request a Commercial Roofing Opportunity Review").
- **Setup table:** headline, description, CTA button, landing page.
- **Image suggestion:** problem vs outcome (e.g. a split screen), an image headline and a supporting line.

### 3. Complete website copy

1. **Eyebrow:** "FOR [INDUSTRY]."
2. **Headline:** "Go From [current pain] to [desired outcome], Using Our [mechanism]."
3. **Subhead:** "Stop [three pains]…" plus one line on what you do, then "You handle [their work]. We handle [your part]."
4. **[Watch the video below]**, plus a suggested video topic.
5. **[CTA button]:** "Request your [named review]." Repeated after each section.
6. **"Does this sound like you? The Old Way."** 6–8 symptom bullets, then "So what do you do?" plus 4–6 things they currently try, then "There's a better way."
7. **"The New Way":** "Imagine…" plus what they'd receive, then **Step 1–5** of how it works.
8. **Case studies:** 3 titled layouts, **clearly placeholders**, not claims.
9. **FAQ:** 4–6 real objections (e.g. "How is this different from buying leads?", "Are these owners ready to sign?", "How much do I need to spend on ads?").
10. **About Us:** "About [Your Business Name]," plus 3 numbered principles.

### 4. Extras that appeared

- **Run 1:** "Make your offer easier to say yes to" (offer structure); "What I would test first" (a recommended initial campaign); a compliance note on email and privacy; and a closing recommendation ("Sell the quality of the introduction, not the value of an imaginary future contract").
- **Run 2:** recommended campaign structure; offer economics; one improvement to the offer (a small, low-risk pilot); and a final clarifying question ("Do you only generate leads, or do you also book the consultations?").
