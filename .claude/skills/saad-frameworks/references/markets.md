# Saad — Picking Markets, Signals and Lists

Saad's edge is choosing industries nobody would think to go into. He goes
deep into the data those industries are forced to publish. This file covers how
he chooses, what he looks for and where he gets the lists.

## 1. Selection lenses

Use these as a checklist. A strong lane passes several.

1. **Hidden or "ghost" industries.** They move serious money but are invisible
   to people starting businesses online. Examples: food import, honey, airport
   paving, kennels, daycare meals [S02, S06, S07, S10].
   - Few people pitch them, so it's "easier." [S07]
   - Contacts are often on Gmail or Yahoo, so they "never get pitched." [S03,
     S10, S16]
2. **Forced spending.**
   - Industries legally required to spend, like food safety testing,
     certification and audits [S01].
   - Batch testing required by law [S15].
   - "Every time someone is forced to pay something, somebody else wants that
     money. And the person in the middle always gets paid more than both." [S01]
3. **Government money.** "Always ask yourself, what kind of businesses does the
   government itself give money to, and work backwards." [S06]
   - Airport improvement funds [S06].
   - E-Rate school internet [S09].
   - CACFP daycare meals [S03].
   - USDA rural loan guarantees [S38].
   - Public funding usually means public bids, and public bids mean public
     losers.
4. **Sell to the losers.** A contractor that just lost a public bid is primed:
   "Hey, you just lost an airport. Here's another airport that's about to buy.
   Do you want it?" [S06, S09]
5. **Penalty-based markets** [S33, S41, S50]. The formula:
   1. **Scrapable public shame.** The government publishes the company, the
      violation, the date and the penalty.
   2. **Clock pressure.** There's a hard response deadline.
   3. **Can't fix it alone.** It needs an outside specialist.
   4. **The specialists are bad at getting clients.** They have no outbound.

   His lanes: EPA enforcement → remediation firms [S33]; FDA warning letters
   (CGMP) → remediation consultants [S41]; OSHA citations → safety consultants
   [S50]; KYC rules → KYC providers [S49]. He calls it a "macro market" series
   (FDA, SBA, OSHA) [S50].
6. **Permanent demand.** These are consumables bought weekly, forever: coffee
   beans for cafés [S08], food for kennels [S10], cleaning between guests [S16].
   Every business in the category is a live buyer, so no trigger is needed.
   "Rare and lucrative." [S08]
7. **"What had to happen before this?"** Keep descending from the visible event
   to the hidden one that moves the money [S13].
   - Example: TikTok product → manufacturer → founder choosing a manufacturer →
     trademark filed.
   - "A beauty brand is born on paper before it's born in a bottle."
8. **Timing window.** Find buyers before they've chosen a supplier [S04, S13].
   - "Don't find gyms, find gyms before they become gyms," using "coming soon"
     and presale pages [S04].
   - Use license dates for daycares [S03] and trademark filings for brands
     [S13].
9. **"Vibe check."** Pick an industry that already spends money to get what it
   wants and invoices for work, not one paid only on close [S14]. Studios bill
   upfront, so they don't resist paying upfront [S17]. Some industries are
   already used to paying brokers [S10, S11, S12].
10. **Four market ingredients** [S40] (market-agnostic):
    - demand spikes (they need help now);
    - multiple suppliers competing for that client;
    - a high cost of delay;
    - timing asymmetry: "nobody knows who needs who right now except you."
11. **Saturated isn't bad.**
    - In a saturated market, a different pitch acts as "category disturbance."
      [S05, S11]
    - "Saturated means validated." [S22]
12. **From your own life.** Several of his lanes came from personal problems:
    - dog boarding [S10];
    - a pharmacy out of melatonin [S14];
    - owning a coffee shop [S08].
13. **Reading.** He reads newsletters and annual reports every morning to spot
    two markets that need each other [S41].

### Direction rule

Start from what both sides already want. Don't start from what you can sell
(AI, meetings) and then hunt for an industry to force it on. He lost time and
money doing it backwards [S02, S07, S10]. The daily question: "Do people
actually need what I sell?" [S03]

### Which side pays

- **The side that already spends to acquire customers pays.** "In what planet
  will a host pay $3,000 for access to cleaners? Cleaners would." [S16]
  Examples: equipment dealers, labs, staffing agencies, studios, wholesalers.
- **The scarce side doesn't pay, and the abundant side does.** There are about
  135 farms per lab, so farms pay [S15].
- **Pitch the side that needs the other more.** [S21]
- **Both sides can pay for different services:** access for supply, sourcing
  for demand [S01, S08, S37]. See `offers-and-pricing.md`.

## 2. Signals: what changed, and the pain it creates

"A signal is something that just changed within a company. That change creates
problems; problems need money spent; whoever shows up first gets the money." A
connector doesn't solve the problem. They find who can [S49].

| Signal | Translation (his) | Who can solve it |
|---|---|---|
| Got funded | "Under pressure to make that money count": investors expect ROI [S49]. Window: 30–90 days post-funding [S58, S60] | Recruiters, agencies, partners, advisors |
| Hiring more people | "More work than before, and it's starting to hurt" [S49] | Recruiters, staffing agencies, outsourced services |
| Many open roles or the same role posted repeatedly | "Literally in pain." 37 open posts is a strong signal [S49] | Recruiters |
| Important hire joined | "Something wasn't working in that department" [S49]. A new CMO has 90 days to prove themselves [S58] | Agencies for that department |
| New product launch | New problems in distribution, sales, support and ops [S49] | Matching providers |
| Market grew | More demand → stress → things break [S49] | Ops and delivery providers |
| New license, permit or registration | Something just opened and hasn't picked suppliers yet [S03, S12, S16] | Vendors to that new business |
| Trademark filed | A brand about to need manufacturing, packaging, fulfillment, compliance and ads [S13, S17] | Manufacturers, studios |
| Lost a public bid | The bidder has capacity and needs the next job [S06, S09] | The next buyer |
| Enforcement action or citation | Public shame plus a deadline [S33, S41, S50] | Remediation and compliance specialists |
| Backorder or drug shortage | The primary supplier can't deliver [S14] | Secondary wholesalers |
| Liquidity event (exit) | Needs wealth structuring [S23, S32]. **Privacy-sensitive; see the flags** | Wealth managers |

Funding and hiring cover "90% of the time"; expansion covers some of the rest
[S49]. Stacking two pains on the same company is stronger, e.g. funded in the
last 60–90 days *and* a new CMO in the last 30–90 days [S58].

## 4. List building rules

- **Volume.**
  - About 100 per side is often enough [S05, S07]; up to 1,000 per side [S01].
  - About 50–65 high-intent records can be enough [S39, S41].
  - "Volume is not king." [S38]
  - 100 × 100 gives 10,000 possible pairs, and you only need one [S10].
- **Start with the person.** Use titles that sign deals [S31]:
  - CEO, founder, chief business officer;
  - head of partnerships, VP business development, corporate development.
- **Fit the title to the lane.**
  - Choose the budget holder over HR. For warehouse staffing that's the
    operations manager [S39].
  - Penalty lanes use a fallback order: CEO → Director of EHS → VP Operations →
    Plant Manager → Safety Manager [S50].
- **Exclude noise.** Drop staffing, consulting, marketing, HR and outsourcing
  from industry results ("damage control") [S31]. Filter scraped results
  afterwards, because scrapers return junk (a "bicycle repair technician" for a
  software engineer search) [S49].
- **Company size.**
  - 11–200 is his common band: under 10 is a "science project," and over 200 is
    already partnered [S31].
  - Use revenue ≥$1M where available [S31, S49].
- **Verify every email**, even when Apollo says "verified." Send only to valid,
  never to risky. Bounces hurt deliverability [S01, S36, S39].
- **The email can be real and still be the wrong person.** Check fuzzy company
  matches for wrong geography or entity [S33].
- **Keep a ledger** so nobody gets emailed twice [S15, S16]. Re-run the pull
  weekly for new openings (the "money loop") [S03, S09, S14].
- **Pull extra contacts** to allow for verification drop-off [S15].
- **Pick one state or city** to dominate and build a brand there [S38]. Match
  geographically [S03].

state-legal, and flag it to the user before using [S15].

one case "broke my heart" [S50]. Never use the incident for emotional leverage
in copy. Name the regulatory situation factually.
