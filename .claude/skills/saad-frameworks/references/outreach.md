# Saad — Outreach Copy, Sequences and Replies

All copy below is Saad's own wording from the sources, with names generalized
into `{placeholders}`. Before reusing a line, check that every claim in it is
true for the user's situation (see `voice-and-exclusions.md`).

## 1. The copy formula (two sides, three moves)

"Never change" this [S18, S50]:

| Side | Move 1 | Move 2 | Move 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Supply** (sellers) | I have relevant demand | I'm not selling you leads / not randomly sending this | I'm checking you have capacity before I route anyone |
| **Demand** (buyers) | I have a relevant supplier | I'm not throwing vendors at you | I'm checking the issue is still active before I route anyone |

**Principles:**

- **"He who has the thing names it plainly."** Lead with what *they* want: "I
  have X." [S01]
- **"The most boring message you can send" works.** No personalization trick,
  no "noticed you were walking your dog last week." [S04, S11]
- **Specific public facts are the personalization**: the license date, the
  permit, the lost bid, the request date [S03, S06, S09].
- **Use the industry's own words**, such as "wholesale accounts," "crews,"
  "residents," "turnover," "placements" or "accounts." [S08, S11, S12, S14, S16]
- **Put the offer in the CTA** so they can answer yes or no. "Want me to put one
  or two in front of you?" [S05]
- **Check capacity on the supply side**: "do you have room for one more job?"
  [S05, S12]
- **"I do this independently. I don't represent them."** This pre-empts "who
  are you working for?" [S07]
- **Tie the CTA to money**, e.g. overtime at 1.5×, empty beds [S11].
- **A researched market stat signals insight**, e.g. honey prices up 27%, or
  bulk buyers paying $2.45 a pound [S07].
- **Social proof is optional**, and he often deletes it on purpose [S01, S02].
- **"I'd rather be vaguely right than precisely wrong."** [S05]
- **Open with movement, not identity.** "I'm tracking X entering active trouble
  windows." [S41]
- **Give them a clear role:** one side, the other side, and "I'm the bridge."
  [S41]
- **Don't over-close.** Ask permission to continue, not for a call: "If X is
  relevant on your side right now, I can send the details." [S41]
- **The skeleton:** "I see a real event. I sit between the two sides of that
  event. If this is live for you, I can show you something." [S41]
- "People love to feel chosen, not blasted." "The job of the copy is to create
  enough realness that the next step feels stupid not to take." [S41]

## 3. Follow-ups

- **Supply follow-ups:**
  - "Quick check: more brands filed this week before I keep reaching out.
    Didn't want to keep you if new accounts aren't in your lane right now. Do
    you have capacity for new brand accounts?" [S13]
  - "Quick check: two more EPA-driven situations came in this week before I keep
    routing." [S33] Use only if true.
  - "Hey {first}, do you have capacity for new engagements right now?" [S33,
    S50]
- **Demand follow-ups:**
  - "Leaving the door open. When you're ready to get it made, I'm one reply
    away." [S13]
  - "When's the next harvest coming down? Who's testing it?" [S15]
  - "Worth asking again: is turnover sorted, or still a headache?" [S16]
  - "Hey {first}, are your regulars happy with the coffee, or are you still
    dialing in the beans?" [S08]
  - "When your wholesaler says it's on backorder, what do you do?" [S14]
  - "Was this relevant timing-wise, or should I circle back later in the year?"
    [S41]
- **Short bumps:** "Hey {first}, worth the intro?" [S39]; "Let me know if this is
  worth your time." [S41]
- **Breakups:**
  - "Door stays open." [S50]
  - "Seems like timing isn't right now, which is completely fine. I'll close
    this out on my end so I don't clutter your inbox. If priorities change
    later, feel free to reach out." [S41]
  - "Looks like you've already got this taken care of. If not, let me know."
    [S39]
  - "If it's not the right time, one word back and I'll stop." [S02]
- **Sequence length**: usually 3–4 steps [S02, S13, S33, S50].
- **Evidence, not "checking in"**, after a ghost [S18]:

  > "Hey, I had two companies come through this week looking for exactly your
  > profile, one in X and one in Y. I'm routing one to another operator, but I
  > wanted to flag that demand is active in your space."

  Use only if it's true. "Come back with evidence, not 'are you still
  interested?'"
- **After you send a payment link, don't keep following up.** If they have the
  need, they'll reply [S11].

## 4. Matching is many-to-many, messaging is one-to-one

- Never flood one supplier with 20 messages for 20 demands. Send one message:
  "I'm seeing companies like X with {signal}." [S37]
- **Reveal incrementally** [S37]:
  - to demand, say "I know someone," but hold the provider's identity;
  - to supply, say "companies like X," but hold the list.
  - Reveal after they've said yes and paid.
- **Never send names before payment.** "Be uncuttable." You can mention a first
  name, never the company [S06, S10, S11, S13].

## 5. Replies

**Speed:** reply fast. "Speed creates luck." [S15]

**Watermelon rule** for working out a reply [S01]. Write down:

1. what they said;
2. the "watermelon version": what they're really asking, as if two people were
   selling watermelons;
3. the exact reply.

**Reply formula** (three things) [S03]:

1. Say what they told you back to them, mirroring their words.
2. Don't hand anything over yet: no names, no menus, no prices by email when a
   call is the plan.
3. Give two time options.

Example: "Perfect, {first}. Sounds like there could be a fit. I'm speaking with
a few daycares around DFW right now. Can we do a quick 10-minute call so I
understand what size centers you like, pricing, and how your delivery works?
Are you free at {t1} or {t2}?" [S03]

**Exception:** when they ask your price directly, answer directly. "Never hedge,
never redirect to a call to discuss pricing." [S10, S11]

**Other reply lessons:**

- **Read what they fear.** A buyer who offers cash-in-advance on the first PO
  fears running out more than price. That's why access is worth the fee [S07].
- **Match their tone.** A formal exporter gets a formal reply [S02].
- **Ask when they're free.** Don't push your calendar first [S41].
- **If a requirement is publicly verifiable, verify it yourself** (e.g. MSA
  status in a public filing). Asking stalls the deal [S12].
- **When both sides confirm, the research is over.** Push the transaction
  forward. More data-pulling kills deals [S15, S16].
- **When interest is real, prospects do labor**: they clarify, propose times
  and send invites [S41].
- **Hold frame**, and don't chase. "When you force a transaction, it runs away."
  [S10, S50]

## 6. Sending settings

Details are in `stack.md`.

- Text-only first email, no HTML [S02, S03].
- Schedule 7:00am–7:30pm, Monday–Saturday (he sometimes includes weekends)
  [S01, S02, S50].
- Daily limit ~500–600 [S01]; ~30 for a small list [S07]; up to 1,000 [S13].
- Different sender names on outreach inboxes are fine. If asked: "that inbox
  handles initial outreach. I'm the founder, jumping in here." [S13]
- Move to your own Gmail or Workspace once it's commercial [S05, S12].
