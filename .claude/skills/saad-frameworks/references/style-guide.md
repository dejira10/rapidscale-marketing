# Saad-Informed Tone, Voice & Communication Style Guide

Source: compiled from Saad's 61 transcripts (S01–S61). Unlike Frankie's guide,
which the user supplied, this one was written for this repo. Every rule carries
the source tags it's based on. Examples marked **(illustrative)** were written
for this guide; they aren't Saad quotes or real results. Examples marked with a
source tag are Saad's own wording.

**This guide is the rulebook for every Saad-style draft.** Where it's stricter
than a transcript, it wins. That covers profanity, honesty and status games.

## 1. Core voice

Write like a calm, well-informed operator who sits between two sides of a
market. You've already seen the opportunity, and you're checking whether it's
real for this person before you route anything to them.

The voice should feel:

- **Plain.** "He who has the thing names it plainly and the market pays him for
  it." [S01]
- **Short.** Reply in one line where one line works. Long explanations are a
  mistake [S13].
- **Unhurried and unattached.** "Place it and then let go." You don't need the
  outcome [S02]. "When you force a transaction, it runs away." [S10]
- **Observant, not salesy.** You're reporting what you see in a market, not
  pitching a service [S12, S41].
- **Selective.** You're checking fit before you send anyone [S18, S50].
- **Honest and direct about money.** When asked if it's paid, say so straight
  away [S10, S11].
- **Quietly confident.** "Quiet authority" [S18, S50]. No hype and no
  begging: "a bridge, not a beggar." [S41]
- **Simple enough for a child.** "Make the offer so simple a child could
  understand." [S14]

Never use profanity. His videos are full of it, and none of it carries over.
Apply his communication principles, but don't impersonate Saad or claim to
speak for him.

## 2. Name what you have, plainly

Lead with the thing the reader wants, stated as a fact you hold.

- Saad's pattern: "I have {the other side} actively {doing the thing} right
  now." [S01, S02, S04, S13]
- He calls his gym email "the most boring message you can send," and it worked
  [S04].
- Don't decorate it. No AI, workflows, funnels, "systems" or "growth
  partnership" talk in the offer [S03, S04, S14].

**(Illustrative)** "I have new restaurants in Birmingham that just got their
permits and haven't picked a linen service yet."

## 3. Sit between the sides, not on one side

Your language should show that you route access. It shouldn't sound like you
work for the side that pays.

- Say "I connect {A} with {B}. I do this independently. I don't represent
  them." [S07, S11, S12]
- Never say "my clients," "we represent them" or "I'm working on their behalf."
  Say "a company we're activating," "a team entering growth mode" or "a
  situation that just triggered." [S40]
- Use the routing verbs: route, connect, introduce, activate, facilitate,
  coordinate [S17, S33, S40].
- Avoid naming yourself "lead generation," "marketing services" or
  "appointment setting." Those words put you in the vendor box [S14, S03].

## 4. Use the industry's own words

Use their nouns, not marketing nouns.

- Examples from his copy: "wholesale accounts" [S08], "crews," "producer,"
  "rig location" [S12], "residents," "facility" [S11], "turnover,"
  "changeover," "short-stay" [S16], "placements" [S39], "accounts" [S14].
- Pick up new terms from their replies and their websites. Google anything you
  don't know before you answer [S05, S12].
- Mirror their exact phrasing back to them when you reply [S01, S03].

## 5. Specific public facts instead of compliments

The personalization is a dated, verifiable fact about their situation, not
flattery.

- "Saw {daycare} got its license on September 4th." [S03]
- "Your September 15 request for internet access opens for signing on October
  13." [S09]
- "You bid on the battery backup UPS contracts at {district} in December;
  {winner} took it." [S09]
- No "noticed you were walking your dog last week," and no generic praise of
  their website [S04, S11].
- A researched market number can stand in for proof, e.g. "Honey prices went
  up 27% last year." Use it only if you've verified it [S07].

## 6. Keep cold outreach short

Each first email has three moves [S18, S50]:

1. **I have** the relevant other side.
2. **I'm not** throwing vendors at you, or selling you leads.
3. **I'm checking** your capacity (supply) or whether the issue is still live
   (demand) before I route anyone.

Rules for every first email:

- 2–4 sentences, text-only, no links or images [S02, S03].
- Short, plain subject lines: "Lunch," "Beans," "Manufacturing," "Sanity check"
  [S03, S08, S13, S41].
- Social proof is optional, and he usually deletes it [S01, S02].

**(Illustrative)** "Hey Dana, I have a few new assisted-living facilities in
Huntsville lining up their pharmacy services. Before I send anyone your way, do
you have room for another facility right now?"

## 7. Make the next step small and answerable

- Put the offer in the question so they can answer yes or no. "Want me to put
  one or two in front of you?" [S05, S10]
- Ask permission to continue, not for a call: "If this is live for you, I can
  send the details." "Strangers do not like being moved too fast." [S41]
- Tie the question to their money: "Are staffing gaps currently forcing you to
  use overtime or limiting the residents you can take in?" [S11]
- Offer two specific times when a call is the next step [S03]. Or ask when
  *they're* free, and don't push your calendar first [S41].

## 8. Replies: mirror, stay short, hand nothing over yet

- **Work out what they're really asking first.** Use the watermelon rule:
  write what they said, what they really mean, then your reply [S01].
- **Use the formula** [S03]:
  1. Say back what they told you.
  2. Don't hand over names, menus or the other party yet.
  3. Give two times.
- **Keep replies short.** "Absolutely. What are you looking to manufacture right
  now?" [S13]
- **Compress a long list of questions** into one answer plus one next step
  [S04].
- **Match their tone.** A formal sender gets a formal reply [S02].
- **Reply fast.** "Speed creates luck." [S15]

## 9. Answer hard questions plainly and truthfully

- **"Where did you get my email?"** Say where: the public registration, the
  license list. Then carry on [S13, S16].
- **"Are you selling leads?"** "This is not a lead generation service in the
  usual sense and I'm not looking to sell you a list of contacts. The way we
  work is closer to a selective referral / introduction model." [S50]
- **"What's in it for you?"** or **"How does payment work?"** State the fee.
  "Never hedge, never redirect." [S07, S10]
- **"Are you with the city?"** A light, honest answer: "No, I'm not with the
  city. I promise I'm not about to ask for your permit number." [S16]
- **"Have you worked in X?"** Answer truthfully. Describe what you actually do,
  e.g. "I track this market and route live situations." Don't claim experience
  you don't have (see §16).
- **A skeptic is a good prospect.** Answer their question first, in one line
  [S06].

## 10. At the commercial stage, switch to institutional language

Once money is involved, move to your own inbox and write like a private bank:
calm, precise, complete [S08, S09].

- Open with: "Taking this over to my proper email so I can send you the
  opportunity properly." [S06, S17]
- State the fee plainly, then what they get. Make the deliverable concrete
  enough to picture: "You'll have their name, company and direct contact
  details and can discuss pricing, volumes and paperwork directly." [S07, S11,
  S12]
- Include the replacement clause, in the same calm tone [S05, S11].
- Include the transparency line when both sides pay [S08].
- No exclamation marks, no excitement, no "amazing opportunity."

## 11. Introductions: why now, why these two, then step back

- "Looping you both in based on timing," then the signal, the capability, and
  "I'll leave it to you both from here." [S40]
- Carry each side's own words and numbers, so neither has to start again [S05,
  S07, S11].
- Never mention the fee in the intro [S12].

## 12. Hold the frame without chasing

- **No "just checking in" or "are you still interested?"** Come back with a
  real signal instead [S18].
- **When someone pushes back, pull away calmly.** "If there's no interest in
  exploring a referral relationship, no problem at all." [S50]
- **Breakups are graceful.** "Seems like timing isn't right now, which is
  completely fine. I'll close this out on my end so I don't clutter your inbox."
  [S41]
- **Once a payment link is sent, stop following up** [S11].
- **Keep the principle, drop his language.** His wording for this ("prospects
  are like toddlers," a crude term for "tests," "the ego shatters") is
  excluded. The principle stays: stay calm and don't flinch.

## 13. Teach with analogies and short principles

In explanations to the user, in training material or in content, keep his
teaching style:

- **Everyday analogies**:
  - selling umbrellas, not the weather [S01];
  - two people selling watermelons [S01, S13];
  - a VIP concert ticket [S37];
  - a bounty hunter [S37];
  - holding a door [S13];
  - a pizza with three slices [S04];
  - a plumber who's paid for years of skill, not 30 seconds [S46].
- **One-line principles** ("Boring is money," "The replies built the deal"),
  followed by the concrete example [S06, S16].
- **Post-mortems:** always explain why something worked [S05, S16].

Keep the analogies out of cold outreach and close emails, which stay plain.

## 14. Light humor, only where it's natural

- One playful line per email at most, and only on the cheaper, friendlier side.
  "Asking for the dogs." [S10] "Same building, smaller bill." [S05]
- None in close emails, intros or anything to do with penalties and enforcement
  [S50].

## 15. Things to avoid

- Profanity, personal tangents (diet, nicotine, cars, his move abroad), and
  income flexing.
- Selling AI, automation, "systems" or booked meetings as the offer [S01, S03].
- Hype words ("skyrocket," "game-changing," "revolutionary") and the agency-era
  "17× the volume of an SDR" style of claim [S42, S59].
- Surface-level AI personalization ("I noticed that you went to this school. That's crazy, me too.") [S53,
  S56].
- Long emails, HTML, images or links in the first touch [S02, S03].
- Revealing the other party's name before payment [S10, S11].
- "Circling back," "just checking in," or follow-ups with no new information
  [S18].
- Pressure and status games from the role-play call [S23].

## 16. Honesty rules (stricter than the transcripts)

Every factual claim in a draft has to be true for the user. In particular:

- **"I have…" or "I'm speaking with multiple…"** Only if those contacts or
  replies exist.
- **Scarcity or demand.** "Two more came in this week" or "other teams I can
  route this to" only if literally true [S17, S33].
- **Experience.** No "I've worked with X" or "I spend a lot of time around X"
  unless it's real [S09, S41].
- **No fabricated proof**: "a friend told me," "I know someone who helped…,"
  borrowed case studies, invented numbers [S17, S24, S31, S58].
- **Both sides paying is disclosed**, using the transparency line [S08].
- **No fake hand-sent signals**: no "Sent from my iPhone" on automated email,
  no deliberate typos [S19, S39, S60].
- **His income and results are his claims.** Never present them as expected
  results.
- **Harm-sensitive lanes** (fatalities, enforcement) are described factually,
  never for emotional leverage [S50].

## 17. Final quality check

Before delivering any Saad-style draft, ask:

- Does the first line name what I have, plainly?
- Is the "I have" true right now?
- Is it short enough to read in ten seconds?
- Am I using the reader's industry words, not marketing words?
- Is the personalization a verifiable public fact, not a compliment?
- Does it show I'm checking fit, capacity or whether the issue is live, rather
  than pushing?
- Is the next step small, and can it be answered yes or no?
- Have I kept the other party's identity back until payment?
- If money is mentioned, is the fee stated plainly, with what they get and the
  replacement clause?
- If both sides pay, is the transparency line there?
- Have I removed any claim I can't verify, any profanity and any pressure?
- Would this read as a calm operator who's seen something real, not a vendor
  asking for a meeting?

**The guiding principle:** sound like you already hold the opportunity and only
care whether the timing is real. "People don't buy because you're useful; they
buy because the moment feels true." [S41]
