# Saad — Earlier "AI Sales Systems Agency" Era

Sources: S19–S22, S24–S30, S34–S36, S42–S48, S51–S61.

In these videos Saad ran a sales-systems agency. He built Make.com and n8n
lead-gen pipelines and sold them, or the meetings they booked, for $1.5–15k.
He later rejects that model: "don't sell AI, automation or meetings" [S01, S03,
S31]. Use this file for **signals, list building, personalization technique
and buyer insight**, not as the offer. Where it conflicts with the connector
doctrine, the connector doctrine wins.

## Keep: buyer insight by industry [S22]

- **Q1 timing.** January to March is the busiest quarter for B2B, because
  budgets are approved in Q4. Prepare your outreach in Q4.
- **Biotech:**
  - PhD founders who are well funded and weak at sales;
  - deals around $5M;
  - "won't flinch at $8–10k," and anything below about $8k looks
    illegitimate;
  - their words: partnerships, collaborations, strategic alliances, business
    development, licensing, regulatory pathway, clinical validation, market
    access;
  - they prefer 6+ month engagements;
  - data: ClinicalTrials.gov, NIH grants, FDA pipeline trackers, Crunchbase,
    news alerts.
- **Recruitment:**
  - "Recruitment agencies are sales companies disguised as staffing
    companies."
  - Placement fees run 15–30% of first-year salary [S22, S26, S46, S56].
  - Their words: job orders, placements, candidates, fill rate, time-to-fill,
    placement fee.
  - Closing line: "one placement pays for the entire system for the year."
- **Investment firms:**
  - fees of 1–2% of AUM, so $10–15k is pocket change;
  - they say "clients," not "customers," and "opportunities/pipeline," not
    "leads";
  - they find cold calling insulting.
- **Fractional executives:** for companies that "don't have a CTO but need one
  and can't justify a full-time role" [S51, S52, S53].

## Keep: targeting by pain, not persona [S30, S36, S53, S58]

- **"Pain sniffing"**: identify urgent problems before they ask, "selling
  water in the desert." [S30]
- **A hiring post means the budget is already allocated** to that problem
  [S30, S34, S51]:
  - sales roles → outbound;
  - SEO or copywriter roles → marketing;
  - developer roles → dev agencies.
- **Apollo technologies filter**: target companies using a tool your client
  replaces or complements, e.g. QuickBooks, Shopify [S36].
- **Stack two pains**: Series A–D in the last 60–90 days *plus* a new CMO in
  the last 30–90 days [S58].
- **Number of funding rounds** (e.g. five) suggests they solve a real problem.
  For biotech, skip pre-seed and seed [S45].
- **Contact the person accountable for the pain.** For a sales hire, that's
  the sales manager, because they "know exactly what it costs when a new AE
  sits with an empty calendar for their first 90 days." [S59]

## Keep: personalization technique

- **"Dream email" / constrained variables / "anti-fragile method."** Write one
  perfect email by hand to a perfect-fit prospect, then turn it into a template
  with tightly constrained variables. "AI is bad at whole emails, good at
  constrained variables." [S19, S28, S45, S51]
- **Clean company names.** Write "Yuliko," not "Yuliko Inc." Strip LLC and
  LTD. Shorten job titles ("sales rep") and dates ("February 4th"). "People
  don't talk like this." [S27, S53, S57]
- **The congrats line** comes from a real recent win, case study, launch or
  funding. Keep it to 6–8 words in a Spartan, casual tone with no frilly
  language [S43, S51, S53].
- **No surface-level observations** like "I went to that school too." Speak to
  their business model in their lingo, so it reads as "this person actually
  did their homework." [S53, S56]
- **Use the buyer's lingo** [S47, S56]:
  - e-commerce: "brand," "profitable SKUs," "ROAS";
  - recruiters: casual and human.
- **Tone by buyer** [S20, S29]:
  - founder-to-founder casual for tech founders;
  - more direct for financial services;
  - "partnership" language for $1M+ firms.
- **First email light, second email carries the offer**, for tech founders
  [S29].
- **Offers are outcome-focused**: never talk about tools or workflows [S46,
  S57].

## Keep: offer construction [S35, S46]

- **The four ingredients** [S35]:
  1. Clarity. "If you confuse, you lose." Make it child-simple.
  2. Revenue-focused.
  3. Solves a painful must-have problem.
  4. Risk reversal without planting fear.
- **The auto-delivery lesson** [S46]: clients who *see* work arriving daily
  perceive more value and stay longer.
- **Outcome-focused offers by buyer** [S46]:
  - "We'll build you a system that feeds you a daily pipeline of recently
    funded companies so you can pitch before your competitors even know they
    exist."
  - VC funds and accelerators: "a backend tool that feeds [portfolio companies]
    leads, partners, hires, tools and vendors by tracking who just raised."

## Copy from this era (use only where true)

- **Hiring signal** [S27]: "Hey {first}, noticed you're hiring for a {job
  title} with a job posted on {date}. Looks like things are scaling up over
  there…"
- **Recruiter to hiring company** [S26, S55]: "Hey {first}, noticed you're
  hiring for {role}. I'm connected with {agency}. They have vetted candidates
  ready for you, no cost unless you hire. Worth the intro?" This is the
  recruiter's contingency offer, used with the recruiter's permission.
- **Agency to companies hiring devs** [S30]: "A lot of them are hiring for {X}
  devs, but honestly they'd move way faster partnering with a team like yours.
  If you're open to it, I can connect you with a few who'd likely be interested
  in working with you instead of building in-house."
- **"Deep thought" line** [S25]: "I was thinking about the challenges of
  {industry challenge} and how teams like {company} are navigating them. It
  seems like {pain} could be a priority, but I don't know exactly how you're
  approaching it right now."
- **Follow-up that abandons gracefully** [S57]: "Following up on my last note.
  I'll assume timing is not right at the moment."
- **Breakup** [S30]: "This will be my last email, hope there are no hard
  feelings. If anything changes, feel free to reach out."

## Do NOT copy from this era (honesty flags)

| Line or tactic | Why | Source |
|---|---|---|
| "A friend of mine told me about {company}" | Fabricated | [S24] |
| "I know someone who helped a new {industry} CMO get 22 sales-ready leads…" with the industry swapped per lead | A fabricated proof claim | [S58] |
| "Last month we generated over $85k / $105k for [client]," "17× the volume of an SDR," "$10M in pipeline" | Unverified claims or placeholders | [S24, S42, S43, S59] |
| "We can do this on a performance basis" while charging upfront, "just to get them on a call" | Misleading | [S21] |
| "Our sales systems filter through thousands of agencies" | Must be literally true | [S47] |
| AI-generated "I've been following your traction" / "we've helped other X projects" | Fabricated | [S29] |
| Emailing prospects "on behalf of" a company that doesn't know you, citing its case studies | Misrepresentation | [S19] |
| Listing maintenance tasks "just so they think they're getting a lot," when the real work is 30 seconds | Inflated value | [S46] |
| Personal emails and mobile numbers from People Data Labs for cold outreach | Privacy, TCPA and GDPR risk | [S46] |
| "Sent from my iPhone" on automated emails | Implies a hand-sent email that wasn't | [S19, S21, S38, S45] |
| Deliberate typos ("it triggered a reply") | Manipulative | [S39, S60] |
| "Circling back" / "just checking in" follow-ups | He later replaces these with evidence-based follow-ups [S18] | [S27] |
