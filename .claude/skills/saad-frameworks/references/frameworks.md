# Saad — Frameworks Overview

Read this first. It summarizes the whole system on one page and points to the
topic files. Tags like `[S08]` refer to `source-index.md`.

## The model in one paragraph

Saad is a **connector**. He finds two sides of a market that need each other but
haven't found each other yet, like:

- labs and food makers;
- paving contractors that lost a bid and airports about to buy;
- staffing agencies and nursing homes.

He sits in the middle and sells **access**, meaning timing and priority. He
doesn't sell AI, automation, "booked meetings" or lead lists [S01, S03, S40].
What he delivers is a context-rich email introduction [S05, S06]. He gets paid:

- **upfront**, before the introduction [S04];
- by one side or by both sides, each for a different service [S08, S37];
- under a **replacement-only** guarantee [S01, S10, S13].

He finds both sides in boring public data, such as:

- licenses, permits and trademark filings;
- procurement awards;
- government enforcement actions;
- funding and hiring posts.

He emails both sides at the same time with plain copy [S01, S16].

## Core beliefs (his words where possible)

- "He who has the thing names it plainly and the market pays him for it." [S01]
- Sell what people already want. Don't decorate it with AI, funnels or
  workflows. [S04, S07, S14]
- "Automating the guessing doesn't fix the guessing. You just guess faster." AI
  is machinery behind the scenes, not the product. [S03, S09]
- "The meeting is not our product. The relationship is theirs." [S03]
- "Every time someone is forced to pay something, somebody else wants that
  money." [S01]
- "The money is in the looking. The barrier is doing the boring work. Boring is
  money." [S06]
- "You're not selling, you're saving. You shortcut both." [S37]
- "You're creating optionality on both sides and waiting for intent to reveal
  itself." [S37]
- "Your guarantee should stay exactly where your work ends." [S13]
- "Never actually look at the work you do. Look at the value that your work can
  create." [S15, S16]
- "When interest is real, prospects start doing labor." [S41]
- "Does this put me in front of someone who could pay me? If yes, do it. If not,
  kill it." [S09]
- Only look for knowledge when reality asks for it. Learn the industry from the
  replies, and Google the terms you don't know. [S09, S12]
- "The replies built the deal": send instead of predicting, and let reality
  correct your filters. [S16]

## The system, step by step

| Step | What happens | Topic file |

| 1. | 2. Find the trigger | Find the public data that shows something just changed, and who is about to buy | `markets.md` "Signals" |
| 3. Decide who pays | Usually the side that already spends to acquire customers. Sometimes both sides, transparently | `offers-and-pricing.md` |
| 4. Build both lists | About 100 to 1,000 per side, verified emails only | `markets.md`, `stack.md` |

| 6. Handle replies | Mirror their words, give nothing away yet, offer two times or answer the price directly | `outreach.md` "Replies" |
| 7. Match | Count only the matches where both sides said they need each other | `closing-and-delivery.md` "Routing" |
| 8. Close | Move to your own Gmail and send the close email with the fee, the invoice, what they get and the replacement clause. Use a call only if the deal asks for one | `closing-and-delivery.md` |
| 9. Deliver | Send the intro email with full context, and optionally a thesis brief | `closing-and-delivery.md` "Intro" |
| 10. After the intro | One check-in after about 24 hours, light tracking, ask the free side for a case-study video, offer a continuation or retainer | `closing-and-delivery.md` "After" |

## Contradictions and evolution (don't resolve silently)

1. **Selling meetings vs. selling access.**
   - Earlier videos sell meetings, intros or systems on a "performance basis"
     [S20, S21, S26, S54].
   - Later he calls selling meetings a trap [S03] and sells access windows
     [S40].
   - Follow the later doctrine.
2. **Transparency about charging both sides.**
   - [S08] says to tell each side that the other also pays, for a separate
     mandate.
   - [S39] says "they don't need to know you're charging the other side."
   - Follow [S08].
3. **Calls.**
   - In the S05–S12 videos he closes by email, saying "a call is just a tool."
   - In [S18, S33, S40, S50] he uses 10–20 minute calls.
   - Both stand. "Let the deal tell you whether you need to hop on a call."
     [S06]
4. **Payment model.**
   - One-off upfront introductions [S05–S17].
   - Retainers and continuation windows: 60–90 day engagements [S08, S14]; $5–9k
     a month for priority routing [S18, S50]; a 6-month minimum [S50].
   - Hybrid: setup fee, retainer and rev share [S39].
   - The upfront part is constant. The size and structure grow with proof.
5. **Personalization.**
   - The agency era used heavy AI personalization [S24, S53].
   - The connector era sends "the most boring message you can send," with no
     flattery [S04, S11].
   - Follow the connector era. Specific public facts (dates, permits, bids) are
     the personalization [S03, S09].
6. **Follow-up tone.**
   - The agency era used "circling back" and "just checking in" [S27].
   - Later he says to come back with evidence or a signal, never "are you still
     interested?" [S18].
