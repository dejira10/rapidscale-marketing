# Word of the Week — Term Explainer

Goal: take the weapon out of the word. Give readers the real meaning so a label
can't stand in for an argument.

## Structure (about 200–300 words)
1. **The word, and how you've heard it used**: the common current usage,
   described neutrally.
2. **Where it comes from**: its origin and original meaning, with a source.
   For example, "woke" comes from Black American vernacular, meaning staying
   alert to racial injustice, and was documented well before its recent use as
   a catch-all insult.
3. **What it actually means**: the precise definition from scholarly or
   reference sources (Merriam-Webster, Oxford, Britannica, Stanford
   Encyclopedia of Philosophy).
4. **How the meaning got stretched**: when and how it became a catch-all, with
   dated examples.
5. **Reality check**: concrete tests readers can apply. For "socialism": does
   the government own the means of production? Contrast with programs
   Alabamians already use and support (Social Security, Medicare, TVA, public
   schools, farm subsidies) and explain why those are different, or the same.
6. **The takeaway**: one sentence readers could use in a conversation.

## Rules
- Cite at least two reputable reference sources.
- Be fair to people who use the word sincerely. Explain, don't sneer.
- Keep a list of terms already covered in `covered-terms.md` (create it on
  first run) so we don't repeat.
