# Frankie — Outreach and Follow-Up

**His preference:** "Cold outreach is my least favorite way." He prefers one
door with 10,000 buyers behind it (a shoe store) over knocking 10,000 doors. He
uses cold outreach mainly to:

- test handraisers, because strangers give brutal honesty;
- reach influencers and shoe stores;
- reply to individuals who've posted about a specific problem. [Z8, Z9, Z17]

## The four buckets of messaging [Z5, Z17]

1. **Open:** personalization plus a handraiser. The goal is "tell me more", not
   a sale.
2. **Idea:** the plan in idea form, 3–5 steps, given away free.
3. **Implementation:** what it looks like if we do it, delivered as a Loom or
   Google Doc.
4. **Follow-up:** "most of the sales actually get won and lost in step four."

## Message 1

- **Personalize from a real observation** with "Saw you…" or "Noticed you…".
  Drop the "I" on purpose. Use statements of fact, not compliments ("your
  content is simply outstanding" reads as phony). [Z4, Z16]
  - "Saw you just published a video about X." [Z4]
  - "Saw you and your partner went to Duke." [Z5]
  - "Saw you just rebranded from [old name] to [new name]." [Z5]
  - Even "I can't find any website" or a broken link works as an opener. [Z5, Z13]
- **Homework scales with the upside.** Do at least 30–60 seconds on anyone; a
  high-value shoe store can be worth 30 minutes. [Z5, Z30]
- **Then the handraiser.** Make the outcome specific, often an idea offered free:
  - "Hey dude, saw you guys do water and fire and mold out in Omaha. Had an idea to get you 50 to 100 flood job phone calls this month. Can I share a little bit about what I had in mind?" [Z17]
  - "Do you ever have [symptom from research]?" → "Do you mind telling me a little bit about that?" [Z4]
  - "Hey, if we could do [blank] for you, would that be interesting to you?" [Z10]
  - Royalty example: "Would you be open to me sending you a royalty check every month?" [Z5]
- **The barbecue test:** if you met this person at a barbecue, is that how you'd
  say it? Say it out loud. [Z5]
  - Fails: "Hey, quick question. If we could get you 15 to 100 booked
    appointments… would you want to jump on a call to discuss?"
  - Passes: "Hey Vicki, I was checking out your YouTube channel… Your best video
    got 100 views and I was thinking about how you put a zero on that and get a
    thousand. Can I run the idea by you?"
- **Golf: know where you are on the course.**
  - From the tee, a cold prospect, use the driver: "if we could do this, would it
    be interesting?" Make it easy to reply ("PPC" is 3 letters).
  - Close to the hole, just answer the question and putt.
  - High-friction asks like "let's jump on a 15 min call" kill replies. [Z5]
- **Why openers miss** [Z5]:
  - talking to everybody instead of one person;
  - no handraiser at all;
  - an ask that's too big;
  - writing a novel;
  - the benefit to them isn't clear.
- **Count the "I"s.** Bad DMs are all I/we/our services, a menu, "hope you're
  doing well", "do you have time for a quick chat?". [Z4, Z14, Z15]
- **Clear beats clever.** Name their product ("name their babies"), not "more
  workshop sales". [Z5]
- **Say what they're thinking:** "I know you think I'm just asking you this
  question so I can sell you something, but I'm actually just doing market
  research right now and your opinion would be super valuable… I'm not going to
  hammer you with anything." [Z30]

## Cold email format [Z15]

- **3–4 sentences.** The first line is personal (from their About Us page) and
  needn't relate to the offer. Only the last sentence uses "I".
- **Example:** "Saw you and Dr. Jones met 15 years ago in college… Was thinking
  about ABC Dental. Had an idea for you to pull implant cases out of your Dentrix
  that are probably sitting around. It would cost you way less than trying to run
  ads to convince strangers. Do you mind if I share a little about what I had in
  mind?"
- **Subject:** "Question for you, Dr. Jones" or "Question about ABC Dental". Not
  "quick question", which is overused. The preview line should mention them and
  their business.
- "Would you mind if I sent you a little video?" is an easy "no, I wouldn't mind".
- **Write 10 by hand before automating anything.** Don't buy an email-warmup
  stack. [Z14, Z15]

## Repetition and multi-channel [Z16, Z30]

- **Expect 4–7 messages to break through**, likely more as AI spam grows. Run it
  as a campaign. Keep the same free sample and tweak only the personalization.
- **Show up on several channels** the way real relationships do: LinkedIn,
  Facebook, YouTube, email, mail. Before messaging, comment genuinely on 2–3 of
  their posts over a week so they recognise you.
- **Track it** in a CRM or a "gangster CRM" Google Sheet (buckets, last touch). [Z17]
- **Expect about 5 replies per 10 messages** with this formula, with sales
  coming over weeks. [Z17]

## Follow-up: go deeper on the plan [Z14, Z17, Z18]

- **Never send** "circling back", "just following up" or "did you see my
  proposal?".
- **Each follow-up isolates one step** of your 3–5-step plan and adds a concrete
  idea. Write it as if you're already working together (Dusan Djukich's stance)
  and tie it to the outcome they stated.
- **The SEO sequence:**
  1. keywords;
  2. title tags;
  3. Google Business Profile (photos, reviews vs a named competitor);
  4. authority links;
  5. content.

  Close with: "No rush on getting back to me, but I'm super excited to catch up
  with you and get started on this."
- **Example:** "Hey Dr. Smith, I was checking out ABC Dental. I know you said you
  were really interested in getting Invisalign patients. So I was checking out
  these keywords… I made this list. Anyways, I'm really excited about working
  together. I know you've been busy, but I just wanted to send you a link so
  whenever you're ready, we can get started." [Z18]
- **When a video wasn't watched,** use humour and assume they're busy. "Hey, I
  heard you got kidnapped by aliens. No, just kidding. But I know you've been
  busy, just wanted to give this a friendly little bump and let you know this
  video is still here for you to check out." [Z8]
- **Remember the scale:** "That email is the most important thing in your world;
  it's 1.5 out of 10 in theirs." [Z14]
- **"Three possibilities: you buy it, you die, or you unsubscribe."** Keep
  watering for years when the upside is high. [Z14, Z29]

## Shoe-store / influencer outreach: the giver's mentality [Z27, Z30]

- **Treat it as building real relationships at scale.** "Can I interview you?"
  is the easiest opener (10–15 minutes on a topic they know in their sleep). [Z30]
- **Give first: high impact, low effort** (5–10 minutes each). Use their existing
  assets where you can:
  - an article for their magazine;
  - promo or sponsor emails for their event;
  - social posts;
  - short clips made from their best video;
  - YouTube thumbnails;
  - a press release and media room;
  - an introduction to someone useful. [Z27, Z30]
- **Give one thing at a time, not ten.** [Z27]
- **Example sequence (trade magazine editor)** [Z27]:
  1. "Went on a [magazine] binge today… Are you looking for writers by chance?"
  2. "Not looking for pay, just want the exposure."
  3. Deliver the article.
  4. Ask what they need: "What's a bigger priority for you — increasing
     readership, selling ad space, filling the event, something else?"
  5. Be transparent: "Full transparency, I'm just going to help you with
     whatever I can… I hope down the road when people ask if you know anybody,
     you'll think of me as a referral, though no pressure or obligation to do so."
- **Physical gifts** tied to their own words: he mailed a custom "servant
  marketing" hat to a coach who uses that phrase. The follow-up says what you do
  honestly: "I'm the internet guy for the water damage guys… trust is earned." [Z27, Z30]
- **Plan for all three outcomes:**
  - ignored → give again;
  - yes;
  - no.

  Send something twice (send, then a bump), then move to the next give. Only an
  actual no counts as a no. [Z27]
- **Daily input:** 3–5 messages a day to shoe stores or influencers, each one
  offering to do something for them, kept up for 90 days. [Z30]
- **Collaboration pitch (free training):** "Hey, I put together a free training
  about how to deal with that issue. You down to do something to help your
  people?… It's all value and there's no pitch." [Z9, Z11]
- **Always acknowledge referrals.** [Z27]

## Organic handraisers to friend lists [Z14, Z29]

1. Friend 10–30 shoe buyers a day.
2. Add them to custom Facebook lists (about 100 per list).
3. Post a 1–3 sentence handraiser visible only to those lists.
4. DM the people who engage: "Hey Russ, saw your comment on…".

They believe they found you. Expert status: "I want you to believe that you
found me and not the other way around." [Z29]

## Direct mail [Z5, Z9, Z12]

- **Less but better.** He sent 5 hand-picked letters, each with a tweaked opener,
  and 3 recipients bought. "Rather send 100 letters to one lawyer with a history
  of spending than 100 random."
- **A sample letter idea:** "I got this really [great] idea based on the fact
  that you pay all this money for traffic and 19 out of 20 people don't call
  you. What if we could do something with the 19? Can I tell you what I had in
  mind?" [Z5]
- **Early-stage CTA:** "text me at my number if you want to hear more, I'll send
  you a little video". [Z12]

## Never

- Spray-and-pray stacks: scrapers, 50 a day per domain, warmed-up inboxes. [Z5, Z15]
- Fake compliments, invented facts or false scarcity. [Z4, Z15]
- Deceptive tricks, such as a fake "internal forward". [Z27]
