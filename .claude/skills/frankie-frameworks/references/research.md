# Frankie — Research: Who to Sell To and What Hurts

## 1. Pick the who first

- Decide **who** before deliverables. "Everything you do in an agency is a
  hammer." For example, industrial equipment makers need email, follow-up and
  brochures, not TikTok. [Z14, Z23]
- **Pick one niche.** "A man who chases two rabbits catches neither." "Dear
  business owner" fails, and generic messaging ends up talking about the
  mechanism. [Z4]
- **Choosing the niche:**
  - pick one you're happy to talk about for 2 years;
  - prefer niches with a barrier to entry, such as licensed professions, because
    their buyers are more serious;
  - there are broke and rich businesses in every industry;
  - widen geographically (other English-speaking countries) before widening the
    niche. [Z4, Z6]
- **Niche for a repeatable deliverable.** Test: the second client should need
  almost no new work. [Z7]
- **Starter question:** "If I could only get paid after I got a result, who
  would I work with and what would they need to show up with?" [Z7]
- **Look at your happiest and unhappiest past clients.** Usually the best client
  is an established business with about 10 employees that already answers the
  phone. The worst are single owner-operators. [Z7]
- **Bigger businesses are easier, not harder.**
  - They have bigger problems, more money and faster access ("here's my guy").
  - "All the competition is at the bottom of the market."
  - Don't start with small fish "until you're ready". [Z9, Z29]
- **Size the who to set the price.** Who decides price more than what. A
  50-person law firm pays $5–20k a month; a solo lawyer pays $500–1,500. [Z9, Z26]
- **Five stages of growth** (service firms): solo → first hires → manager
  swamped by the team → managers reporting to a CEO → multiple locations. Speak
  to stage 2–4 problems. The magic word is **payroll**. [Z10]
- **Clients to avoid:**
  - broke clients;
  - bad-attitude clients (entitled, unkind, unrealistic expectations);
  - the "comfortable middle", which is risk-averse and rarely buys. [Z10]

## 2. Shoe stores and shoe buyers

- **Shoe buyers** have "closets full of 200 pairs": they are spending money on
  marketing now. Trust behavior, not words.
  - Spending signals: an expensive website, SEO, Google Ads, Facebook ads, radio,
    TV or billboards.
  - "You do not turn losers into winners." [Z6, Z7]
- Only 5–10% of any market buys. "I don't want to be 100% known by my market,
  just 100% known to the 5–10% who spend all the money." [Z7, Z9]
- **Two sales:** (1) that they should buy marketing at all, which is hard;
  (2) that yours is the right one. Only sell to people who've already made sale 1. [Z8, Z11]
- **Fast checks:**
  - Google "[service] [city]": the results labelled "Ad" are buying Google Ads
    right now.
  - Check for ad pixels (he mentions Coldlytics).
  - Count staff on the About Us page.
  - Look at the review count. [Z7, Z9, Z13]
  - In this repo, use `ad-spend-finder` for these checks.
- **Shoe stores** are places where buyers already gather and spend. Build a
  spreadsheet, a task he hands to a VA, with these columns:
  - gurus and influencers (their courses, masterminds, events);
  - books;
  - discussion groups and communities;
  - associations and organizations;
  - software and tools;
  - big events and their exhibitor lists;
  - competing products and services;
  - mailing address and social links for each. [Z7, Z8, Z27]
- **Jay Abraham's question:** "What did they buy before, after and instead of?" [Z8]
- **Questions for spotting shoe stores:**
  - Who are the influencers?
  - What are they reading?
  - Who are the authors and community owners?
  - Which associations do they belong to?
  - What software do they use?
  - What are the big events?
  - What competing products are there? [Z30]
- **Hidden buyers in groups** don't come with flashing lights. They talk about
  other things they're buying: tools, chairs, CRMs, financing companies. Friend
  them. Skip dead groups. [Z14, Z30]
- **A shoe store's friends and followers** (on Facebook or LinkedIn) are buyers
  or other shoe stores. Vendor YouTube channels work as ad placements. [Z15]
- **Three uses of a shoe store:**
  1. paid ads to its audience;
  2. a free training for its audience, offered to the owner;
  3. interviewing the owner first and pitching later ("chess, not checkers"). [Z7, Z9]
- **Golf course: start closest to the hole.** Target people who:
  - recently bought;
  - are past customers;
  - recently let a vendor go;
  - almost bought (abandoned quotes, no-shows, people who asked but didn't buy). [Z7, Z8, Z15]

## 3. Find the symptom (listen, don't guess)

- **Symptomatic marketing:** point to a specific thing they're doing now that
  they don't want to be doing. Accuracy beats hype. [Z7, Z26]
- **The symptom vs the upstream cause.** Prospects only see the symptom ("little
  girls drowning"), not the cause upstream.
  - Selling the cause, or selling future gain, motivates about 10× less than
    present pain.
  - Example: "reviews under 4.5 stars" is a cause; "people who want me to fix
    their animal for free" is a symptom. [Z26]
- **Reddit and Quora.** Search "what is the hardest part of being a
  [veterinarian / dentist / lawyer]". Upvotes are validation. Copy the quotes
  word for word. [Z8, Z23]
- **Jay Abraham's Amazon method.**
  1. Find books for the niche.
  2. Read the 5-star and 1-star reviews, prioritizing upvoted ones.
  3. Make two columns: moving away from (1-star → problems) and moving towards
     (5-star → outcomes).
  4. Use their phrases. "Amazon wrote the sales letter." [Z8, Z30]
- **Questions to ask in communities and of clients:**
  - "What kinds of [patients/jobs/clients] do you wish you had more of? And less of?"
  - "How are you getting them now? What's working?"
  - "Why would you tell somebody NOT to become a [roofer]?" The top upvoted
    answers make the best handraisers.
  - For existing clients: "Before you paid me money, what problem were you
    trying to solve?" [Z13, Z23]
- **Interviews** (Frankie's script):
  1. "Hey, I'm super interested in what it's like running a roofing company. Would you be open to just telling me what a day is like for you?"
  2. "Why would you tell somebody not to start one?" (hell)
  3. "What do you like?" (heaven)
  4. "What do you normally have to do?" (sharks)

  If they ask why: "I'm doing a ton of research… your opinion would be
  amazingly valuable." [Z13]
- **Mine 3–5 real sales calls or notes** for what comes up again and again (for
  example "the chair is empty"; "roller-coaster income, dependent on three to
  six referral partners"). Have 5–10 real conversations before building a Loom. [Z6, Z10, Z13]
- **Temperature-test every symptom** (one way he labels it):
  - **Frequency:** does it happen every day? A thorn in the paw.
  - **Emotion:** brushing teeth is frequent but not emotional.
  - Colour-code them: red = frequent + emotional, then orange, then yellow. [Z8, Z26]
- **Where pain lives:** in their current process or plan, as the side effects of
  what's already working. "Your job is not to create hell, it's to uncover where
  it already is." [Z13, Z15]
- **The outcome comes from them too.** Example (vet): "seeing the love in my
  animals' eyes and knowing I helped at least one other family". [Z8, Z26]
- **The $100 bill.** Ask "what kind of clients do you wish you had more of?"
  Examples:
  - restoration: a $10k flood job, not a $50 toilet overflow;
  - plastic surgery: mommy makeovers;
  - dentistry: implants;
  - law: motorcycle and commercial cases. [Z12, Z21]
- **Spend about 3 hours on market research** before building any service. Then
  choose the 2–3 deliverables that actually produce the result. [Z23]

## 4. Learn the industry's language

- Learn 20–50 insider terms. Examples:
  - dentists say "patients", not "clients";
  - orthodontists say "start", not "close";
  - restoration has "TPA work";
  - lawyers have "billboard lawyers" and "are you going to fight it or just mediate it?"

  The wrong word loses deals. Drill the terms (he uses ChatGPT voice). [Z4, Z7, Z13, Z14]
- **Know who they're skeptical of** and name it. Lawyers, for example, distrust
  Avvo, Justia, LegalMatch and SEO spammers: "I know you're skeptical… I would
  be too." [Z15]
- **Know the most-pitched niches** ("prom queens"): roofing, HVAC, dentists,
  plumbers and attorneys. Expect skepticism there. One way to see the noise is
  to look at the messages a prospect-like profile receives. [Z13, Z30]

## Research brief output (use in frankie-market-research)

The brief has these sections:

1. Who, with binary criteria.
2. The buyer signals you found.
3. The shoe-store spreadsheet.
4. Symptoms with verbatim quotes and links, scored on frequency and emotion.
5. Outcomes in their words.
6. The $100 bill.
7. Their current plan and the other plans they've tried.
8. Industry terms.
9. Who they distrust.
10. The closest-to-the-hole segments.
