# Frankie — Offers

## Offer is not service

The service is the meals. The offer is "$16.99 all-you-can-eat BBQ ribs with
corn biscuit, dessert, baked potato and a live band tonight." [Z1, Z7]
The biggest mistake people make is treating their service as the offer. [Z7]

"I don't see myself as a copywriter. What I'm good at is creating good offers
that… create great copy." [Z8] Offer-making beats copywriting: a great profile
beats a great copywriter working with a weak one (his dating-profile
analogy). [Z7, Z30]

## The offer matrix (4 quadrants) [Z8, Z9, Z28]

| From the market (parrot it; "I don't guess at these") | From you |
|---|---|
| **Symptoms**: what they're experiencing now and don't want | **New and exciting way out**: the plan or mechanism, ideally named so it escapes the "I've heard that before" box (Eugene Schwartz's "unique mechanism") |
| **Results / outcome**: what it literally looks like when done | **TIMER**: what they *don't* have to spend or risk |

You can strengthen it with guarantees, bonuses and ethical scarcity or urgency. [Z9]
Before writing any copy, always fill in: symptoms, outcome, new and exciting
way out, TIMER risk, and who it's for. [Z9]

### TIMER — every reason they say yes or no [Z7, Z8, Z26]

- **Time.** Example: "within two hours up and running".
- **Identity.** "I'm a dentist / plumber / yoga teacher, not a marketer." You do
  your thing; I'll do my part.
- **Money.** What it makes *or saves* (retargeting at 5¢ a click vs a $200 PI click).
- **Energy.** Magic phrase: "It's already done for you… you just say go." Or
  "copy and paste, swipe and deploy."
- **Reputation.** "I'll make you look good." This matters more the higher up the
  income ladder you go.

Busy, successful buyers object on time, energy and reputation more than on
money. The most valuable thing is often what you **eliminate**: "Get paid more
money to bring you less effort." "Don't hand them plate 21; take three
away." [Z10, Z13, Z14, Z26]

### Hard-to-sell vs hell-yes offers (Travis Sago's checklist, as taught by Frankie) [Z26]

- **Hard to sell:** nonspecific, not relevant, unattainable, not measurable, not
  timely, sold on future gain. On TIMER it means more time, identity risk, more
  money, more energy and reputation risk to change.
- **Hell yes:** super specific, relevant, attainable, measurable, timely, and it
  relieves a **present pain**. On TIMER it means less time, energy and money to
  change than not to, an improved identity and an improved reputation.

## Handraiser (test the offer in 1–3 sentences) [Z1, Z7, Z14]

- It's a 1–3 sentence offer summary, put in front of buyers **before** you build
  videos, funnels or tech. "If you can't sell your offer in 1–3 sentences,
  adding funnels and tech won't change that."
- **Formula:** "If you are [type of person] experiencing [symptom], we can help
  you get [outcome] using our [new and exciting way out] without [TIMER thing
  you don't want]." Add a demo if you can. [Z7]
- **Conditional frame:** "If you have [blank], then we can [blank]." For example:
  "if you've got at least 100 people in the last month who told you no, we can
  probably win you back a job or two." [Z14]
- **Write it as an invitation, not sales copy** ("I'm throwing a barbecue…
  should I save you a spot?"). Use simple, third-grade English. Leave "I think"
  out of the handraiser; save honesty for the conversation. Low-friction
  replies such as "drop the comment 'implant'" or "drop a 1" work well. [Z7, Z14]
- **Message 1 aims for** "that sounds interesting, tell me more". It doesn't aim
  for a sale. [Z5, Z14]
- **Examples from the sources:**
  - "Roofer. You've got 100 quotes who told you no in the last month. You're probably sitting on a job or two. If I did all your follow-up and selling, would you be open to a little test?" [Z14]
  - "Dentists — patients tell you 'I can't afford implants.' We figured out how to present price before the consult… You only do consults with people who know the price and can afford it. If you'd like to see how it works, drop the comment 'implant'." [Z14]
  - "Coach — your email list is full of buyers… We can probably book you at least 20–30 more sales calls this month without chasing people or sending another DM. Drop a comment 'buyers'." [Z14]
  - "Hey, do you have roller coaster income and need more clients because you're overly dependent on three to six referral partners who control your entire business?" (from 40–50 repeated lawyer calls) [Z7]
  - Attorney group: "Who here would be interested in getting motorcycle cases?" got 8–9 "me" replies; the sales came 1–3 months later. [Z5, Z7]
- **Nobody raises a hand?** Change ONE variable. The order to check:
  1. the audience;
  2. the symptom (changing only the symptom has taken a handraiser from crickets
     to 75 hands);
  3. the outcome;
  4. the plan;
  5. the price. [Z8, Z14, Z15]

  Example: an email-marketing test using "emails landing in spam?" got zero
  replies; "half-empty program you want to fill?" got 50–100. [Z15]
- **"I'm the offer guy. I make offers that don't work."** Expect about 8 in 10 to
  get crickets. Earlier posts still feed later responses. [Z29]
- **Handraisers act as a bouncer.** Call out symptoms that only bigger
  businesses have. Example: "TV cases costing you more than $1,000 per signed
  case?" rather than "need more clients?" Size them by staff, not revenue: "two
  or more crews". [Z9, Z26]

## Specificity [Z4, Z6, Z15, Z22]

- **Broken offers** use undefined "more", "quality leads", or an over-the-top
  guarantee ("or we'll pay you $1,000"). [Z22]
- **Define "quality" in the client's terms.** Example: "We can get you motor
  vehicle accident cases for $500 a signed case that are inside the statute of
  limitations, not currently represented by an attorney, have insurance, and
  were not at fault — using Facebook ads." [Z22]
- **The specificity ladder:** lose weight → lose 10 lbs → in 30 days → with
  10-minute home exercises → and still eat cake. [Z22]
- **A 10-degree shift:** "we do SEO" becomes "we get you residential category 3
  flood jobs using SEO and PPC". [Z13]
- **Be specific about the problem; early on, stay vague about the plan and
  price** (one sentence) so they can't object yet. Once the offer is validated,
  put all the details upfront. [Z15]

## Add-on vs replacement [Z6, Z7, Z14]

- **Replacement** ("our ads are better") needs two sales: fire the current
  person, then hire you.
- **Add-on** bolts onto what they do. "Is he going after motorcycle cases for
  you? … Let him do what he's doing."
- Database reactivation is an add-on, so it's a better foot in the door than
  SEO, which is a replacement with a 2-year sales cycle.
- Take what's already working and bring it somewhere new (for example,
  Instagram content onto Reddit). [Z15]

## Coffee date → first date → marriage [Z6, Z20]

- **Coffee date (free).** An engineered quick win, doable in 10–15 minutes, that
  shows you know what you're doing. Examples: a two-sentence Google-review
  request script; one Facebook ad to try; a free ranking analysis against a
  named competitor.
- **First date (small paid commitment).** Examples: a 4-day database
  reactivation; "give me one keyword in 30 days"; a $50 video-plus-thumbnail.
  It also screens out difficult clients: "just don't offer a second date."
- **Marriage (ongoing).** Don't lead with it. A big commitment needs lots of
  proof. "The way to show people you can help them is by actually helping them."

## Tiers of value and nickels vs $100 bills [Z21]

- Tiers, from low to high:
  1. activity;
  2. leads;
  3. qualified leads;
  4. face-to-face appointments;
  5. sales.

  Get as close to the sale as you can. "Five sales up here is better than 100
  leads down here."
- In every tier there are nickels (volume, little profit) and $100 bills
  (highest ticket). Sell their $100 bill directly. [Z12, Z21]
- Most things can be sold or booked on a website without a call. Example:
  "I'll just sell your memberships for you." [Z21]

## Promise ONE [Z14, Z16, Z22]

- Codify the outcome as one high-value thing: "one high-value motorcycle case",
  "one commercial roofing job".
- Ask: "What is the most small-but-meaningful ONE of something I can promise?"
  Example: "Give me 24 hours, I'll send an email and get you a single booking."
  Then over-deliver.
- Avoid grandiose promises ("an extra $500k a month"). [Z7]

## Price: IVQ (Investment, Value, Qualify) [Z6, Z8, Z11, Z12]

- **Formula:** "The investment is $X. For that you'll typically get [outcome].
  It works really well if you are [who / binary condition]."
- Examples from the sources:
  - Restoration SEO: "The investment is $2,000/month. For that you'll typically
    get between 35 and 40 calls a month. If you get just one $5,000 flood job
    you'll have made your money back. It works really well if you're able to
    handle multiple jobs at a time." [Z12]
  - Motorcycle: "investment is $12k (4k × 3 months); if we get you just one case
    you've doubled your investment; average motorcycle case worth $25k+; works as
    long as you can close on the phone and handle motorcycle cases." [Z11]
  - Website: "investment $2k; if you make a handful of sales from your website
    you'll make your money back in 30 days; works as long as you drive traffic
    to it." [Z11]
- **Single-unit pricing** ($10 for a lead on an $80k case) sets low
  expectations, so clients are thrilled when they get three. [Z9, Z12]
- **If asked for the price out of sequence,** just tell them, plus "and for that
  you'll typically get…". Don't hide being higher-priced ("used Toyota vs
  Bentley"). [Z3, Z10, Z14]
- **After stating a price, stay silent for 10–15 seconds.** [Z5]
- **Cost of doing nothing.** "What happens if you don't do anything? How long
  can you keep doing that?" Doing nothing is usually their real plan. [Z14, Z25]
- **Pricing a new offer:** "What would make this a hell-yes, no-brainer for
  you?" Accept reasonable terms, take a little upfront so they have skin in the
  game, and ask for a case study. [Z13, Z14]
- **Good/better/best packages.** Upsell question: "Do you want just one or do
  you want more?" [Z9, Z28]

## Proof and risk [Z11, Z25]

- Every objection comes down to "working with you represents a risk" (TIMER).
- **Starting out, remove the risk and be honest.** Magic phrase: "Let's just run
  a test. And if for some reason it doesn't work out, you won't owe me
  anything." Full version: "Hey, I'm just being honest with you. This is a new
  idea I just came up with and I've never run it for anybody. Would you be open
  to just running a little test?" Add "I'll make you look good."
- **The proof you need scales with the risk** of the offer.
- **Borrowed credibility:**
  - interviewing a known name;
  - building something free for a respected client in exchange for the case study;
  - "I was talking to [name] the other day…". [Z11, Z14]
- **Proof can simply be an accurate symptom shown next to the desired outcome.** [Z25]

## Assets over hours [Z4, Z8, Z14, Z27]

- **"Digital landlord":** lease working systems (campaigns, landing pages, quote
  widgets) rather than doing custom work. "Instead of 100 clients, lease 100 assets."
- **Own the system, not the client.** Only do reusable work cheaply.
- **Revenue share with partners who already have the buyers.** They hold the
  audience, the offer and the reputation, and you plug in the missing piece.
  "Half a watermelon vs 100% of a grape." Show up as a buyer, not a seller. [Z25, Z27]
- **Give away step 1 and sell step 2.** Run them up against the next challenge
  they can't get over alone. [Z24 (Travis), Z28]
- **Offers expire.** Copycats appear after about 3 years, so offer-making is the
  durable skill. [Z28]
