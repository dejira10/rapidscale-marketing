# Frankie — Sales Conversations (text-first)

He runs most sales conversations over text (SMS, email, Facebook Messenger).
Text lowers people's guard, they can walk away, and you can hold 10
conversations at once. [Z4, Z9] What most people struggle with "is not that
they don't know how to close, it's that they actually don't know how to open
them." [Z4]

## Frame

- **The doctor frame.** A doctor doesn't introduce himself or his surgery. He
  asks "Tell me what's happening. Where does it hurt?" Only 1–3% of the
  conversation is about you. [Z13]
- **The 3 Cs:** curiosity, caring, concern. Mostly ask questions. [Z13]
- **The friend test.** Remove the credit card. If a friend said "it's been
  slow", you'd ask "what do you mean? what's going on?" [Z4, Z30]
- **The benevolent dictator.** Politely set the order. "I'm happy to answer all
  those questions… but before we even get there, let's just make sure we're a
  fit for each other. Can you tell me a little bit more of your situation?" [Z9, Z27]
- **Abundance, zero neediness.** "I can just put out another hand raiser and
  have 10 more tomorrow." Be the chooser, not the chosen. [Z4, Z9]
- **An advisor, not an arm-wrestler.** Bring your chair around to their side of
  the table. [Z5]

## Opening

- **Inbound:**
  - "Hey, it's so nice to hear from you. What's happening in your world that made you want to reach out?"
  - "What had you reaching out today?" [Z4, Z9]
- **The million-dollar question:** "Do you mind telling me a little bit about
  your situation and what you're looking to do?" Variants:
  - "…what you're looking to build?"
  - "Can I ask you a little bit about what you're building?" [Z4, Z9, Z13]
- **For busy, successful people:** "Tell me what you're doing right now that
  you don't want to be doing. What can I get off your plate?" [Z26]
- **Referral warm-up:** "Hey, I really appreciate the referral from [name]. I'm
  glad we're talking. Since I don't know as much about you as I should, can you
  just tell me a little bit about your situation and what you guys are looking
  to do?" [Z10]
- **When they request a video or details:** "I'm happy to send you the video.
  Let me just go ahead and grab the link. In the meantime, do you mind just
  sharing a little bit about your situation and what you're looking to do?" [Z10]
- **Don't open with** "I'm really excited to tell you about our service". That
  loses them. [Z4]

## Go deeper: 30,000 ft → street level

- The first answer is general ("need more leads") about 9 times in 10. Don't
  jump to selling. [Z4, Z9]
- **The parrot.** Repeat what they just said and add a question mark: "Slow for
  summer?" "More leads?" [Z4, Z9]
- **Dig with questions like these:**
  - "How slow is slow?"
  - "How many leads did you get? How many do you actually need?"
  - "Why do you think it's been that slow?"
  - "What made you decide today that you needed more leads?"
  - "Why is that a problem?" [Z4, Z9]
- **The finger pointer.** Keep going until you can point to how the symptom
  shows up in their day. Yoga example: "every time Jenny gets in there, only six
  people, and we can hold 20." Emotion usually shows up here. [Z4, Z9, Z15]
- **You need both** the emotion (sales are emotional) and the specifics (to know
  when the job is done). [Z4, Z13]
- **Heaven Island / Hell Island / the Sharks** (recap sequence) [Z13]:
  - "Tell me about what you're building." (heaven)
  - "Tell me about what sucks."
  - "Tell me about what stops you." (sharks)
  - "Tell me about what you're doing now."
  - Then: "Sounds like you're trying to go from this island to that island…
    concerned about these sharks… we got a way to go from here to there and
    around those sharks. Would you be interested?"

  The service is just the boat or the helicopter.
- **Before the solution, ask:** "What else have you tried?" / "What are you
  currently doing to bring in X?" [Z4, Z9]
- **Same want, different reasons.** Personalize the offer to their why. Example:
  "so next time your daughter asks… here's the check". [Z4, Z13]

## Present the plan

- **Transition:** "Hey [name], I had some ideas for you on how you could
  potentially [their outcome]. Is it cool if I share?" ("In the entire history
  of asking that question, nobody ever says no.") [Z4, Z9]
- **Red light / green light.** Give one bite-sized piece and wait for a green
  light. [Z5]
  1. "Can I lay it out for you in steps?"
  2. "Here would be the first step. Does that make sense?"
  3. "Then I would do this. You following me still?"
  4. "Do you see when we put all those steps together, how that thing you said you want… those things would help with that?"

  On a red light: "Where was that not clear?… let me explain another way.
  Here's a picture."
- **Don't send a wall of text** (a long ChatGPT-style reply killed one deal).
  Even with a pre-recorded video, ask first: "Is it okay if I send it to you?" [Z5]
- **3–5 steps from 30,000 ft.** Make it clearly different and desirable, often
  better because it's simpler. Contrast their old plan with the new plan. Avoid
  buzzwords ("proprietary system") and avoid over-technical detail (pixel
  events). [Z3, Z6, Z8, Z10]
- **The magic phrase:** "What do you think of that plan?" [Z4, Z9]
- **Objections happen at the plan**, even though they show up at the price. If
  you're not on the same page about the plan, don't proceed until you are. [Z4, Z8, Z9]
- **Dig into failed past attempts.** "What did you guys actually do? Who did you
  target? What did the ad say?" Then: "Is it possible you guys just did Facebook
  ads wrong and not that Facebook ads themselves don't work?" [Z4]
- **"We already do retargeting" → "Tell me what kind of retargeting you do."**
  Then contrast one rinky-dink ad with 20 ads per platform aimed only at people
  who've already shown interest: "Do you see how that would be different?" [Z4, Z10]
- **Ask:** "Where did you get the impression that what I was going to do for you
  and what they were going to do are the same thing?" [Z8]
- **If they flatly reject the mechanism** ("I hate Facebook ads"), don't present it. [Z4]
- **Address the elephant.** "Yeah, it is [SEO]. And it sounds like you've had
  nothing but bad experiences with SEO. Do you mind sharing why that is?" Then
  show the specific differences and offer a small test. [Z13]

## Close

- **The magic line:** "Can I tell you a little about what working together on
  that would look like?" If they agree with the plan, 8–9 in 10 just buy. [Z4]
- **Plan without price first:** "What do you think of that plan?" → "Would you
  like my help with that?" [Z10]
- Then IVQ (see `offers.md`), what happens after the sale, and how to buy. [Z10]
- **"Talk to my partner" or "I need to think about it"** is a stall, because they
  didn't buy into the plan. Fix the plan earlier: "the moment of the credit card
  is when you're least believable." [Z9]
- **A real request for time is fine:** "Yeah, take two nights if you want…
  sleep on it… get back to me tomorrow." [Z10]
- **Take the sale away when it fits:** "I only send payment links if you're 100%
  hell yes." [Z6, Z25]
- **Expectation setting**, right after they commit and before the payment link:
  "Hey, is it cool if I send you the payment link? Before I send it, I just want
  to be clear that…" Scale it to the size of the promise and the time to
  result. [Z26]
- **Upsell softly:** "Were you looking to buy just one today, or a package of
  six?" [Z9]

## Objections and no's

- **Upstream.** Fix objections before they come up. One person's objection is
  usually everybody's, so put the answer into the plan section of your
  Loom/Doc. Version 1.0 of a Loom has 1–2 plan objections; version 2.0 usually
  sells. [Z10]
- **A plan objection** ("I'm not interested in taking ads out"): "That's totally
  cool. I'm not going to try to convince you to run ads, but it sounds like
  you've had a bad experience in the past. And I'm trying to improve my stuff.
  Can I just ask you, what about ads didn't work for you?" [Z10]
- **When they say "no thank you":** "Hey, I'm not going to try and sell you, but
  I'd love your honest opinion for why not?" [Z10]
- **The brutal honest feedback script:** "Hey, I would just love your brutal,
  honest feedback. I ain't going to try and sell you anything, but it sounded
  like you were interested at first, and then it became a no. Can you just tell
  me why?" It must include "brutal", "honest" and "not going to try and sell
  you". The objection is usually one small detail. [Z13]
- **Staying with the current provider:** "I really appreciate your transparency…
  let's just keep in touch. If you do decide to make that switch down the road,
  I'd love to revisit this conversation." Don't argue. [Z10]
- **Effort-of-switching objection:** add a "what switching over to us looks
  like" step to the plan (access in 2 minutes, ads already built, "already done
  for you"). [Z10]
- **Out-of-sequence questions** (pricing, 8-question lists): "I'm happy to get
  you all of those things, but I just had a few more questions… so I can make
  this relevant and specific to you. Do you mind if I ask you a little bit about
  that?" [Z9]
- **"Which other clients?" early on:** a testimonial page from people who talked
  to him and didn't buy ("let other lawyers know this call is safe"). [Z7]
- **A high-ticket sale is a process, not an event.** A no usually means "not
  right now". After a no: be kind, say thank you, and ask "If I have additional
  ideas or information in the future, can I send it to you?" [Z14]
- **Most rejection is timing** (a lawyer inquires 90 days before a settlement
  check arrives). Keep watering. [Z29]
- **If they've been backed up for 6 months and want no change:** "Cool man, nice
  talking to you." [Z10]
- **Screen for attitude:** "Well, I'm happy to send you the details, but I'll
  just let you know, it's a no for me and we're just not going to be a fit." [Z9]
- **When you've let someone down, fall on your sword:** "I'm really, really
  sorry. This is what happened… I would like to make it right for you. Can I
  start there?" [Z26]
