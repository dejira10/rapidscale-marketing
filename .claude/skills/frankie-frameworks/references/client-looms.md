# Frankie — Real Client-Facing Sales Looms and Onboarding

These are finished sales videos Frankie made for prospects, plus an onboarding
questionnaire. Use them as **models of structure and tone**.

The numbers, years of experience and assets in them (16 years, the 150-site
press network, call ranges, prices) are **Frankie's own claims about his own
business**. Never reuse them for another marketer or client unless that person
confirms they're true for them.

Sources: Z31, Z32, Z33 and Z34 in `source-index.md`. The auto-detailing website Loom
(Z2) is summarized in `loom-and-doc.md`.

## Z31 — "Get Flood Jobs Using Our SEO Services" (restoration SEO Loom)

### Structure, in the order he presents it

1. **Open on the symptom, in the owner's words.** The "what do you want us to
   do now, boss?" conversation when there's no work and payroll is still due.
   He relates as a boss himself. The goal: keep crews busy so they're
   "income producing employees rather than costing you money".
2. **Demonstration.** A ranking heat map from a real client about 2 months in
   (a major Texas city), translated into what the owner experiences: the phone
   call where someone says "hey, I found you on the Google", and that caller is
   pre-sold.
3. **"Who it works well for", before how it works.** Three conditions:
   - a crew you need to keep fed with work;
   - a process for capturing reviews, or a willingness to build one ("Google
     just does not work without reviews");
   - **4–8 months of runway**.

   He also adds a website that "actually deserves to be number one".
4. **An analogy that sets expectations.** His old car's air conditioning only
   worked full blast ("zero or hero"). Google is the same: positions 1–3 get the
   calls and #5 gets none.
5. **How it works ("really just boring fundamentals"):**
   1. On-page work.
      - Rewrite the Google title and description so they sell, not just state
        the company name. His example: "top rated, 500+ Google reviews, 24/7,
        45 minutes or less, certified and insured".
      - Target keywords people actually search, combined with the location.
        He warns that some marketers show rankings for keywords nobody searches.
   2. A heat map and keyword buckets, with variations: "water damage
      restoration", "restoration near me", "restoration companies near me",
      "basement water damage", "storm damage".
   3. Reviews. He recommends a review habit and software (he names Housecall Pro).
   4. PR and news ("a little bit of our secret sauce"). He says he has his own
      press-release network of about 150 sites, mostly local Fox, ABC and CBS
      affiliates.
   5. A content machine. "Google is a big question and answer machine."
      Publishing the most and best content gives you the most answers and
      credibility: a business-card site vs 150 articles.
6. **Typical results and timing.** About 50–100 extra calls a month, in 4–8
   months. He shows progress right away with a marathon analogy: "you're not
   gonna finish the race in five minutes, but… we're further along than the
   starting place."
7. **Price.** "Our typical cost for that is about 1,800 a month", including the
   news, PR and content.
8. **Communication, used to handle burned-by-SEO skepticism.**
   - Weekly 2–3-minute video updates for the first 60 days (what we're working
     on, why, how it's going, what we're tracking), then monthly.
   - Most communication is by text. Calls are scheduled, not ad hoc: "if you're
     a call me, call me, call me kind of person… we may not be a good fit."
9. **What we need from you.** Access to the site, hosting and Google Business
   Profile.
10. **Credibility.** He has worked as a white-label subcontractor, so many
    rankings that are his work aren't in his name.
11. **Next step and graceful exit.**
    - Fill in the form at the bottom of the website, and he or someone from the
      team will reach out to "see if we're a good fit".
    - If not, they'll point you elsewhere (referrals from plumbers and insurance
      adjusters, help getting paid on past work): "we'd like to leave you better
      off no matter what."

### Differences from the workshop example (Z12)

The Z12 workshop example uses $2,000 a month and 35–40 calls a month, while
this Loom says about $1,800 and 50–100 calls. Both are examples, not a price
list. Ask for the real figures.

## Z32 — "Restoration Website Design Services" (website Loom)

1. **Open on the outcome.** The website as "your restoration salesperson… out
   there digitally selling", followed by quick examples of recent sites (packout
   and contents, carpet cleaning, restoration).
2. **Name the old plan.** Most sites are "glorified business cards": company
   name, contact info, list of services. Pretty, but they don't sell.
3. **New plan.** A "Google-optimized digital salesperson" with two goals: humans
   (make them call and book) and Google (load speed, images, architecture).
4. **What goes on the site, with the reason for each:**
   - **Calls to action first:** call, text, form, or optionally a chat bot.
   - **Services:** at minimum water, fire and mold. A bigger site has a page per
     service (frozen or burst pipe, flooded basement, wind) "when you want to be
     absolutely dominant", because each page is another way to be found.
   - **FAQ:** shows experience, and the site answers the same questions every
     time, perfectly.
   - **Unique selling points:** response time, 24/7, insurance experience.
   - **Insurance carriers:** list the logos. The consumer doesn't know you've
     done 100 Nationwide jobs, and seeing their carrier puts them at ease.
   - **Proof, using the "lawyers must bring evidence" analogy:** reviews and
     testimonials, and before/after photos (the most neglected proof; they
     probably already take them for insurance).
   - **Service-area map:** people 40 miles out assume you won't come.
   - **Your story and team photos:** "people like to do business with real people."
   - **Contact and About pages,** plus an optional blog or blog package.
5. **What you get.** A professional site, revised "until you're completely
   happy". Turnaround is 2–8 weeks, and "the biggest actual factor… is you": the
   last 10–20% is back-and-forth.
6. **Investment.**
   - A starter site from about $2,500. Sites with many service pages run up to
     about $8,000.
   - 50% down, then a payment plan as low as $99 a month with no interest.
   - Domain (about $10–20 a year) and hosting ($59 a month if you use theirs;
     optional) are separate. He's transparent that "everybody pays it".
7. **Communication.** An update in 10–14 days, then requests for assets and
   "what do you think of this version?" The test site is built on their server
   and migrated in about 10 minutes, so there's no downtime.
8. **Standing behind it.** Two hours a month of updates are included, which
   covers most people's needs.
9. **What we need from you.** Images, the list of services, timely feedback,
   and later, access.
10. **Next step.** The form on the website, then a quote for what they want to build.

## Z34 — "Motorcycle Case Leads Loom" (PI law firms, about 4 minutes, timestamped transcript)

1. **Open on the offer in one line** (0:02). "Hey, it's Frankie here. I want to
   show you really quickly, how for your law firm, you can get motorcycle case
   leads for $40."
2. **Remove the objections up front** (0:09):
   - "That includes all costs. There's no separate advertising cost…";
   - "we've also taken off your plate… a year's worth of nurturing these leads
     and follow up."
3. **Why this niche** (0:24). "Motorcycle cases tend to be higher value cases…
   more damages."
4. **How it works** (0:38).
   - **Proof without details:** "currently doing this for a couple of lawyers
     and for legal reasons, I can't show you all the details".
   - **Targeting:** Facebook ads within about 50 miles of their location(s), at
     people who bought motorcycle accessories, ride motorcycles and bought
     helmets.
   - **Landing page and licensed book** in the firm's name. It's all included,
     with no extra setup. Compliance: "if you want to look it over just to make
     sure everything's cool with your state bar, that's all good."
   - **Pricing trigger:** they pay $40 when someone opts in to download the book.
   - **Ad examples:** "Must-have guide for bikers: who to talk to, how to handle
     your damaged bike, when to seek medical attention…"; "bikers' rights";
     "stay safe on the road with this free guide".
   - **A year-long drip campaign** (2:08), built on "the questions you get
     asked religiously":
     - thank you for requesting our guide;
     - don't try and figure this out on your own;
     - how's everything going?;
     - don't be a victim to the insurance adjuster's tactics;
     - can I sue without insurance?;
     - are you still thinking it over?;
     - how much money can I get?;
     - how much does it cost to hire a lawyer?
   - **The logic** (3:02). About 2–2.5% of these people get injured in motorcycle
     accidents, "and you'll be the first person they call because you've been
     the one who sent them the book".
5. **Offer and terms** (3:17).
   - You pay only for leads. There's no ad cost, ongoing fee or minimum, except
     a starter package for setup: **25 leads × $40 = $1,000**. He misspeaks
     ("40") and then corrects himself.
6. **Value framing** (3:51). "You could probably spend just five grand on leads
   and get one motorcycle case and the numbers would work out for you."
7. **Next step** (4:14). "If you're interested in getting a starter package or
   if you have some questions, go ahead and fill out the form below."

**Notes:**

- This is a **pay-per-lead first date**: the risk is removed, the price is
  small, and the setup is "already done for you".
- **Its figures differ from other sources.** Elsewhere Frankie cites about $10
  motorcycle leads and a 1–2% yearly crash rate [Z6, Z9, Z14]. These are
  different offers at different times, so don't merge them; ask for current
  numbers.
- The "for legal reasons I can't show you all the details" line is Frankie's own
  situation. Don't reuse it unless it's true for the user.

## Patterns across Z2, Z31 and Z32, Z34

These are observed in the client Looms and match the workshop framework [Z8, Z12]:

- **Opening line:** "Hey, it's Frankie here. I want to show you…" followed
  straight by the outcome. There's no company intro.
- **The demonstration is shown the way the owner experiences it:** a phone call,
  "found you on Google", a site that sells.
- **Who it's for, with honest conditions,** comes before or alongside how it works.
- **Old plan vs new plan** (business-card site; SEO that shows keywords nobody
  searches).
- **A 3–5-part plan with a reason for each part,** in plain language and with
  one analogy (air conditioning, marathon, courtroom evidence).
- **Price stated plainly** with what's included and what's separate.
- **Expectations are set inside the video:** timelines, "the biggest factor is
  you", 4–8 months of runway.
- **Communication terms as part of the offer:** video updates, text first,
  scheduled calls only.
- **What we need from you.**
- **A low-pressure next step and a graceful no:** "leave you better off no
  matter what".
- **Tone:** casual and honest, with light self-deprecation. Drafts for this repo
  stay profanity-free.

## Z33 — Client Onboarding Questionnaire (PDF)

**Authorship isn't stated in the file.** It's a general agency template, not in
Frankie's voice. Use it as the post-sale intake form; don't present it as
Frankie's method.

Note that Frankie's own guidance favors dead-simple onboarding: the
auto-detailing Loom has a single questionnaire of about 45 minutes, and the Loom
workshops ask only for access plus a quick welcome [Z2, Z6]. Trim the
questionnaire to what the deliverable needs.

### Sections and questions

- **Client information:**
  - primary contact and their details;
  - preferred communication method and the best times to reach them;
  - who signs off on deliverables;
  - backup contact;
  - client-facing address for each location;
  - supporting documents;
  - average customer lifetime value;
  - current customer acquisition cost;
  - gross and net margins;
  - other KPIs.
- **Marketing platform credentials:**
  - Google Analytics;
  - Search Console;
  - Google Business Profile;
  - Google, Facebook and other ad accounts;
  - social accounts;
  - other accounts.
- **Marketing information:**
  - target audience and demographics;
  - priority geographies, languages and spelling;
  - existing research or customer data;
  - top 3–5 competitors, and their strategies to admire or avoid;
  - how the product solves the customer's problem;
  - brand style guide, high-res logo location and fonts.
- **Project information:**
  - goals;
  - most successful tactic to date and why;
  - KPIs tracked;
  - 6–12-month milestones;
  - key dates and launches;
  - previous similar projects;
  - timeframe;
  - file-format requirements;
  - what the agency does vs what the client provides.
- **General:**
  - what excites them about working together;
  - support expectations;
  - how they found us;
  - report frequency and format.
