# Frankie — Closing with a 5-Minute Loom (or Google Doc)

## Why

- He reports roughly 99 in 100 deals closed without sales calls for years. [Z6]
  Outcomes after the video:
  - about 1/3 never reply;
  - about 1/3 just pay;
  - about 1/3 ask a last-minute question, usually "will this work for me?" [Z3]
- The video covers the 80/20 of a sales call. It answers the four things
  prospects want to know: [Z7, Z10]
  1. What's the result?
  2. Will it work for me?
  3. How does it work?
  4. How much is it?
- It works best on people with money and little time. Broke prospects have time
  for 90-minute calls. [Z6]
- It sifts and sorts, and makes "the offer do the selling". [Z6, Z13]
- A **Google Doc version works too**: "Pretty close… same framework." It's good
  for people who don't like video and for non-native speakers. He also sells
  with the same structure in email. [Z5, Z7]

## When to send it

- Only after a handraiser and a short chat. "Don't create video until people
  request it." [Z7]
- Frame it as the plan: "Hey, if it's cool with you, give me a day and I will
  sketch out a plan so you can see what we can potentially do for you." [Z7]
- If they offer call times, ignore them and send the video: "Hey Phil, here's a
  quick little video for what we can do. Let me know what you think." [Z6]
- If you're still doing calls, send it before the call: "we've noticed most
  people have the same kind of questions, so I put a little video together".
  Most calls then disappear or shrink to about 5 minutes. [Z9, Z10, Z12]
- Customize the first 5–10 videos so you learn from them, then reuse the same
  video. [Z7]
- Close in the same medium the handraiser started in (Facebook ad → Messenger;
  email form → email). [Z12]

## Build in reverse, present forward [Z6, Z8, Z12]

Build it starting from the CTA:

1. the call to action;
2. the price (IVQ);
3. how it works (the plan);
4. who it's for;
5. the demonstration of the result.

Spend the most thinking time on the demonstration.

Then present it in this order:

1. **Headline and dramatic demonstration** (~70–80% of the sale is made here, in
   about 15 seconds).
   - **Types:** symptom, outcome, or before/after. Show it the way **they**
     experience and measure it (a phone call saying "we found you on Google"),
     not the way you measure it (a #1 ranking). [Z6, Z14]
   - **Make it micro, not macro:** one real call, form fill or lead beats "100
     leads" or "+175% traffic". [Z17]
   - **Examples:**
     - a recorded flood call ("it's raining in the basement, can you get out here real fast?") over a screenshot of a client's leads [Z12];
     - a dentist's $40k website next to "the chair is empty" [Z6, Z15];
     - a kitchen lead email with a photo of the kitchen [Z7, Z8];
     - his inbox of payment notifications [Z6].
   - "A picture is worth a thousand words but a demonstration is worth a
     thousand pictures." [Z6]
   - **Opening line**, without introducing yourself: "Hey, if you're a
     restoration company, let me show you how we can get you emergency flood
     calls." [Z8, Z12]
2. **"This will work for you if…"** Use 1–3 **binary** conditions, not
   "coachable". Examples:
   - "at least 1,000 website visitors a month";
   - "at least two crews";
   - "you need to be able to call them back within three minutes… if you can't,
     don't buy this."

   "Nothing against you if that's not you." [Z3, Z6, Z7, Z14]
3. **How it works: 3–5 steps** (complex deliverables like SEO can run to 6–7).
   - This is the longest part. Use real screenshots, not stock images. [Z3, Z8, Z12]
   - Contrast their old plan with the new one. Answer the objections you already
     know about here, for example "When we say investor, I don't mean a broke
     real-estate-course guy…". [Z8, Z12]
   - Show that you know what you'd do after they hire you. "Biggest thing that
     stops beginners from getting clients is that if a client hired them today
     they don't know what they would actually do." [Z12, Z18]
4. **Offer and IVQ.** Recap the handraiser offer, then "The investment is… for
   that you'll typically get… it works really well if…". [Z8, Z12]
5. **Next steps for all three cases.** [Z8, Z14]
   - "If you want to get started, here's what you need to do next. Send me a
     message and say, I'm in, and I'll send you the details of what you need to
     do next."
   - "If it's not a fit for you, you can go ahead and just tell me no, you won't
     hurt my feelings."
   - "And if you have any questions left, go ahead and feel free to send me a
     message and I'll let you know whether or not it's something we can do."
   - Optional addition: "If you don't tell me no… I'm going to bother you until
     you tell me no." [Z8]
   - Spell out exactly how to buy: button → payment link → onboarding form →
     email with next steps. [Z3, Z14]

Keep it to about 5 minutes. "The first Loom video you make will always be your
worst." [Z6, Z7]

## Worked example to copy the shape from (restoration SEO, abridged) [Z12]

> "Hey, if you're a restoration company, let me show you how we can get you
> emergency flood calls. [play call] What you're looking at in this screenshot
> are all the flood calls we generated for a client of ours last month. We found
> this works really well if you've got at least two crews… Let me just show you
> really quickly how it works. … folks are looking on Google for emergency flood
> and restoration… [Google Business Profile, website, PR, links] … We're
> basically giving Google what it wants… The investment is $2,000 a month.
> Typically for that you'll get between 35–40 calls a month, and we find if you
> just close one job you're going to be profitable… If that sounds like you and
> you're interested, what you need to do next is send me a text message…"

Use this as a template only. Never reuse its numbers or claims for a different
client.

## Real client-facing example (auto-detailing website) [Z2]

- **Opening:** "Hey, it's Frankie here. And I want to show you really quickly
  what your auto detailing website can look like, as well as some of the
  benefits and reasons why we do things a little bit differently over here."
- **Honest qualifier:** works best for a physical shop.
- **Process steps:**
  1. deposit;
  2. a questionnaire of about 45 minutes;
  3. build;
  4. approve;
  5. go live;
  6. monthly updates.
- **Close:** "if you're ready to go, you don't necessarily need to talk to anybody."

## After the sale [Z6, Z7]

- **Onboarding wow:** an Edible Arrangements gift the same day, then a
  handwritten card about a week later, so they're two separate events. This
  fills the buyer's-remorse gap.
- **The welcome message** is copy-paste with a personalized first sentence.
- **Client updates:** a Loom every 2 weeks covering "the good, bad and ugly",
  instead of meetings. [Z6]

## Pre-sell content (to scale) [Z9, Z19, Z25]

1. Run 5–10 text chats after a handraiser.
2. Build the Loom.
3. Once it converts, create pre-sell content that contrasts the old plan with
   the new plan.

Example: 3–5 Facebook posts on why Loom beats sales calls, published before the
workshop offer. Pre-selling raises a prospect's "temperature" from 2–3 out of 10
to 7+. [Z19]

**Frankie's 6 P's of pre-selling** [Z25]:

- **Problem:** a binary symptom, plus what happens if they never fix it.
- **Plan:** clearly different and desirable.
- **Price:** IVQ, or an anchor.
- **Philosophy:** what you believe about the problem.
- **Personality:** be yourself; it repels people who'd dislike you later.
- **Proof.**

Nail the problem first. Travis's related "5 Ps" are in Z24.
