## A21.1: Factories hiring forklift operators ↔ warehouse staffing agencies (S39), connector

- **Demand signal:** LinkedIn Jobs, "forklift operator," last 24 hours, entry level, full-time + temporary (temporary means urgency). About 50 high-intent leads: "from about 50 you can close 1–3." Contact the **operations manager** (who controls budget) rather than HR.
- **Demand email, subject "Pre-vetted intros":** "Hey, saw you're hiring forklift operators in {city}. When those roles sit open, production slows and costs pile up fast. Before I waste your time: do you already have a partner consistently keeping those seats filled? If you already have someone great, amazing, I'll leave you to it. If not, I can get you operators through partners who specialize in warehouse environments…"
  - **Follow-up 1:** "Hey {first}, worth the intro?"
  - **Follow-up 2:** "Looks like you've already got this taken care of. If not, let me know."
- **Supply email, subject "New placements open this week":** "Hey {first}, are you currently taking more industrial placements in {state}? I'm speaking with multiple plants right now that are struggling to keep warehouse and forklift roles filled…"
- **Replies (exact), the next day:**
  - **Supply:** "Hey Saad, our initial deployments are within 3–5 days. Eager to hear more about that first intro whenever you're ready."
  - **Demand (a factory hiring forklift operators in Petersburg, VA):** "Hey Saad. Yes, please. Can you share how the process works and what you need from us to get started?"
- **Outcome:** unresolved in the video. He moved on to his "universal SOP": qualify both sides on calls, answer "who are you?" with a true identity, sell a system rather than single intros, and structure the deal before the intro.
- **Do not copy:**
  - "I'm speaking with multiple plants right now" (level 2 stated as level 3).
  - His suggested deliberate typo ("pre-veted").
  - "They don't need to know you're charging the other side" (contradicts S08 and our rules).
  - "I've vetted 50+ agencies" or "I've connected 20 companies … even if you didn't."
- **Keep:** the demand email's room-to-decline line ("If you already have someone great, amazing, I'll leave you to it") is the best example in the transcripts of giving the reader a way out.

## A21.2: USDA rural lending (S38), connector

- **His shown stats:** about 252 sequences, about a 4% reply rate, about 90% of replies positive, then one deal at about $16k ("volume is not king"). These are his claims.
- **Supply email (lenders):** "Hey Martin, have business brokers and buyers actively working rural acquisition deals right now. Need lenders who know the B&I program. Would love to send one your way."
- **Demand email:** "Hi Bob, have lenders actively approved for USDA B&I up to $25 million built for rural acquisitions. If you have deals stalling on financing, worth a quick call?"
- **Subject lines:** "B&I opportunity"; "Sanity check" (which he calls "a winning subject line").
- **Lesson:** a very small, precise campaign can produce one high-value close. Judge by money, not reply volume.

## A21.3: Agency era: funded companies plus a new CMO (S58)

- **Copy:** "Hey {first}, noticed you joined {company} as {title} about {X} weeks after their {$amount} Series {A/B} round. Congrats on the role. The 90-day mark after funding is brutal for new marketing leaders. Boards expect pipeline, but your team isn't fully built. **I know someone who helped a new {industry} CMO get 22 sales-ready leads in their first month before their first board review.** Worth a quick intro?" Subject: "{first}, quick help."
- **Shown result:** "four positive replies from seven companies."
- **Replies (exact):**
  - "Hey Saad, **how did you get my contact info? Can you share a bit more about how you helped that other CMO?**"
  - "This sounds relevant to some of the challenges we're addressing post Series A. Of course, I'll review and loop in the appropriate team members if it aligns with our priorities."
  - "I'd love to speak."
- **Why it matters:** the first reply **asks about the proof claim**. The bold line was templated with the industry swapped per lead, so it was fabricated. A prospect asking about it shows exactly where a false claim collapses.
- **Keep:** stacking two signals (funding in the last 60–90 days plus a new marketing leader in the last 30–90 days). **Never copy:** the "I know someone who helped…" line.

## A21.4: Agency era: funded biotech CEOs (S60/S61)

- **Copy:** "Hey {first}, congrats on the Series {X} round. Quick question: are you actively looking for pharma partnerships / clinical trial sites / additional Series {next} investors right now? If you're open to intros, let's chat." (One version opened "Just raised ${amount} on {date}.")
- **Replies (exact):**
  - "Hey Saad, appreciate the note. The funding was a big milestone for us. Right now we're primarily focused on scaling our core ops post-[raise], but we're definitely open to strategic pharma partnerships and trial site expansions in the near term."
  - "Thanks for reaching out. Series B is not on our table yet, but we're always open to intros if there's strong alignment. Send your calendar."
  - "We're not actively seeking new investors just yet."
- **Lessons:**
  - A list question ("partnerships / trial sites / investors?") lets the prospect pick what's live.
  - "Not now" replies still give useful information.
  - His KPI: "Always track only reply rates and actual money made."
- **Do not copy:** he credits a subject-line typo with "triggering a reply." No deliberate typos.

## A21.5: Agency era: a cold campaign for a company that didn't know him (S19)

- **Setup:** about 700 pharma leads, with a "connector angle" email on behalf of Vamstar, a company that had no idea he existed.
- **Result:** **2 positive replies** ("Hey Saad, yeah, definitely interested in intro. Can you connect us?"). Then he emailed Vamstar's CEO: "Hey [name], got a few strategic partners that would be aligned with [company]'s vision. Mind if I make the intro?"
- **Why it's here:** a low-result case. About 700 sends produced 2 positives, a useful contrast with the trigger-based connector campaigns.
- **Do not copy:** emailing "on behalf of" a company without permission, and citing its case studies, is misrepresentation.

## A21.6: The S23 "$20K close", a role-play, not a real deal

- **What it is:** presented as a "live raw recording" of a closed $20k deal. At the end it's revealed to be a role-play with a member of his community.
- **What it shows:** a demand-side call (a founder after an exit, needing wealth advisors), including:
  - the frame-setting opener;
  - "Which firms are you with?";
  - "What if it doesn't work?" (he separates what he controls from what he doesn't);
  - price under time pressure: $15k for 60–90 days became **$20k for a 30-day priority window** ("same work, shorter clock means sharper focus, not cheaper work");
  - a split payment ($10k upfront + $10k on the first qualified conversation);
  - declining references while offering a written process summary;
  - "send me a proposal."
- **Already analyzed** in the objection notes (in the drafts). **Do not copy:** "doubling down" when called out, using pressure, "not really" in answer to "any hidden fees?", and the vague credentials.
- **Status:** **role-play.** Never count it as a real outcome.
