## 2. The outreach (exact)

**To beekeepers (supply):**
> "Hi Brian, reaching out because I connect local beekeepers with food companies. I do this independently, I don't represent them. I've got a food company in California looking to buy honey in bulk directly from beekeepers. Bulk buyers paid $2.45 a pound on average last year. Would selling straight to a food company be worth it?"

**To food companies (demand):**
> "Hi Michelle, reaching out because I connect food companies with local beekeepers who have honey to sell. I do this independently, I don't represent them. I've got a beekeeper in California looking to supply honey directly to food manufacturers. Honey prices went up 27% last year. Would buying straight from the beekeeper help your margins?"

**What the lines are for, in his words:**

- **"I do this independently, I don't represent them":** "A lot of these prospects would tell you, are you representing them? Are you working on their behalf? … you're preempting the objection beforehand."
- **The market stat:** "This is coming from actual research … even though you've never worked in this industry before, you actually know some deep intel."
- **The CTA:** a money question ("worth it?", "help your margins?"), following his "money backwards law."

## 5. Decision analysis

| Moment | What he did | Why (his words / **inference**) | Not done |
|---|---|---|---|
| Choosing who pays | Charged the **buyer**, not the supplier | **Inference:** the buyer revealed the fear (running out) and asked about his fee. "I'm not trying to extract as much money as possible" | Charging both sides |
| "What's in this for you?" | Answered it inside the close with the exact fee | She asked directly, so the answer is direct | Hiding the fee, or moving to a call to "discuss pricing" |
| The supplier's question he couldn't answer (minimum volume) | Reframed it as the reason they fit, without inventing a number | "I didn't answer that question because I don't know the minimum. So I reframed it… you guys should talk about this, not ask me" | Guessing a number, or delaying to find out |
| Before charging | Googled the supplier to confirm they're real | Avoiding embarrassment, and protecting the replacement promise | Charging on the reply alone |
| Timing | Closed within 24 hours of launch, once both replies matched | "We're going to stop talking and actually pull the trigger" | More qualifying rounds |

## 6. Frankie comparison

- **The CTA is an outcome question tied to money.** That's Frankie's "money backwards" idea: tie it to making or saving money.
- **Reading the buyer's real fear** (running out, not price) is Frankie's symptom work. Saad found it from a reply rather than from interviews.
- **Frankie's IVQ price line** ("Investment, Value, Qualify") maps onto this close: fee, then what you get, then who it works for. The only change is that Saad promises the introduction, not an outcome.

## 7. Rules from this case

**Transferable:**

- The opener "I do this independently, I don't represent them" heads off the "whose side are you on?" objection.
- One researched market number in the first email signals insight.
- Charge the side that fears missing out most, and answer "what's in it for you?" with the number.
- When you don't know an answer, reframe it as the reason to talk, never invent it.
- The free side gets "no fee or obligation; my arrangement is with [the other side]."

**Specific to this market:** honey prices and volume terms (55-gallon drums, truckloads).

**Do not copy / caution:** "I've got a food company in California looking to buy honey in bulk" was written before any food company had replied. It's level 2 data stated as level 3. Under our rules, say something like "Food manufacturers in California are paying record prices for bulk honey…" until a buyer has actually engaged.
