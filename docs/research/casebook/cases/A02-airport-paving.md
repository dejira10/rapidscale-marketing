## 2. The outreach (exact)

**To airports (demand), opportunity handraiser:**
> "Hey Tom, I have paving contractors who've already bid on airport runways and taxiways, and they're looking for their next airport job. Want me to point a couple of them at your next bid? More bidders, better price."

**To contractors (supply):**
> "Hey Michelle, I have airports in Illinois that want more contractors bidding on their next runway and taxiway job. Thanks for reading. Also, no need for a call. I can easily make the introduction."

The supply email has no question. It ends on "no need for a call." That line later triggered skepticism.

## 4. The close email (exact)

> "David, taking this from the other thread so I can send you the opportunity properly.
>
> Quick clarification: I'm not representing a sub and I'm not selling you data. I find airports with upcoming work, qualify what they're looking for, and charge the contractor for the direct introduction.
>
> I have one Illinois airport right now that told me they're open to additional paving bidders. Their exact requirement was pretty simple: they need contractors that are pre-cleared for IDOT DOA aviation specs and have experience on a 7,000+ foot runway. They asked me to send over the firm names and DOT registration IDs so their engineering side can review them.
>
> From what I found on Plote, you've already done major airport paving work on runways well over 7,000 feet, so you looked like an obvious company to put in front of them.
>
> My fee for the qualified introduction is $8,600. Invoice is attached. Once that's taken care of, I'll put you directly on a clean thread with the airport contact and let your team send over the exact DOT registration and clarification details they want engineering to review.
>
> If the airport materially does not match what I described above, I'll replace the introduction at no cost."

Stripe product: **"Qualified Illinois airport introduction."** The description: "Direct introduction to an Illinois municipal airport currently open to review an additional qualified paving contractor for upcoming aviation work."

## 5. Decision analysis

| Moment | What he did | Why (his words / **inference**) | Not done |
|---|---|---|---|
| Choosing who pays | Charged only the contractor | Charging both "would slow the experiment down"; airports need nurturing and calls | Charging both sides |
| The skeptical "what's your angle?" | Answered the skepticism **first**, in one plain sentence, then gave the fee | He asked for the fee, so he gets the fee. "Never hedge." | Defending, explaining at length, or offering a call |
| The airport's request for "firm names and DOT IDs" | Didn't send the names. He got the contractor to pay first, then put both on one thread | "The whole value is we know Tom." Names before payment means they can go around you | Forwarding the names for free |
| Verifying fit | An hour of public-records research (authorized bidders, project history) | "This is what I mean by I've done like an hour of research … these things require actual looking" | Asking the contractor to prove qualifications |
| Moving channels | Both threads moved to his own Gmail | "This is like an actual commercial conversation" | Staying in the cold email tool |

## 6. Frankie comparison

- Frankie's warning that "let's jump on a call" kills replies is the mirror image of this case. Saad's "no call needed" line caused suspicion of a different kind ("what's your angle?"). Both point the same way: don't make the first email about the call at all.
- Answering the skeptic's direct question straight away matches Frankie: "If asked for the price out of sequence, just tell them."

## 7. Rules from this case

**Transferable:**

- Sell to the losers of public bids. A lost bid means motivation, capacity and qualification.
- When a prospect asks "what's your angle / what's your fee," answer in the first line, plainly.
- When the free side asks for names, treat it as interest. Don't send names; close the paying side first.
- Name the payment product after the opportunity, not the service.

**Specific to this market:** state procurement portals, IDOT/FAA rules, runway specs.

**Do not copy / caution:**

- "No need for a call" in a first email attracted suspicion. Use it carefully, or not at all.
- "I have airports in Illinois that want more contractors" was sent before any airport had replied. It's level 2 data written as a level 3 claim. Under our rules, say "Illinois airports are posting runway and taxiway work…" until an airport has actually engaged.
