## 1. What he knew before sending

- **Trigger data:** state drilling permits, which list the operator, the filer and an email. "Freshest permits first."
- **The skill's lists:** about 1,000 oil companies and 2,500 service companies across 16 states.
- **Price logic:** contracts run $5–50M, so $6,500 is small.
- **Delivery definition:** "I'm not going to handle any booking, any scheduling … that's not my deliverable … they are grown adults."
- **Honesty level at send:** level 2. A permit approval is a strong public signal.

## 2. The outreach (exact)

**To oil operators (demand):**
> "Hi [Linda], reaching out because I connect oil companies with local crews looking for work. I do this independently, I don't represent them. I've got a couple of crews in Texas with their own trucks and equipment. Do you need extra crews to keep things running?"

**To service companies (supply):**
> "Hey Lorena, reaching out because I connect local crews with oil companies. I do this independently, I don't represent them. I've got a couple of oil companies in Texas that could use another crew. Before I send anyone your way, would you let me know if you have room for more jobs?"

He uses industry terms: "crews," "producer," "rig location." There's no social proof.

## 4. The close email (exact as read)

> "Taking this over to my proper email. I run [business], and basically what I do is find oil companies that need outside crews and connect them directly with oilfield service companies looking for more work. I found an oil company based in Midland that just had several permits approved in Andrews County. I spoke directly with their contact and she told me they might have a few openings for extra crews next month. She specifically mentioned MSA compliance, which you already have covered. Since you told me you have room for more work in Texas and are open to paying a referral fee, I figured this was a pretty obvious fit.
>
> Here's exactly what you're getting for $6,500: one direct email introduction to the contact at this oil company. I'll introduce you both on the same thread, explain what they're looking for, mention your available crews and equipment, and hand the conversation over to you. You'll have their name, company, direct email, and the context of our conversation. From there, you can discuss the actual work, locations, rates and scheduling directly with them. No middlemen, no calls with me. … If the oil company materially doesn't match what I've described, I'll replace the introduction with another qualified opportunity."

His note on the deliverable: "So now they can imagine the actual delivery. It's not abstract anymore."

## 6. Frankie comparison

- **Making the deliverable easy to picture** is Frankie's "make it so simple a child could understand."
- **Verifying instead of asking** keeps momentum. That fits Frankie's "Don't send a wall of questions."

## 7. Rules from this case

**Transferable:**

- Fresh permits are a strong demand signal.
- If a requirement can be checked publicly, check it yourself; don't add a round trip.
- Industries that "usually go through brokers" are pre-sold on paying for introductions.
- Describe the deliverable so concretely the buyer can picture it.

**Specific to this market:** drilling permits, MSA status, Texas counties.

**Do not copy:**

- "I run [business] and basically what I do is find oil companies that need outside crews…" He admits on camera: "There's some [expletive] here … I started doing this today, but I can say that technically." That's an overclaim. Truthful version: "I track new drilling permits in Texas and connect operators with crews that have capacity."
- "I've got a couple of crews in Texas" was sent at level 2.
