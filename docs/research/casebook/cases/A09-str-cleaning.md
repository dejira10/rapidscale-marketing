## 4. The close and the warm-up (exact as read)

**To the cleaner (the ROI framing):**
> "Thanks for the kind message. Thought I'd take this over from the other thread so I can handle it properly. This is Saad, I run [business]. We help businesses get customers by finding people who are already looking for exactly what they do. I have an Airbnb owner in New Orleans running multiple active listings with constant turnovers, including guests who check out and check in the same day. Her current cleaner can't keep up with that volume and she asked me to send her somebody who can. **So this isn't a one-off clean. It's her ongoing turnover work, week after week, for as long as she's running these properties**, which is pretty much exactly the kind of account you told me you're after, with recurring schedules and last-minute cleanings. The way I'm structuring this is $3,000 for the introduction. Invoice below. Once that's taken care of, I'll introduce you both directly by email, and you can handle the cleaning schedule, pricing, property access and whatever else from there. If the host clearly does not fit what I described, I replace them at no cost."

His comment: "I show them the ROI of the opportunity … Never look at the actual work that you do in front of your computer, but look at the ROI that your work creates."

**To the host:**
> "…No, I'm not with the city. I promise I'm not about to ask for your permit number. … So I found somebody for you. Their local cleaning company already handles Airbnbs and vacation rentals in New Orleans. They're taking new properties right now and they can handle recurring turnovers as well as some last-minute cleanings depending on the day, which sounds pretty close to what you were asking for with the Sunday turnovers and same-day check-ins. I'm putting the introduction together right now."

His comment: "I'm leading the interaction here. You always need to lead the commercial relationship." His notes say the honest answer to "how did you get my email" is the public registration data.

## 5. Decision analysis

| Moment | What he did | Why (his words / **inference**) | Not done |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Are you with the city? How did you get my email?" | A light, honest answer and a joke, then straight to her need | **Inference:** trust questions from an interested prospect need a short true answer, not a defense | Evading, or a long explanation |
| The host already had a cleaner | Treated it as a capacity gap | "Send the emails instead of trying to predict everything" | Disqualifying her |
| Pricing an arbitrary-looking $3,000 | Framed the ongoing account value | "Look at the ROI that your work creates" | Discounting or justifying effort |
| Timing | Moved fast once both sides confirmed | "She probably would have [solved it herself] within a week or two because her problem was urgent and she's resourceful" | More research |
| After payment | Intro, then stopped | "I'm not gonna be their cleaning manager. I don't negotiate rates. The introduction was the delivery" | Managing the relationship |

His post-mortem:

1. "We emailed both sides simultaneously … creating forces on both ends of the same transaction."
2. "The replies built the deal": both corrections came from people in the industry.
3. Once both sides confirm, the research is done.
4. Stop after the intro.

## 7. Rules from this case

**Transferable:**

- Recurring services are about capacity: "already has a provider" isn't a disqualifier.
- Send, don't predict. Let replies correct your filters.
- Answer "how did you get my email?" with the public source, lightly.
- Make a flat fee feel small by framing the ongoing account it opens.
- Lead the commercial relationship ("I'm putting the introduction together right now").

**Specific to this market:** city short-term-rental registration data.

**Do not copy:** "I work with a few cleaning crews here" and "I keep hearing the same complaints" were sent at level 2, before any cleaner had engaged. Use the symptom as a question until it's true ("Sunday no-shows are common in short-stay turnovers. Is that an issue for you?").
