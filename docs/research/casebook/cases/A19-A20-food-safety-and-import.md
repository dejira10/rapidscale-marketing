# A19–A20: Food safety and food import: the watermelon rule and honest match counting (S01, S02)

These two early videos are where Saad introduces the watermelon rule (S01) and the honest "connection table" (S02). Neither shows a payment. Both are **unresolved**, with replies routed to short calls.

---

## A19: Food makers ↔ labs, auditors and certifiers (S01)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Market lens** | Forced spending: food safety testing, certification and audits are required. "Every time someone is forced to pay something, somebody else wants that money." |
| **Data** | USDA Organic Integrity database (owner name, email, phone, certification number). A certification number is proof they already pay for this; a recent one means something just happened |
| **Planned pricing** | Supplier: $2,589–5,564 for 3–6 food makers over 60–90 days. Food maker: $1,997 sourcing. Replacement promise on both |
| **Outcome** | **Unresolved.** Positive replies on both sides within hours; routed to calls |

**Outreach (exact):**

- **To suppliers:** "Hey [first], I have certified food makers (snack, beverage and sauce brands already in production) actively lining up lot testing and their next audits right now. … Would love to send one your way."
- **To food makers:** "I have accredited food labs and certification partners actively taking on new food brands right now."

**The watermelon rule (exact):**
> "Business language makes very simple questions sound complicated. But once you turn the same situation into watermelons, your brain usually knows the answer immediately. … You just type in what they said, which is the actual reply. Then the watermelon version, which means what they are really asking, explained like we are two idiots selling watermelons. And then the watermelon man version, which is the exact words you send back."

**Replies and answers (exact):**

| They said | Watermelon version (his) | His reply |
|---|---|---|
| Supplier (certifier): "Can you elaborate on what kind of audits they need?" | "You tell me you have people who want watermelons. What kind do they actually want?" | "Mostly certifications and renewal audits for food makers already in production. I'm qualifying the exact names before I send anyone over. Worth a quick call so I can understand what you handle best?" |
| Food company (organic truffle products): "We're always interested in good testing as we bring new products. What kind of testing are we talking about?" | "Yep, I'm buying watermelons as I launch new stuff. What kind do you have?" | "Mostly product and lot testing around new launches, plus certification support where needed. Sounds like there could be a fit with what you're bringing out. Worth a quick call?" (He mirrored "new launches.") |
| Food company (supplements): [asks if they handle dietary and protein supplements] | "Do your watermelon people also handle this specific kind?" | "Yes, some do. I can narrow it down to labs that handle dietary and protein supplements specifically. Worth a quick call so I can understand what you're launching and only send the right ones?" |
| Prospect: "Yes, when can we talk?" | "Yes, I want the watermelons. When can we talk?" | "Absolutely. I can make time this week. Send over a couple of times that work for you and I'll get one locked." Then: "There's no selling. They already crossed the bridge." |
| Food company (expanding in the US) | "I'm expanding my shop. What can your people help with?" | "Mostly product testing, certification and audit support for food makers expanding distribution. Sounds like there could be a good fit for what you're doing in the US. Worth a quick call so I can understand what you need and only send the right partners?" |

**Scale math (his):** $30k a month = 4 supply-side ($3,589) + 8 demand-side ($1,997) = 12 sales, "basically one food safety company per week and two food companies per week … it's not some mystical hidden secret."

## A20: Foreign food brands ↔ US importers and distributors (S02)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Market lens** | "Ghost industries." "What had to happen before this?" Ramen in your kitchen → a store → an importer → a foreign brand that wanted the US |
| **Data** | Foreign governments' export-program lists; US trade-show exhibitor lists (some state the US as a target market): "companies that have already raised their hand"; association membership lists for importers |
| **Planned pricing** | Brand: $4,997 sourcing. Distributor: $6–9k for 3–6 brands over 60–90 days |
| **Outcome** | **Unresolved.** Several replies on both sides; calls being booked; potential "today" counted at $16–19k |

**Outreach (exact):**

- **To distributors (subject "new import brands"):** "Hey [first], I have international food and beverage brands (Spain, Italy, Japan, Turkey) actively looking for a US importer or distributor right now. … Are you taking on new import brands right now?"
- **To brands:** "I work with US importers and distributors who are actively taking on new international food brands right now. Would love to send one your way."
- **Follow-up:** "Quick check: more brands came through this week before I keep sending intros … If it's not the right time, one word back and I'll stop. Otherwise, 15 minutes to scope."

**Replies and answers (exact):**

| They said | His reply |
|---|---|
| Brand (canned fish, Argentina): "Hello Saad. Yes, we are very interested. We are a producer of canned fish here in Argentina and we are looking to work with distributors in the US. Can we speak tomorrow? We can send you our catalog and products also." | "Thanks for getting back to me. Absolutely. I can call tomorrow at [X] or [Y]. Which one works better for you? Also, feel free to send the catalog over." |
| A distributor wanting a call | Two time options, plus: "**I'll also send over a quick overview of the brands I'm working with before the call.**" High level, no names. "Now when they are on the call, they're not evaluating whether the opportunities are real, because they saw them before the call." |
| A formal brand (very professional tone) | He matched the formality: "Thanks for getting back to me. Absolutely. I'm working with US importers and distributors that are open to new international brands. I can do [X] or [Y] for a quick call. Which one works better for you?" |

**The honest connection table:**
> "I'm only counting matches where both sides literally told us that they need each other. Not matches where I think it could work."

- **Not counted:** the Argentine canned-fish brand. No distributor had said it handles canned fish or Argentine products.
- **Not counted:** a Midwest distributor of Mexican and Hispanic food. No matching brand had replied yet.
- **Counted:** matched pairs only, worth **$16–19k** "today."

**Scale math (his):** $30k a month = 3 distributors + 3 brands, about 6 sales. "I wouldn't lower [the price]."

**Personal brand as the moat:** "Selling access is a business, but being known as the person who does it is the moat."

2. Attend the industry's trade shows (Fancy Food Show, Expo West…).
3. Keep a private directory of patterns and publish a free quarterly "state of the industry."

## Decision analysis (both)

| Moment | What he did | Why | Not done |
|---|---|---|---|
| Any confusing reply | Watermelon translation before writing | "Your brain usually knows the answer immediately" | Over-thinking industry jargon |
| Every answer | Mirrored their words; promised to "only send the right ones" | Relevance, plus a reason for the call that serves them | Sending lists or names |
| A pre-call overview | A high-level summary of opportunities, no names | Removes "are these real?" before the call | Revealing companies |
| Counting pipeline | Both-sides-confirmed only | "Just because we want to be honest" | Inflating the pipeline |

## Frankie comparison

- **The watermelon rule** is a cousin of Frankie's "friend test": "If a friend said 'it's been slow', you'd ask 'what do you mean?'" Both strip out sales language and keep plain human meaning.
- **The pre-call overview** is a small version of Frankie's pre-call Loom: "We've noticed most people have the same questions, so I put a little video together."

## Rules from these cases

**Transferable:**

- The watermelon rule for every reply.
- Mirror their words, and give a reason for the next step that benefits them ("so I only send the right ones").
- Send a high-level, nameless overview before any call.
- Count only matches where both sides said they need each other.
- Match their tone (formal gets formal).

**Do not copy:** "I have certified food makers … actively lining up lot testing," "I have international food and beverage brands … actively looking," and "I work with US importers … actively taking on new brands" were all sent at level 2. The data showed a category (certified makers, exhibitors that named the US), not engaged parties.
