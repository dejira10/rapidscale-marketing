## 4. His dissection of why the opener worked

1. **"It opens with the movement, not identity."** "Tracking manufacturers entering active trouble windows feels alive … you're near the fire. You're not selling a bucket from far away."
2. **A clear role.** One side, the other side, "I'm the bridge." "No fog, no agency sludge."
3. **It doesn't over-close.** "If X is relevant on your side right now, I can send the details" asks permission to continue, not for a call. "Strangers do not like being moved too fast."
4. **Timing.** "It showed time when the problem is real."

His summary lines:

- The skeleton: "I see a real event. I sit between the two sides of that event. If this is live for you, I can show you something."
- "People don't buy because you're useful; they buy because the moment feels true."
- "People love to feel chosen, not blasted."
- "**When interest is real, prospects start doing labor.** They call, they clarify, they suggest alternatives, they keep the thread alive."
- "The job of the copy is to create enough realness that the next step feels stupid not to take."

## 7. Rules from this case

**Transferable:**

- Open with movement ("I'm tracking X entering Y"), not your identity.
- The first ask is permission to send details, not a call.
- "Have you worked in X?" means "Are you real?" Answer with what's true, and show why this situation is live.
- Ask when they're free. Let them do the work of scheduling.
- Watch for "labor" (clarifying, proposing times) as the real interest signal.

**Specific to this market:** FDA warning letters, Form 483s, CGMP.

**Do not copy:**

- "**Yes, I have worked with cannabis before**" is very likely untrue in this context (nothing in the transcripts supports it). A truthful version: "Not cannabis specifically. What I track is manufacturers in active FDA remediation windows, and this one looked like a real window, not a slow advisory job. Happy to walk you through it. When are you free?"
- The social-proof lines ("one client added $105k in 90 days…") are his unverified claims. Never reuse them.
- "I came across a manufacturer recently hit by the FDA" is fine **only** if it refers to a real, specific warning letter.
