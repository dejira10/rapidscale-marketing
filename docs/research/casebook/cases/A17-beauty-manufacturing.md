Why this case matters: it shows **how he thinks between the replies and the close**: matching, finding leverage, short replies, and answering "who are you?"

## 1. What he knew before sending

- **His thinking method:** "What had to happen before this?" A viral beauty product needed a manufacturer, which needed a founder to choose one, which came after the founder filed a trademark. "A beauty brand is born on paper before it's born in a bottle."
- **Data:** USPTO trademark filings, Class 003 (cosmetics, skincare, fragrance). About 3,000 a month, about 600 reachable brands a month, often with the founder's email.
- **One brand, several opportunities:** a manufacturer, packaging, fulfillment, and compliance (MoCRA, the US cosmetics safety law).
- **Honesty level at send:** level 2.

## 2. The outreach (exact)

**To brands (demand). Subject: "Manufacturing":**
> "I have manufacturers actively looking to take on new beauty brands right now. … Have you sorted out who's making it yet?"

**Brand follow-up:**
> "Leaving the door open: when you're ready to get it made, I'm one reply away."

**To manufacturers (supply):**
> "I have beauty brands that filed their trademark in the last 90 days actively looking for someone to make their product. Before I send anyone, I wanted to understand capacity."

**Manufacturer follow-up:**
> "Quick check: more brands filed this week before I keep reaching out. Didn't want to keep you if new accounts aren't in your lane right now. Do you have capacity for new brand accounts?"

It's a 4-step sequence, with a daily limit of up to 1,000.

## 4. The routing matrix

He mapped each brand's need against each supplier's offer:

- **A brand founder wanting simple haircare:** a no-minimum manufacturer **or** the turnkey manufacturer. "If [the first] gives us some [expletive], we can just send this intro to [the second]." That's his **leverage:** alternatives if one side stalls.
- **A founder with two trademarks (likely several products):** the turnkey manufacturer, which "can help from the product idea": repeat business.
- **Total potential:** 4 brands × $1,997, plus 5 manufacturers × $3,589–6,564, ≈ **$25.9k–40.9k**.

"Don't name the other party, to avoid being cut out." "Holding access is like holding a door: both sides want each other; you hold the door."

## 5. Decision analysis

| Moment | What he did | Why (his words / **inference**) | Not done |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Who are you with?" | Company, a one-line role, congratulations, then a question back to them | Positioning as a connector makes them evaluate the opportunity | A long company pitch |
| An eager "tell me more" | (Ideally) one line: "Absolutely. What are you looking to manufacture right now?" | Long explanations are a mistake; ask the next obvious question | Sending details or a list |
| A sender-name mismatch | "That inbox handles initial outreach. I'm the founder, jumping in here" | Status, and the truth | Hiding it |
| Matching | A routing matrix, with backups for each brand | Leverage if one side stalls | One-to-one matching only |

## 6. Frankie comparison

- **The routing matrix** is the connector's version of Frankie's offer matrix: who needs what, who can deliver it.

## 7. Rules from this case

**Transferable:**

- "What had to happen before this?" leads you to the earliest public trace of the event.
- Eager reply: one line, and the next obvious question.
- "Who are you with?": company, role in one line, congratulations, and a question back.
- Build a routing matrix with a backup supplier for each buyer.
- Answers come from what the market told you. Never invent them.

**Specific to this market:** USPTO Class 003, MoCRA, minimum order quantities.

**Do not copy:** "I have manufacturers actively looking…" and "I have beauty brands … actively looking…" were sent at level 2. The filings prove a brand exists, not that it's actively looking. Use something like "You filed [brand] in the last 90 days. Have you sorted out who's making it yet?" (a discovery handraiser).
