> **Sensitivity:** these cases involve workplace deaths. Saad says one case "broke my heart." Copy must stay factual and never use the incident for emotional leverage.

## 1. What he knew before sending

- **The lane:** companies named publicly by OSHA after a fatality or explosion. The record is public: name, what happened, the fine. "Now that company desperately needs someone to come in and fix everything."
- **Demand data:** his own Apify scraper ("OSHA violation deal routing signals"). Filters: fatality required, willful violation required, minimum penalty in USD. Output includes company, location, what happened, penalty, urgency.
- **Who to contact at the cited company, in order:** CEO → Director of Environmental Health & Safety → VP Operations → Plant Manager → Safety Manager.
- **Supply data:** Instantly lead finder ("national safety consultant"), about 200 contacts.
- **Honesty level at send:** level 2.

## 2. The outreach (exact)

**To safety consultants (supply). Subject: "Safety remediation":**
> "Hey [name], I have companies that just received OSHA citations actively looking for safety remediation support right now. Before I route anyone anywhere, wanted to understand your capacity and whether the fit is there. Worth a quick call?"

In his words, it does three things: "(1) I have demand, (2) I'm not randomly sending it, (3) I need to know if you can handle it."

**To cited companies (demand), the inverted version:**
> "Hey [name], I have a couple safety operators who work with companies after OSHA cases involving [confined space and tank cleaning hazards]. Before I route anyone anywhere, wanted to understand if the issue is still active on your side and whether the fit is there. Worth a quick call?"

It does: "(1) I have relevant supply, (2) I'm not throwing vendors at you, (3) I'm checking if the issue is still active before I route anyone."

**Follow-up sequence:**

- **Step 2:** "Hey [first name], do you have capacity for new engagements right now?"
- **Step 3:** "Leaving the door open: when you're ready for more remediation work, I'm one reply away."
- **Step 4:** "Door stays open."

Sending runs Monday–Saturday, 7 am – 7:30 pm.

## 4. Positioning and the thesis

- **His website:** "research and introductions"; published theses and current signals, with "email me if any of these are yours"; "currently accepting two new counterparties this quarter." In his words: "selective in a very gentle premium way."
- **The thesis** for a match from this campaign, sent with the invoice before revealing the intro:
  - **Header:** signal date, urgency 5/5, lane fit A+.
  - **Stakes reframed:** "This is not a $600k penalty match. It's a $5–15M exposure match where the penalty is [a small fraction]. The customer doesn't need a safety consultant. They need a third-party remediator credible enough to stand between them and the next fatality."
  - **Why this supplier:** third-party credibility, fall-protection specialization, construction-industry depth.
- **His thesis prompt:** "Take a match between a demand-side company and a supply-side provider, then write a sharp operator-grade thesis explaining why the match matters." He wrote them by hand first because "the AI model was inventing stuff."

## 7. Rules from this case

**Transferable:**

- The three moves of the supply copy: I have demand / I'm not random / can you handle it.
- "Is this lead gen?" means: agree with the true part, reposition with their own word, ask about their fit.
- A second pushback means pull away calmly and leave the door open.
- Answer "what do you do?" in one line, plus a sharp market insight, plus a qualifying question.
- Speed of reply (minutes) suggests a live, under-served market.

**Specific to this market:** OSHA data, the decision-maker fallback order, fatality-related sensitivity.

**Do not copy:**

- "I have companies that just received OSHA citations **actively looking** for safety remediation support" was sent at level 2. The citations were true; "actively looking" wasn't confirmed.
- His descriptions of prospects ("toddlers," crude terms for being tested, "the ego shatters").
- The emotional detail of the fatality in any copy.
- Interpreting the asked-for call as a win before money changes hands: the outcome is unknown.
