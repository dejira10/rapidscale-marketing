## 2. The outreach (exact)

**To schools (demand). Subject: "Your internet request from September 15":**
> "Hi Danielle, your September 15 request for internet access opens for signing on October 13. About one in nine requests like this one in Florida draw a single bid or none at all. Less competition sounds fine until someone asks you to show the price was fair. Is the request still open? Happy to point one more company at it before you go to sign — one that's already done this work for schools in Florida."

**To losing vendors (supply):**
> "Hey Philip, you bid on the battery backup UPS contracts at Seminole County Public Schools in December; [winner] took it. A school in [state] is buying Wi-Fi and cabling right now and hasn't picked anyone yet. Want me to put you in front of it before it signs?"

**What each email tests:**

| Email | Hook | Question | Tests |
|---|---|---|---|
| School | Their own dated request, plus a risk (a single bid looks unfair) | "Is the request still open?" | Whether the need is still live (discovery) |
| Vendor | Their own lost bid, plus a live replacement | "Want me to put you in front of it before it signs?" | Interest and timing |

Saad calls the school email "one of the best copies." The statistic ("one in nine") should be verified before reuse.

## 4. The close and the warm-up

**To the vendor (exact as read):**
> "[I run a business.] I spend a lot of time around K-12 technology infrastructure and vendor-side deals, particularly where schools are actively buying and the right company just needs to get in front of the opportunity. Taking this over from the other thread so I can send you the live project properly. I have a Florida school district that looks like it could fit what you've described. They're currently buying network equipment for existing school sites, primarily HPE Aruba switches and Extreme Wi-Fi 7 equipment. [The contact] confirmed the request is still open and said if [you] can handle the deployment schedule, she'll look at getting you into the [process]. My fee for the qualified introduction is $10,000; invoice attached. Once payment is taken care of, I'll introduce you directly. If the school clearly does not fit what I described, I replace the introduction."

Subject: "[County] School Project." The RFP documents were attached. Stripe product: "Qualified K-12 technology customer introduction: one qualified introduction to a Florida school district with an active Wi-Fi network infrastructure requirement."

**To the school (exact as read):**
> "I work on the commercial side of matching companies into live buying demand. Right now I'm working through a handful of Florida K-12 technology opportunities where the district already has an active requirement and the vendor has to actually be capable of delivering it. ## 5. Decision analysis

| Moment | What he did | Why (his words / **inference**) | Not done |
|---|---|---|---|
| Choosing the market | Government-funded buying with public bids | "What kind of businesses does the government give money to?" Public bids create public losers | Generic B2B targeting |
| "Whatever finder's fee you need" | Went straight to the close with a fixed fee | Pre-sold; no more qualifying needed | Haggling over the fee, or asking for a call |
| The school's requirements | Mirrored them exactly in both emails (cutover timing, E-Rate) | **Inference:** her own words are the strongest proof of fit | Generic capability claims |
| The school email | Added the **repeat-source line** | "Instead of chasing a brand new $10,000 customer every single time, I might have the same company paying me again and again." He wants to be "the guy who keeps bringing us Florida school projects" | A one-off mindset |
| Tone at the close | "Institutional language … I'm being very careful because I haven't worked in this industry before" | He raises formality at the money stage | Casual cold-email tone |

## 6. Frankie comparison

- **The school email is a perfect Frankie-style symptom handraiser,** but the symptom is regulatory and social ("someone asks you to show the price was fair") rather than operational.
- **The repeat-source line** matches Frankie's "shoe store" idea: one relationship that keeps sending buyers.

## 7. Rules from this case

**Transferable:**

- Dated, specific public facts make the best opening line ("your September 15 request…").
- Name a risk the buyer will recognize, then ask whether the need is still live.
- "Whatever fee you need" means close now.
- Put the buyer's own requirements in the close, and attach their documents.
- End the free-side email with the repeat-source line, turning a one-off into a recurring source of deals.

**Specific to this market:** E-Rate rules and deadlines, district RFP portals.

**Do not copy:** "I spend a lot of time around K-12 technology infrastructure and vendor-side deals." He admits on camera: "Not really, but I did spend the last hour trying to understand this. So technically I can say that." That's an overclaim. Truthful alternative: "I track K-12 technology requests in Florida and connect districts with vendors who can deliver them."
