## 1. What he knew before sending

- **Forced demand:** every batch must be lab-tested (a certificate of analysis) before it can be sold.
- **Scarcity:** about 135 farms per lab in California, so labs are the scarce side. **The abundant side pays.**
- **Pricing from first principles:** four questions (how bad is the problem, how badly do they need it gone, how hard is it to get, what's blocked). "They're paying because there is money trapped behind that problem": a harvest can't be sold without the test.
- **Data:**
  - **Farms:** the state cannabis licensing database (license dates and numbers).
  - **Labs:** a list of licensed labs.
  - **A ledger** so nobody gets emailed twice.
- **Industry knowledge:** "30 minutes before this, I didn't know shit about weed testing … I read the documents. It took me 30 minutes."
- **Honesty level at send:** level 2.

## 2. The outreach (exact)

**To farms (demand):**
> "Hey Ross, saw Apex Agriculture is licensed in Yolo County. I work with testing labs that pick up on time and turn a harvest around fast, so a finished batch isn't sitting unsold waiting on a certificate. Would love to send one your way."

**To labs (supply):**
> "Hey Jeff, I work with licensed [cannabis] farms and the makers turning that flower into products: operations that pay a lab on every single batch by law. Before I send anyone anywhere, I wanted to understand what you run and how fast. What's your turnaround right now?"

**Variant follow-up to farms:**
> "When's the next harvest coming down? Who's testing it?"

## 4. The close and the warm-up (exact as read)

**To the farm:**
> "Ross, taking this over from the other thread so I can handle it properly. I have the lab you asked for. They already have samplers covering your area, they handle the California compliance work you'd need, and their normal turnaround is about three business days, with one and two day rush options available on most panels. So this lines up with the two things you told me matter: they'll pick up, and they'll turn the results around quickly. The way I'm structuring this is $8,000 for the introduction. I've included the invoice below. Once that's taken care of, I'll introduce you both directly by email, and you can handle the batches, testing schedule, pricing, pickup timing and whatever else you need from there. If the lab clearly does not fit what you asked for, I replace them."

**To the lab. Subject: "Yolo grower…":**
> "[I have a newly licensed grower in Yolo who cares about] pickup and fast turnaround once you have the samples. Your California sampling coverage and standard three business days turnaround line up well with what they asked for. I'm putting the introduction together now. If everything moves forward on their side, I'll introduce you both directly by email and you can take it from there. This will be at no cost to you. **The only thing I'd ask: if this account works out, for future farms I send you, give me one person on your team I can message directly to tell me two things quickly: can you pick up from this farm's area, and roughly how fast are results turning right now? That way, when the next farm comes to me, I can know in five minutes whether you can actually help them.**"

His reasoning: "Labs aren't the rare thing … I want to keep Jeff close because it's a lab. Farms are easy to find." (He means labs **are** the scarce side, so the relationship with the lab is the asset.)

## 5. Decision analysis

| Moment | What he did | Why (his words / **inference**) | Not done |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who pays | The farm (abundant side), not the lab (scarce side) | Labs are scarce, so keep them close and free | Charging the lab |
| The farm's two criteria | Mirrored them exactly in the close ("the two things you told me matter") | "I'm literally just mirroring what both people said" | Listing every lab capability |
| More qualification | Stopped | "If one person is literally asking for something and the other person already told you they can do it, just stop making the process hard … I didn't need another 17-question qualification" | More questions |
| Names | Withheld the lab's name before payment | "This is what makes you uncuttable. Never mention the other party" | Revealing it |
| The scarce supplier | Asked for a **standing contact** instead of a fee | To resell the scarce side to many buyers quickly | A one-off relationship |

## 6. Frankie comparison

- **"Let us know how you want to be compensated"** means the price question arrived early, and Frankie and Saad agree: answer it.
- **The standing-contact ask** is a relationship investment, like Frankie's giver's-mentality outreach to "shoe stores."

## 7. Rules from this case

**Transferable:**

- Identify the scarce side; charge the abundant side and keep the scarce side close.
- When the buyer names its criteria, the close repeats exactly those criteria, met.
- Once one side asks and the other can deliver, stop researching.
- Ask the scarce supplier for one direct contact to check capacity quickly for future deals.

**Specific to this market:** state cannabis licensing, compliance testing panels. **Legal restrictions apply.**

**Do not copy:** "I work with testing labs that pick up on time…" and "I work with licensed farms…" were sent at level 2.
