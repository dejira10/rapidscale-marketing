## 1. What he knew before sending

| Item | Detail | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Market thesis | Buildings with big power bills want lower bills; solar installers want large roofs | Assumed |
| Data | City **building energy benchmarking reports** (e.g. Los Angeles), which list energy use per building and sometimes a contact name and email | Public, verified source |
| Price logic | $3,700: "High enough that this is still good money, but low enough that you don't need like a giant pile of trust to justify it." He wanted "the opportunity itself to do the selling, not my reputation" | His reasoning |

**Honesty level at send time:** buyer side = level 2 (a public signal: big energy users); supplier side = level 2 (the same list).

## 2. The outreach (exact)

**To buildings (demand), discovery/opportunity handraiser:**
> "Hey Aaron, I have solar companies that put panels on buildings like yours and they're looking for their next roof. Want me to put one or two in front of you? Same building, smaller bill."

**To solar companies (supply), capacity handraiser:**
> "Hey Peter, I have warehouses, stores and office buildings in California with big power bills. Before I send anyone your way, do you have room for one more job?"

Sending setup: Monday–Saturday, 7:00 am – 7:30 pm. 100 per side, California.

**What each question tests:**

| Email | Question | What it tests | What it qualifies |
|---|---|---|---|
| Building | "Want me to put one or two in front of you?" | Interest in solar right now | Nothing yet; it's a yes or no |
| Installer | "Do you have room for one more job?" | Capacity | Whether they can take a large job |

He explains the CTA himself: "The CTA here also has an offer … the prospect doesn't have to think. It's either yes or no."

>
> Direct introduction: I'll personally introduce you both by email with the full context of the opportunity, their energy requirements and why I believe your team is a good fit. You won't be receiving a generic lead or a contact list. After the introduction you'll be in direct contact with the building's decision maker. Your team can take it from there: discuss the facility, assess feasibility and work through the engineering and interconnection requirements.
>
> Our guarantee: if the opportunity materially differs from what I've described, I'll replace the introduction with another qualified opportunity at no additional cost.
>
> [payment link] … I'll make the introduction within the next 24 hours with everything your team needs to pick up the conversation."

Sent from his own Gmail: "At this point it's like a commercial relationship. It has to leave the cold email inbox."

**The five uncertainty questions, answered:**

| Question | Where in the email |
|---|---|
| Who are you? | "This is Saad. I reached out earlier about…" |
| Can you deliver? | The building's stated needs matched to *their own* projects |
| What am I paying for? | "Direct introduction … not a generic lead or a contact list" |
| What happens after I pay? | Intro within 24 hours, then direct contact with the decision maker |
| What if it doesn't work? | "If the opportunity materially differs … I'll replace the introduction" |

## 6. Decision analysis

| Moment | What he did | Why (his words, or **inference** where marked) | Alternatives he didn't take |
|---|---|---|---|
| First email to buildings | Kept it vague ("buildings like yours") | "I'd rather be vaguely right than precisely wrong." He didn't know the building type | A personalized opener per building (he says no personalization tricks are needed) |
| Supplier "flexed" with case studies | Treated it as buying intent and moved toward the invoice | The fee-advance offer showed intent | Asking more qualifying questions |
| Buyer called him "vague" | Didn't defend himself. He did research to match the buyer's three stated requirements | **Inference:** the skeptic gave him a requirements list; meeting it exactly was the strongest reply | Arguing, or sending a generic solar pitch |
| Before charging | Verified fit from the supplier's own website and the project address | He wouldn't send an intro he couldn't justify ("locked myself in a room") | Asking the supplier to prove it, which adds a round trip |
| The close | Own Gmail, fee stated, deliverable listed, replacement clause, **no call, no proof** | "A call is just a tool to reduce uncertainty"; he answered those questions in writing | Booking a call, or attaching case studies |
| The free side | "Fair point, I appreciate you being specific," plus the evidence, plus "no cost on your side" | **Inference:** concede the fair criticism and keep the skeptic engaged until payment arrives | Revealing the supplier's name before payment (he only described them) |

## 8. Rules from this case

**Transferable:**

- Supplier capacity check as the whole first email: "Before I send anyone your way, do you have room for one more job?"
- A skeptical buyer reply that lists requirements is a gift. Match each requirement with public evidence.
- Use the supplier's own published work as proof, not testimonials.
- Close from your own inbox in a new thread. State the fee, the deliverable and the replacement clause.
- The intro carries both sides' words and numbers.

**Specific to this market:** energy benchmarking data exists only in some cities. LADWP and cold storage are local, technical requirements.

**Do not copy:** nothing problematic. The "I have solar companies…" line was literally true (100 installers were being contacted the same day), but it reads as an existing relationship. Under our honesty levels, prefer "I work with" only once a supplier has engaged.
