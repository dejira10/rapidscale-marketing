## 1. What he knew before sending

| Item | Detail |

| Data | Medicare **payroll-based journal (PBJ)** records. Each nursing home reports hours worked by its own staff vs. contract (agency) staff, e.g. "paid nurse agencies for 23,000 hours, January–March 2026." This is "the proof that we're not guessing" |

## 2. The outreach (exact)

**To nursing homes (demand). Subject: "Nurses available in Maryland":**
> "Hey Alejandro, reaching out because I independently connect nursing homes with nurse staffing agencies. I've got an agency in Maryland with nurses and aides available to cover shifts. Are staffing gaps currently forcing you to use overtime or limiting the residents you can take in?"

**To agencies (supply):**
> "Hi [name], reaching out because I independently connect nurse staffing agencies with nursing homes. I don't represent them. I've got a nursing home in Maryland that's already paying agencies to cover nursing and aide shifts. Would you be interested in taking on another facility?"

**Why the wording:**

- **"Facility"** is the industry's own word.
- **The demand question is tied to money:** overtime is paid at 1.5×, and empty beds mean zero revenue.
- **No compliments or flattery.** He says no "how cute their dog is."

## 4. The close email (exact)

> "Dante, Saad here, taking this over to my proper email. I run [business]. Basically, I find businesses that need customers and put them directly in front of businesses that want to sell to them.
>
> I have a nursing home in Laurel, Maryland that looks unusually close to what you already provide. I spoke directly with their facility contact, and she told me they're still using quite a bit of agency staffing. They've already increased their rates, introduced referral bonuses, and even same-day pay, but they're still short on **RNs, LPNs and GNAs**. She specifically asked where the staffing agency I mentioned is based, which is actually pretty funny, because you're literally in Laurel too.
>
> I also checked your company's staffing services, and you already provide nursing staff to long-term facilities. And you mentioned being able to start supplying staff within 48 to 72 hours after signing, which makes this a pretty obvious fit.
>
> And to answer your question about my fees: it's $8,000 flat for the qualified nursing home introduction. Here's the invoice. Here's exactly what you're getting: I'll introduce you directly to the facility contact on the same email thread, explain the staffing needs they've already shared with me, and let you both discuss shifts, staffing rates and contracts, and getting your people into the facility. Once payment is taken care of, I'll send the introduction immediately. That's my entire delivery. No meetings or calls needed with me. If the nursing home materially doesn't match what I've described, I'll replace the introduction."

Stripe product: **"Qualified Maryland Nursing Home Introduction."** The description: "One direct email introduction to a nursing home in Laurel, Maryland currently using outside staffing agencies and actively seeking additional [roles]; includes the facility contact, the staffing needs, direct email handoff…"

## 5. Decision analysis

| Moment | What he did | Why (his words / **inference**) | Not done |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Send your fees and facility name" | Sent the fee and the invoice; **withheld the name** | "Even if he gets back to us saying, well, can you give me the facility name first? No, that's not how it works … you can just talk to each other" | Revealing the facility |
| Who pays | Only the agency | "Although we can charge her, it's going to probably stall the deal" | Charging the nursing home |
| Bolded roles in the close | Highlighted RNs, LPNs, GNAs | "That way he can skim through it. His eye will laser focus on these roles" | A paragraph of prose |
| "That's my entire delivery. No meetings or calls" | Stated the scope boundary explicitly | "This is me establishing my authority" | An open-ended service promise |
| After sending the invoice | No follow-up | "We're not going to keep following up … the only thing we have to do right now is just wait" | Chasing |

## 6. Frankie comparison

- **The nursing home's reply** ("we've bumped our rates, referral bonuses, same-day pay…") is exactly what Frankie's "What else have you tried?" digs for. Here it arrived unprompted, because the first email's question named the symptom (overtime, limited residents).
- **The bolded roles** match Frankie's "street level" specificity.

## 7. Rules from this case

**Transferable:**

- The best public data proves the buyer **already spends** on the exact service (PBJ contract hours).
- Tie the demand-side question to money (overtime, empty beds).
- When a supplier asks "fees and name," send the fee and keep the name.
- Bold the buyer's specific needs in the close so they can be skimmed.
- Scope boundary: "That's my entire delivery. No meetings or calls needed with me."

**Specific to this market:** PBJ data, healthcare staffing roles. Referral fees in healthcare can be regulated; check before using.

**Do not copy:** "I've got an agency in Maryland with nurses and aides available" and "I've got a nursing home in Maryland that's already paying agencies" were sent at level 2. The nursing-home claim is close to true from PBJ data (it describes a category, not one relationship). The agency claim implied a relationship he didn't yet have. Use "Maryland nursing homes are paying agencies thousands of hours a quarter…" until an agency has engaged.
