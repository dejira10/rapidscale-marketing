## 1. What he knew before sending

- **Why this market:**
  - Studios already spend to hunt for clients, and they bill their own clients upfront (about 75% on award), so they don't resist paying upfront.
  - Brands already have budget set aside; the only question is who produces the content.
  - He calls it one of the best markets for a first customer.
- **Data:**
  - **Studios:** AICP (production association) directories and UK production trade shows.
  - **Brands:** USPTO trademark filings for new product or brand names (about 2,000 a week, with founder emails). Reran weekly.
- **Tool:** his ConnectOS product, which pulls the data, matches it, and writes the emails. He removed the "white-label case studies" his tool offers.
- **His prior campaign data:** step 1 produced most opportunities (12 in one campaign, 19 in another). That gave him a **hypothesis:** "If it's a strong market, most of the opportunities are going to come from the initial message … a medium market, most of the replies come from follow-ups."
- **Honesty level at send:** level 2.

## 2. The outreach (exact)

**To brands (demand):**
> "Hey Jessica, I have vetted commercial production teams with live capacity right now. Would love to send a fit your way."

**To studios (supply):**
> "Hey Bryce, I have brands with active commercial campaigns looking for production support right now. Before I route anyone anywhere, I just wanted to understand your capacity and whether the fit is there. Worth a quick call?"

"No social proof. No case studies. … He who has the thing names it plainly and the market pays him for it."

## 4. The close and the warm-up (exact as read)

**To the studio:**
> "Bryce, Saad here from [business]. I route live commercial opportunities to a small group of production teams. Taking this over from the other thread so I can send you the opportunity properly. Thanks for sending the recent work over. I have a Canadian outdoor brand launching a new footwear collection next week, and it looks very close to the case study you sent me. They're looking for footage of the product being used outside, trail shots, close-ups of the product, shorter videos for ads and social. The person handling it leads marketing and brand strategy directly, and she told me they're ready to look at scope and budget right away. Your [case study] work is why I came to you first. The way I'm structuring this is $5,000 for the introduction. **I do have a few other production teams I can route this to, so if you don't want it, that's fine.** If you want the opportunity, take care of the invoice and I'll introduce you both directly by email. From there you can discuss the shoot, location, budget, dates and whatever else you need."

On the scarcity line, he says on camera: "I'm not lying. I do have other commercial production studios. I'm kind of like adding some urgency here."

**To the brand (the "Trojan horse"):**
> "Jessica, taking this over from the other thread so I can handle it properly. … [I have a Canadian production team that looks like a strong fit … I'll introduce you both directly today at no cost.] **If this turns into a successful project, all I ask in return is a quick 30-second video afterward about how the introduction worked for you. Nothing produced, just you talking to the camera.**"

His reasoning: "She pays nothing. But I get something that will get me more Jessicas … I get video proof, which is going to be an easier $5K sale."

## 5. After the intro: the management rules he set out here

- Set a ~24-hour reminder. If nothing's been heard, send one human message at about 7 am their time: "Hey X, hope you're well. How did the conversation with Y go?"
- Classify the outcome: did they speak, did they like each other, are they discussing work? If yes, leave them alone and check back in a few days.
- Track lightly (he uses Linear): who was introduced and when, did they speak, did it become a project, its approximate value, whether it's usable as a case study. "You don't need the CRM if you don't have your first three or five customers."
- Repeat sale: "Hey, got another one that looks like your kind of thing. Want me to send it over?"

## 6. Decision analysis

## 8. Rules from this case

**Transferable:**

- Market-strength check: count positive replies by sequence step. Step 1 heavy suggests a strong market.
- Choose the supplier whose own published work matches the buyer's brief, and tell them that's why.
- Scarcity only when literally true.
- The free side "pays" with a 30-second video.
- After the intro: a 24-hour check-in, light tracking, and the repeat-sale line.

**Specific to this market:** production timelines, studios billing upfront.

**Do not copy:**

- "I have vetted commercial production teams with live capacity" and "I have brands with active commercial campaigns" were sent at level 2. "Vetted" in particular is an unearned claim at send time.
- His tool's "white-label case studies" (other people's results).
