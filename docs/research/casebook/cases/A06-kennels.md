On tone, he says: "Now we might look awkward, but this is how real people actually speak … now we live in a day and age where AI practically writes everyone's messages."

## 4. The close and the warm-up (exact as read)

**To the supplier. Subject: "Georgia kennel opportunity":**
> "Carissa, Saad here. I run [business]. In very simple terms, I find businesses that need customers and put them in front of businesses that want to sell to them. [I know] absolutely nothing operationally [about your products], by the way. Anyway, I have a Georgia pet resort that looks unusually close to what you've described. They already sell gelato on site, already have freezer space, and Ray, the owner, specifically told me that she's interested in frozen treats or something else they can keep in the freezer, because these smaller add-ons already move pretty well for them. This seems like a pretty obvious fit with what you mentioned around freezer placements. The way I'm structuring this is $4,000 for the customer introduction. Once payment is taken care of, I'll introduce you directly … If I introduce you and the business clearly does not fit what I described, I replace the introduction."

He named a first name ("Ray") but not the company: "That does not mean I'm mentioning the company. Very different."

**To the kennel. Subject: "Frozen treats":**
> "Ray, Saad here. I run [business]. Basically, I spend an unreasonable amount of time finding businesses [that should already be buying from each other] … [I found a Georgia maker] with frozen dog products, and they're currently looking at placing them with kennels and pet businesses. I specifically mentioned that you already sell gelato, have freezer space, and would prefer something frozen that works as one of those small extras customers can have. [I'm] confirming inventory and fit with them now before I put you together. There's no fee or obligation on your side. My commercial arrangement is with the company I introduce. **The only favor I ask: if the introduction works well for you, would you be able to send me a quick 30-second phone video afterwards saying what you were looking for and how the introduction went? Nothing polished at all.** If everything lines up, I'll connect you both directly over email, and you can take it from there."

## 6. Frankie comparison

- **"Asking for the dogs"** is humor in a cold message, which is within Frankie's rules (light, natural).
- **The free-side video ask** is Frankie's "build something for a respected client in exchange for the case study," applied to the side that isn't paying.

## 7. Rules from this case

**Transferable:**

- A playful, human call to action can lift replies in friendly industries.
- "Is this paid / how does it work?" gets a direct answer: it's a buying signal.
- Never send the other party's company name before payment. A first name is fine.
- The free side "pays" with a 30-second video if the intro works.
- "I spend an unreasonable amount of time finding businesses that should already be buying from each other" is a usable, truthful identity line.

**Specific to this market:** freezer add-ons, small-batch producers.

**Do not copy:**

- "I have kennels in Georgia that feed dogs every day…" was sent at level 2; it's true only as a category. Under our rules, phrase it as market information until a kennel has engaged.
- "No call needed" in a first email can trigger suspicion (see A02).
