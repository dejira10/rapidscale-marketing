## 1. What he knew before sending

**His penalty-based market formula** ("every major industry on Earth has these penalty-based little sub-niches"):

1. **Scrapable public shame:** the government publishes the company, the violation, the date and the penalty.
2. **Clock pressure:** hard deadlines to respond.
3. **Can't fix it alone:** an outside specialist is needed.
4. **The specialists are bad at getting clients.**

**Data:**

- **Demand:** EPA enforcement records (ECHO/RCRA), about 4,000 public cases.
- **Supply:** remediation firms via Instantly SuperSearch.
- Claude also filtered out Apollo matches with the wrong geography or company: "the email can be real and still be the wrong human."

**Honesty level at send:** level 2.

## 2. The outreach (exact)

**To remediation firms (supply). Subject: "EPA remediation":**
> "Hey Blake, I have companies that just received EPA enforcement actions actively looking for environmental remediation support right now. Before I route anyone anywhere, I wanted to understand your capacity and whether the fit is there. Worth a quick call?"

**Follow-up 1:**
> "Hey Blake, do you have capacity for new engagements right now?"

**Follow-up 2:**
> "Quick check: two more EPA-driven situations came in this week before I keep routing. I didn't want to skip you if remediation work is in your active lane right now."

It's a 4-step sequence. The demand side got a mirrored version ("I have [specialists] for companies facing regulatory actions…").

## 4. The call (exact clips)

**His opener:**
> "Appreciate you hopping on. So this is a bit of a cold reach, even though you booked it. Honestly, I wasn't sure how much context came through in my notes. I'm not a remediation operator myself. I don't run cleanups or manage waste disposal, but I do sit close to a specific category of demand: companies moving through EPA enforcement. … So is your team typically looking to get in front of more of the enforcement-driven situations, or is that already a strong part of your existing pipeline?"

**What he learned (her answers):**

- They do hazardous waste, recycling and sustainability nationwide.
- On upfront payment: projects over a certain size must pay upfront, "just so we won't have to pursue them for payment."
- "We want manufacturing companies who are going to have **recurring waste or recycling**."

**His repositioning (exact):**
> "Got it. So recurring makes sense. … The enforcement event is the door opener, but the real value for you is when that company also has ongoing waste: recycling, maybe disposal, maybe compliance needs after the urgent problem gets handled. So I wouldn't position this to you as just one-off cleanup chasing. The better lane is enforcement-driven accounts where there's immediate pressure and a reason to engage now, but also enough operational activity behind the company that it can turn into an MSA, maybe a recurring waste stream. Is that something you'd want to explore?"

**His helper tool:** he used his own "Connector Copilot" to draft the opener and suggest lines live. It's his product.

## 7. Rules from this case

**Transferable:**

- The penalty-based market formula: public shame, deadline, specialist needed, specialists bad at sales.
- A detailed question list from a prospect means serious interest. Answer at a high level and offer one specific time.
- When the supplier asks for a call and the deal is ongoing, take it. Open honestly about what you are and aren't.
- Use the call to learn the supplier's real economics, then re-aim the lane around them.

**Specific to this market:** EPA ECHO/RCRA data, waste streams, MSAs.

**Do not copy:**

- "I have companies that just received EPA enforcement actions actively looking for … support" was sent at level 2. They had enforcement actions, but "actively looking" wasn't confirmed.
- "Two more EPA-driven situations came in this week": use only if literally true.
