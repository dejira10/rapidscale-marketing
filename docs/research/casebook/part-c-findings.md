## 2. When he skipped the call, and when he took one

**Closed without a call:** A01–A12 (12 deals). Shared conditions:

1. **One-off introduction** (or, in A11, a first account opening an engagement), priced **$3,000–12,000**.
2. **Both sides confirmed in writing** before the close: the buyer stated a need, the supplier stated capacity [A01–A12].
3. **The buyer was pre-sold or used to paying intermediaries** ("we usually go through brokers," "budget isn't an issue," "whatever finder's fee") [A04, A05, A06, A08].
4. **Fit verified by Saad from public evidence,** not by asking [A01, A02, A08].
5. **One close email answered all five uncertainty questions** [all].

**Took or routed to a call:** A13–A20. Triggers:

- **The prospect asked for one** [A13, A14, A15].
- **The buyer was rule-bound or multi-location** (franchise approved suppliers, five locations) [A16].
- **The details weren't ready** (menus, pricing, which supplier) [A18, A19, A20].
- **The deal was a retainer or engagement,** or high-ticket and new [A13, A14].

His stated rule: "Let the deal tell you whether you need to hop on a call" [A02/S06].

## 5. First emails: what they share

**Shared structure** (every connector case):

1. **What's on offer:** "I have [the other side] actively [doing X] right now," or a dated public fact about them [A05, A09, A15, A18].
2. **(Optional)** a reason for writing now: a market stat [A03], a deadline [A05, A15], or a pain question [A07, A09].
3. **A small question:** a capacity check (supply) or yes/no / still live (demand).

**Handraiser types he used:**

| Type | Saad example | Cases |
|---|---|---|
| **Capacity** (supply) | "Before I send anyone your way, do you have room for one more job?" | A01, A08, A10, A12, A13, A14 |
| **Discovery** (demand, timing) | "Is the request still open?" / "Have you sorted out who's making it yet?" / "Who handles your turnover right now?" | A05, A09, A17 |
| **Opportunity** (demand, yes/no) | "Want me to put one or two in front of you?" | A01, A02, A06 |

**Ask size:** most first emails ask permission or yes/no. Where he asked "Worth a quick call?" in a first email [A10, A13, A14], it was on the supply side. His own best breakdown [A15] says ask permission to send details, not for a call. Frankie and U1 agree.

## 6. Honesty audit

| Issue | Where | Count |
|---|---|---|
| "I have [the other side]…" in the first email **before anyone on that side had engaged** (level 2 written as level 3) | A01–A20, almost every campaign | Nearly universal |
| An overstated identity or experience **in a close or reply** | A05 ("I spend a lot of time around K-12"), A08 ("basically what I do is…" on day one), A15 ("Yes, I have worked with cannabis before") | 3, two of them admitted on camera |
| Fabricated proof | A21.3 ("I know someone who helped a new {industry} CMO…"); Part B (D6 "Kelly" bot, invented excuse) | 2 |
| Unconfirmed claims in follow-ups ("two more came in this week") | A13 | 1 (unverified) |
| Letting a wrong assumption stand ("I'm assuming you've got several active accounts") | A11 | 1 |
| Manufactured urgency | A04 (subject "in the next 24 hours, just to add some urgency") | 1 |
| A role-play presented as a real close | A21.6 / S23 | 1 |

**The core finding:** Saad's method works **without** these. In every paid case, what closed the deal was:

- the buyer's real stated need;
- the supplier's real stated capacity;
- the evidence he found;
- a clear fee.

The overclaims sat in the first emails and identity lines, and can be replaced with truthful versions **without changing the mechanism**:

| Instead of… | Write… |
|---|---|
| "I have X actively looking…" (before engagement) | "[Public fact about the market]…" + "Are you taking on…?" (capacity) / "Is this still open?" (discovery) |
| "I spend a lot of time around [industry]" | "I track [signal] in [market] and connect [A] with [B] when the timing's right" |
| "Yes, I've worked in X" | "Not X specifically. What I track is [situation], and this one looked live" |
| "Two more came in this week" (unverified) | A real new signal, or a plain capacity check |

## 9. What this means for the next design documents

**For the opportunity brief** (inputs needed before writing), the cases show the system needs:

- the market, with demand and supply named;
- the public data source and the specific dated fact;
- the **honesty level** for each side (1–4) and **qualification status** for each side (need confirmed? requirements? authority? willing to be introduced?);
- the industry's own words (from websites and replies);
- the requirements each side has stated, verbatim;
- the evidence of fit (with links);
- who pays, for what service, and the price logic (the value of one customer);
- names held: what can and can't be revealed yet.

**For the outreach standard:**

- the three handraiser types (capacity, discovery, opportunity) with the honest wording table from §6;
- one small question; no call request in first emails (with an exception for supply-side capacity checks, if you choose);
- follow-ups: capacity check → real new signal → door open (Saad), or one plan step each (Frankie);
- how to diagnose failure: the market, then targeting, then offer, then copy.

**For the reply framework:** the reply kinds in §4, with each kind's next move from §3, plus the no-call close template and the conditions for taking a call (§2).

**Still unknown:**

- reply and close rates (selection bias);
- whether the introductions turned into business;
- how Saad answered several trust questions off-screen (A08's "how did you get our data?");
- the 4,600-word field guide mentioned in U1 (not provided).
