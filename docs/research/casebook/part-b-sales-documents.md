## 2. Frankie (D1, D2)

**D1: the pre-sell formula (exact):**
> "Problem → Outcome → Mechanism → Soft Invite. 'If you've got inconsistent months and rollercoaster income, and you want to get to five to ten cases a month, the easiest way to do that is with Facebook ads. I put together a little [video/PDF/podcast] that walks you through exactly how it works. Want me to send it over?'"

- **The report handraiser:** "Instead of saying, 'Hey, do you want to jump on a call?', you can say, 'Hey, I created a simple little PDF report that shows how to get five to ten cases a month … Are you interested in getting the PDF report?' That's way more seduction than chase."
- **"Most people don't realize that the money is in the implementation, not in the idea."** Give the idea away; get paid for doing it.
- **"Can you just do it for me?"** Explain the steps, and buyers ask you to do them.
- **Repositioning:** "We'll do some SEO" becomes "We'll get you five to ten cases a month using SEO."
- **The 10/10 call:** "I don't even know what you sell, but I came here to buy it." This is what good pre-selling produces.

**D2: what the Loom workshop promises to build, and the proof images:**

- **The workshop builds:** "Your irresistible offer, a complete 5-minute Loom video, a clear demonstration that sells your service instantly, your exact handraiser message, your first campaign launched."
- **What the images show:**
  - Loom notification emails: "Your video has been viewed: **Idea For [company]**."
  - Prospect replies, e.g. one to an email titled **"Here's The Video We Discussed"**: "Sounds good, let's do it!"
  - Student posts ("Second sale via SMS to the same client … finalised the details in 3 messages"; "Celebrating my first sale over SMS, 4 months contract, and I haven't seen the face of a client").
  - Rows of payment-received emails.
- **Taking from it:**
  - The Loom title **"Idea For [company]"**.
  - The follow-up email subject **"Here's the video we discussed"**.
- **Leaving out:** income claims ("Ryan closed 1,000 clients in 2025"), profanity, the scarcity pitch.

## 3. Graydon Ursel / Forever Booked (D3, D4, D5): the long-form selling doc

**D3's structure, section by section, and the buyer question each one answers:**

| # | Section | What it does | Buyer question answered |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title for a specific audience ("For Med Spas, Solo Injectors & Aesthetic Pros") | Says who it's for | Is this for me? |
| 2 | Author and credentials | Who's talking | Who are you? |
| 3 | **Track record first:** named client results, before and after, by market | Proof before any argument | Does it work for people like me? |
| 4 | Pain in the reader's words ("the whitespace on your calendar is freaking you out…") | Says their situation better than they could | Do you understand me? |
| 5 | "It's not your fault" | Removes shame and defensiveness | |
| 6 | **The free plan:** 4 steps given away in full (offer, reactivating the database, ads, AI follow-up) | Shows competence; gives the idea away, sells the doing | How does it work? |
| 7 | Three options: hire an agency / do it yourself / us, with costs | Frames the price against the alternatives | Why you, and why this price? |
| 8 | What you get, as a value stack with a value per item | Concrete deliverables | What am I paying for? |
| 9 | Price ("low 4-figure start, then $497/month"), terms ("cancel anytime") | Clear price | How much? |
| 10 | Guarantee (full refund within 4 weeks) | Removes risk | What if it doesn't work? |
| 11 | Delivery roadmap: week by week | What happens after paying | What happens after I pay? |
| 12 | FAQ | Answers the remaining objections | Leftover doubts |
| 13 | Call to action (book a demo) | One next step | |

**The part we can use directly: the two-text reactivation pattern (exact):**

- **Text 1 (handraiser):** "Hi [first name], it's [your name] with [clinic name]. We've had a lot of clients wanting to fix under-eye circles and bags, so we created a new program to do exactly this. Interested in details?"
- **The rule:** "You want to avoid vomiting all your offer details in one initial text."
- **Text 2 (details), only after a yes:** the steps, the bonus, "packages start at $X," "no obligation," then "Does this sound like something that might interest you?"

**D4: offer design:**

- **"Sell cake, not ingredients":** "25% OFF a package of 3 microneedling treatments" becomes "Sign up for our new 12-Week 'Ageless Skin' Bootcamp."
- **The offer graphic,** with four numbered parts: a problem-based headline with name and price ("Get Our Under Eye Rescue Package For Just $97! (Reg $400)") → a problem-based analysis → a trial treatment → a bonus.
- **The "what matters" pyramid:** the quality of the offer is the base.

**Flags:**

- **Unverifiable result claims:** "$2,294,029 in sales," "19X ROI."
- **Pressure:** "WARNING: only 10 per month," "price increasing within the next month."
- **This doc closes on a demo call, not without one.**
- **Regulated advertising** (D5's semaglutide ad rules).

## 4. Dan Wardrop / Flexxable (D6, D7)

**D6, the outreach line at the top (exact):**
> "Hey ___, Happy New Year. I'm working with a few roofing companies on a backend deal. This means I only get paid on performance. Lemme know if you have capacity for more work."

It's essentially Saad's supply-side capacity check, from another marketer. It's usable only if every claim is true (that you're "working with a few roofing companies," and that you're "paid on performance").

**D6's case-study structure:**

| # | Section | Answers |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A story hook (an ex-roofer who couldn't answer leads while on roofs) | Do you understand my world? |
| 2 | A headline question ("Can your best salesperson produce $600k+ profit in 90 days?") | Why should I read this? |
| 3 | A case study with numbers: 319 "dead" leads → 47 responded → 21 booked → about $600k over 90 days (claimed) | Does it work? |
| 4 | The owner's reaction ("Do I need you? Or you?") plus the staff-cost math | What's it worth to me? |
| 5 | **"How does it work?"** shown through the actual conversation (images) | How does it work? |
| 6 | More industries (a law firm, home improvement) | Will it work for me? |
| 7 | Scarcity ("1 of 5") plus a reply-to-email call to action, plus a P.S. video link | Next step |
| 8 | **Template notes:** "Make a copy … change the bits in yellow … keep the case study video link in your P.S." | It's an agency template |

**The conversation in the images** (debt consolidation, UK):

1. **Bot:** "Hi it's Kelly from [company]. Is that the same Tiffany who wanted help with their debt a couple of days ago?"
2. **Lead:** "Hi, yes it is."
3. **Bot:** "Thank goodness, my calendar just pinged me to call, but I didn't want to disturb you. Are you still looking for help?"
4. **Qualification:** "How much your biggest debt is?" → "about £5000" → "What have you tried to do so far to reduce your debt?"
5. **Booking:** "Would you be free to schedule a call … now?" → "Working till 11pm" → "What time suits you tomorrow?" → "5pm"
6. **Commitment:** "I have booked you in with Ian … he is very busy … I don't want you to lose the chance to chat with him, he is brilliant … Can I count on you?" → "Yes" → "Thank you."

Other images: a pipeline board ("70% responded / 48% qualified / 42% call booked"), booking dashboards, and a trial: 96 leads → 38 responded → 17 positive → 11 qualified → 8 calls → 5 sold.

**Structure worth learning:**

- a first message that triggers recognition;
- confirming identity;
- qualification questions ("what have you tried?", which is also Frankie's question);
- a fallback from booking now to booking later;
- commitment tied to a named person.

**Honesty flags (strong):**

- **The bot pretends to be a human** ("Kelly").
- **It uses a made-up excuse** ("my calendar just pinged me to call").
- **The "1 of 5" scarcity** may be invented.

Never copy these. Any AI follow-up we build must not present itself as a person, and must not invent reasons for writing.

**D7:** a "pay what you want after you watch" offer for a training, with student result screenshots. Low relevance; heavy hype.

## 5. James Kemp (D8, D9)

**D8: the offer written in the buyer's voice:**

- **It opens as if the buyer wrote it:** "The Syndicate™. James! I want your support in getting my offers pumping out cash without me having to crank the handle myself. Our goal is building a $100k per month (profit) … business…"
- **Then "You understand…" statements:** what they'll get, phases, office hours, investment ($400 now, then $400 a week, "Pay As You Profit"), "Love It Or Leave It" (leave any time, can't rejoin for 12 months), "No guarantees or Big Promises."
- **Close:** "PM me, 'I'm In!'" A reply is the purchase, with no call.
- **What to learn:** writing the agreement from the buyer's side, and a one-phrase reply to buy.
- **Flags:** inflated "value" anchors ("$24,000 value," "$5,000 an hour rate").

**D9: a 10-email launch sequence to an existing list:**

| Email | Job |
|---|---|
| 0 Welcome | Thanks; positioning; **"hit reply and tell me your #1 roadblock"** (reply-based research) |
| 1 Doors open soon | Announce, plus "reply 'skip' to get in before the rush" |
| 2–4 Case studies | Three stories (retainer to program; a single mum's $7,500 launch; an 8-step breakdown) |
| 5 Optimal behaviour | "Speed wins": decide fast |
| 6 Open the doors | Full offer: what, who, how, bonuses, $100 then 14 × $100 weekly, guarantee ("work with you until you've made at least $10k"), **"reply 'I'M IN!'"** |
| 7 Questions | "Just following up to see if you have any questions?" (Frankie would object to this) |
| 8 Proof | A results page |
| 9 Close the doors | Deadline; "if you're not in, I'd love to know why" (Frankie's honest-feedback ask) |

His rule: "Automate when your offer doc converts at 30%+."

**For us:** this only applies once there's a list. The parts that carry over to cold work are reply-to-buy ("I'M IN") and asking non-buyers why.

## 7. What carries over to the connector model

| Element | From | Use for us |
|---|---|---|
| Handraiser → details only after a yes | D3 (two texts), D1 (report invite), Saad S41 | Our first-message rule |
| Capacity-check outreach line | D6 (Dan), Saad S05/S12/S33 | Supply-side handraiser (honest version) |
| A buyer-questions map for any closing doc | D3, D6 | The **close doc** for retainers and engagements must answer: who's it for, who are you, proof, how it works, what you get, price, risk, what happens next |
| Giving away the idea, selling the doing | D1, D3 | For the connector: share the market insight freely, charge for the introduction or access |
| Loom "Idea For [company]" plus the "video we discussed" subject | D2 | If we close retainers with video |
| Reply-to-buy ("I'm in") | D8, D9 | An alternative to an invoice link, for warm prospects |
| Ask non-buyers why | D9, Frankie's brutal-feedback script | Post-loss learning |
| Qualification inside text conversations ("what have you tried?") | D6 | Reply handling |

For one-off introductions, **Saad's close email plus thesis brief** remains the format; these documents are long because they sell services. For **retainers and 60–90 day engagements**, a short version of D3's structure is the template to adapt.

## 8. Never carry over

- **AI or bots presenting as a human,** or made-up excuses for writing (D6).
- **Unverifiable results** used as proof (D2, D3, D6, D7, D8, D9).
- **Manufactured scarcity or urgency** (D3, D6, D9, D10).
- **Inflated "value" anchors** (D3, D8).
- **Profanity** (D2).
- **"Just following up to see if you have any questions?"** (D9, email 7).
