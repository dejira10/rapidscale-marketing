# Market Research: Residential HVAC Companies

**Target market:** owner-operated residential heating and cooling companies with 3–30 technicians, United States.

Most residential HVAC companies don't simply want more calls. They want more replacement and maintenance-agreement calls, spread across the year instead of crammed into two heat waves.

A company with a ringing phone full of $79 tune-ups and price shoppers can be busier and less profitable than one with fewer, better calls.

What they really want is a steady install board they don't have to pray the weather will fill.

*These are industry-informed hypotheses and representative language from residential HVAC companies, not direct interview quotations or verified survey findings.*

## 1. Problems Residential HVAC Companies Face

### A. Feast and famine seasons
**What this looks like:**
- "July I can't answer the phone fast enough. October my guys are sweeping the shop."
- "I'm paying full payroll for half a schedule."
- "One mild summer and my whole year is off."

Demand follows the weather, but payroll and truck payments don't.

### B. Low-value calls crowding out installs
**What this looks like:**
- "Half my leads want a $79 tune-up."
- "My techs are booked solid and we still didn't sell a system this week."
- "Everybody wants a free second opinion."

Tech time goes to calls that rarely turn into a replacement.

### C. Losing replacements on price
**What this looks like:**
- "They got three quotes and went with whoever was $800 less."
- "They don't care about SEER ratings, they care about the number at the bottom."
- "I spent two hours at the kitchen table for nothing."

It's hard to show why their system and install are worth more.

### D. Shared and junk leads
**What this looks like:**
- "I paid $60 for a lead and three other companies called them before I did."
- "Half the numbers don't pick up."
- "Some of them are renters who can't approve anything."

Paid leads feel like a race and a gamble.

### E. Hiring and keeping technicians
**What this looks like:**
- "I can't find good techs, and when I train one, someone poaches him."
- "If I can't keep them busy in the fall, they leave."
- "I'm turning work away in summer because I'm short-staffed."

Uneven demand makes it harder to keep a good team.

### Unqualified prospects that waste their time

| Unqualified prospect | What happens |
|---|---|
| The price shopper | Collects four quotes and picks the lowest |
| The renter | Wants a new system but the landlord decides |
| The coupon hunter | Books the $79 special and never buys anything else |
| The "just looking" homeowner | Wants a quote for a system they'll replace in three years |
| The DIY researcher | Uses the tech's diagnosis to buy parts online |

**Marketing insight:** sell steady replacement opportunities in the slow months, not more volume in the busy ones.

## 2. How Residential HVAC Companies Currently Get Customers

| Current process | How it works | Main limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Referrals and repeat customers | Past customers call back or tell neighbors | Unpredictable volume |
| Google search and Maps | "AC repair near me" when something breaks | Competitive and expensive in peak season |
| Shared-lead marketplaces | Paid leads sold to several companies | Price pressure and racing to call first |
| Manufacturer dealer programs | Leads and co-op money from the equipment brand | Tied to one brand's rules |
| Builders and property managers | Relationship-based install or service work | Thin margins, slow payment |
| Maintenance agreements | Members get tune-ups and priority service | Takes years to build |
| Direct mail and door hangers | Seasonal offers to nearby homes | Hard to measure |
| Truck wraps and yard signs | Local visibility near jobs | Awareness only |

Most rely on a few of these and the weather, not a repeatable system.

## 3. What Frustrates Them About Their Current Process (TIMER)

Using the TIMER framework (Time, Identity, Money, Energy, Reputation), here is how these frustrations show up.

**TIME:** "I'm on roofs all day and quoting at night."
Selling and follow-up eat the owner's evenings and weekends.

**IDENTITY:** "I'm an HVAC guy, not a marketer."
They feel out of their depth judging agencies and ad reports.

**MONEY:** "I paid $60 a lead and three other companies got the same one."
Spend feels disconnected from installs sold.

**ENERGY:** "Chasing people who never call back wears me out."
Follow-up drains the owner and the office.

**REPUTATION:** "One bad review from a price shopper hurts more than ten good jobs help."
The wrong customers put their name at risk.
