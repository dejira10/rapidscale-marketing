# 2. Outreach Standard

**Purpose:** how first messages and follow-ups are chosen, written, checked, sent and diagnosed. It's built from Saad's real threads (A01–A21), the shared principles in Frankie's handraiser method, and the user's guide U1.

**The guiding principle:** the first email isn't there to show how well we write. Its job is to find someone who already has a reason to continue the conversation `[Part C §5]`.

---

## 1. Choose the handraiser type from the evidence

| Situation (from the opportunity brief) | Type | What the message asks |
|---|---|---|
| Demand side at E1: a public trigger, need not confirmed | **Discovery** | "Is this still open / sorted / a problem?" |
| Supply side at E1: capability likely, capacity unknown | **Capacity** | "Are you taking on [specific work] right now?" |
| Other side at E2: engaged, not yet qualified | Discovery or capacity, with "I'm speaking with a [type] who [their words]" | The same questions, with stronger relevance |
| Other side at E3: qualified and permissioned | **Opportunity** | "Want me to put you in front of it?" |

One message can serve two purposes. A capacity question also qualifies. But **the evidence decides the claim, never the template** `[CG Part III]`.

**Order of contact.** Default to **both sides at once**: it's Saad's stated practice, and the first replies on one side make claims to the other side true faster `[A09, S40]`. Contact one side first when there's a reason to: demand is the uncertainty (go demand-first, like S32), or supply is scarce (go supply-first and keep it close, `[A12]`).

## 2. Anatomy of the first message

Use only the parts needed to earn a relevant reply. A three-line email that does the job beats a five-part template.

| Part | Purpose | Required? | Examples (exact source wording) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Why this company, why now** | One verifiable, commercially relevant fact | Yes, when a trigger exists | "Your September 15 request for internet access opens for signing on October 13" `[A05]`; "Saw [daycare] got its license on September 4th" `[A18]` |
| **The connection** | What you do, or the market fact, in one line, plainly | Yes | "I independently connect nursing homes with nurse staffing agencies" `[A07]` |
| **Relevance or stakes** (optional) | A market stat, or a pain in their words | Optional | "Honey prices went up 27% last year" `[A03]`; "no-shows on Sunday turnovers" `[A09]` |
| **One small question** | Capacity, discovery or opportunity; answerable with yes, no or a few words | Yes | "Are you taking on new import brands right now?" `[A20]`; "Is the request still open?" `[A05]` |

**Rules for the first message:**

1. **2–4 sentences, plain text,** no links, images or attachments `[A04, A18]`.
2. **A short, plain subject** in the industry's words: "Beans," "Lunch," "Manufacturing," "Short-stay work in New Orleans," "Your internet request from September 15."
3. **No compliments, no surface personalization.** The fact *is* the personalization `[A04, A07]`. Don't over-personalize: give the shortest credible reason for this recipient, this transaction and now `[CG Part III]`.
4. **Use the industry's own words:** "facility," "turnover," "crews," "accounts," "wholesale accounts" `[A07, A08, A09, A11]`.
5. **Room to decline** is required as a principle, not as a fixed sentence. Often the question does it ("do you have room for one more job?"). The strongest example:
   > "If you already have someone great, amazing, I'll leave you to it." `[A21.1]`
6. **Don't ask for a call in the first message.** Ask permission to send details, or a yes/no. "Strangers do not like being moved too fast" `[A15]`. Frankie agrees ("Can I share what I had in mind?"), and so does U1.
   - **Exception (user to decide):** Saad often ended supply-side capacity emails with "Worth a quick call?" `[A10, A13, A14]`.
7. **No "no call needed" line.** It attracted suspicion `[A02]`.
8. **Claims follow the claim-permission table** (brief §B2). Under our rules, no first email says "I have…" about a side that hasn't engaged.

### Writing for each side

| | Demand (buyer) | Supply (seller) |
|---|---|---|
| Motive | Solve a current problem or meet a deadline | Get profitable, fitting work |
| Hook | The trigger fact, plus the pain or stakes | The demand category, plus what that demand is doing |
| Question | "Is it still open?" / "Have you sorted out who's [doing X] yet?" / "Who handles [X] right now?" | "Are you taking on [specific work] in [area] right now?" |
| Room to decline | "If you already have someone great, I'll leave you to it" | "Before I send anyone your way…" (we're checking fit, not pushing) |

### Before and after: Saad's lines made honest

| Saad's original (state of the other side at the time) | Rewritten to the evidence |
|---|---|
| "I have solar companies that put panels on buildings like yours…" (E1) `[A01]` | "Solar installers in California are looking for large industrial roofs. Want me to put one or two in front of you? Same building, smaller bill." |
| "I have airports in Illinois that want more contractors bidding…" (E1) `[A02]` | "You bid on [airport] in [month]. Illinois airports are posting new runway and taxiway work. Want me to point you at the next one before it's awarded?" |
| "I have manufacturers actively looking to take on new beauty brands" (E1) `[A17]` | "You filed [brand] in the last 90 days. Have you sorted out who's making it yet?" |
| "I have companies that just received OSHA citations actively looking for…" (E1) `[A14]` | "I track companies recently cited by OSHA that will need outside safety remediation. Are you taking on new remediation engagements right now?" |
| "I've got a food company in California looking to buy honey in bulk" (E1) `[A03]` | "Bulk buyers paid $2.45 a pound on average last year. Would selling straight to a food manufacturer be worth it? I connect beekeepers with food companies, independently." |
| (after an E2 reply from the other side) | "I'm speaking with a [type] in [area] that [their words]. Do you have capacity for that?" `[A07]` |

## 3. Follow-ups

**Never** send "just checking in," "circling back," "just following up," or "did you see my email?" `[A21.3 agency era; Frankie]`.

Each follow-up adds something. Pick one approach per sequence:

| Approach | Use when | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Capacity check** (Saad) | Supply side, no reply | "Hey [first], do you have capacity for new engagements right now?" `[A13, A14]` |
| **A real new signal** (Saad) | There *is* a new, true development | "[N] new [licenses] were issued in [area] this week…" (only if true) |
| **A symptom question** (Saad / Frankie) | Demand side, no reply | "Are your regulars happy with the coffee, or are you still dialing in the beans?" `[A04]`; "Worth asking again: is turnover sorted, or still a headache?" `[A09]` |
| **Deeper on one step** (Frankie) | You've offered an idea or plan (services, retainers) | Isolate one concrete step, written as if already working together |
| **Door open** (Saad) | The final touch | "Leaving the door open: when you're ready to get it made, I'm one reply away" `[A17]`; "Seems like timing isn't right now, which is completely fine. I'll close this out on my end…" `[A15]` |

**Cadence:** 3–4 touches in total `[A13, A14, A17]`. Stop immediately on any no or opt-out.

## 4. Sending rules

| Rule | Detail | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Write by hand first | First 10 per side by hand, to learn the market's language and reactions; this isn't a statistical test | Frankie; user |
| Test size | About 100 per side (50–65 if high intent); one market or state at a time | `[A05, A07, A21.1]` |
| One change at a time | Order to check: **audience → trigger and offer → targeting (decision maker) → copy** | Frankie's order; `[Part C §7]` |
| Verified emails only | Never send to "risky" addresses; check the person and company match | `[A01, A13]` |
| Plain text, business hours | Monday–Saturday, 7:00 am – 7:30 pm recipient time | `[A01, A07]` |
| Commercial threads move to your own inbox | Closing never happens from the cold-sending inbox | `[A01, A04]` |
| Sender identity | A real person and business. If a sending inbox carries another name, say so honestly when asked ("that inbox handles initial outreach; I'm [name]") | `[A17]` |
| Compliance | CAN-SPAM: real sender, physical address, a working opt-out. No texts or automated calls without consent | README |

## 5. Pre-send checklist (each message)

- [ ] It names one commercially relevant, **verifiable** reason for this recipient now.
- [ ] Every claim fits the evidence state (brief §B2). No "I have…" without E3 on the other side.
- [ ] 2–4 sentences, plain text, in the industry's words.
- [ ] One small question; no call request (unless the user allows the supply-side exception).
- [ ] Room to decline, through the question or one line.
- [ ] No compliments, hype, invented urgency, scarcity or results.
- [ ] Barbecue test (Frankie): would you say it like this out loud to this person?
- [ ] An opt-out mechanism is present.

## 6. Diagnosing a campaign

Read the results before touching the copy `[Part C §7]`:

| Pattern | Likely cause | Fix first |
|---|---|---|
| Both sides silent | Wrong market or audience, or a weak trigger. "Diagnose in about an hour" from reply rates | Check the brief's selection lens and trigger, then targeting, then copy |
| One side silent | Wrong outbound on that side, a structurally slower side, or a lopsided market | `[S18]` asymmetric stocking: re-aim, give more time, or stop overstocking supply |
| Replies only on follow-ups | Possibly a medium-strength market (Saad's hypothesis) | Treat as a weaker market; test a sharper trigger |
| Fast replies from step 1 | A strong market signal | Move quickly to qualification |
| "What's your angle?" / "Is this lead gen?" | The frame is unclear | Fix the connection line (§2); see document 3 |
| "How did you get my email?" often | The trigger fact feels like scraping | Make the relevance clearer; answer honestly (document 3) |
| Lots of "not interested" | A wrong audience or offer | Change the audience or offer, not the wording |

**Metrics:** reply rate by side and step; qualified (E3) rate; close rate; money collected. "Always track only reply rates and actual money made" `[A21.4]`, with E3 added so pipeline isn't overstated.
