# 3. Reply Framework

**Purpose:** what a reply means, which move comes next, and how a conversation reaches a written close (usually without a call), a call, or a respectful stop. It's built from the decision patterns in Part C §3, the reply table in the ChatGPT casebook (Part III), and Frankie's conversation method.

**The principle:** answer what they're really asking, in their words, with one small next step. Most "objections" are questions from interested people `[A16]`.

---

## 1. Conversation stages

Each prospect card (brief §B) holds one stage per side:

```
S0 Sent ─► S1 Replied ─► S2 Qualifying ─► S3 Qualified (E3) ─► S4 Close sent ─► S5 Paid (E4) ─► S6 Intro delivered (E5) ─► S7 After-care
                │                │                                  │
                ├─► Not now ─► Door open (stop, or ask permission for later)
                ├─► No / opt-out ─► STOP immediately
                └─► Call requested ─► S2c Short call ─► back to S3 or S4
```

**Rules for moving between stages:**

- **S2 → S3** needs the qualification checklist (brief §B1) **and** permission to be introduced.
- **S3 → S4** needs the close-readiness gate (brief §B3), with **both** sides at E3.
- **S5 → S6:** the introduction is sent within 24 hours of payment `[A01, A10]`.

## 2. The reading method: translate before you write

For every reply, write down three things before drafting `[A19, S01]`:

1. **They said:** a quote.
2. **They're really asking:** the plain version. Saad's "watermelon version," as if two people were just selling watermelons.
3. **What this proves, and what it doesn't** (the table below).

Then draft the reply.

## 3. Reply types

| Type | Example (exact) | Proves | Doesn't prove | Next move | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Price or "is it paid?"** | "What do you charge?" / "Are you looking to get paid … how does that work?" | Strong commercial interest | Agreement or funds | **State the fee plainly now.** If both sides are at E3, send the close; if not, give the fee plus what's still being confirmed | `[A04, A06, A07, A08, A12]` |
| **Pre-sold** | "Whatever finder's fee or cuts you need…" / "We usually go through brokers…" | Used to paying intermediaries | Fit | Verify fit, then close | `[A05, A08]` |
| **Capacity yes** | "Yeah, we have room." / "Initial deployments within 3–5 days" | Supplier interest | Exact capability or availability | Qualify the capability against the demand's requirements; verify publicly | `[A01, A08, A21.1]` |
| **Need plus requirements** | "…won't do casual intros… if they've done massive cold storage builds, send them over" | A real need, with criteria | That any supplier qualifies | Verify each requirement; quote them back in the close | `[A01, A02, A05, A11, A12]` |
| **Information request** | "What types of gyms?" / "What kind of testing?" | They see a possible match | That they handle that exact work | A one-line answer in their words, then the next question or step | `[A16, A19]` |
| **Eager, vague** | "Please tell me more about these businesses" | Interest | Need specifics | "Absolutely. What are you looking to [do] right now?" Nothing more | `[A17]` |
| **Name or list request** | "Send the name" / "Send over the producers and rig locations" | Interest; wants to verify | Permission to share | **Don't name the other side before payment and permission.** Describe them (type, location, needs) | `[A02, A06, A07, A08, A16]` |
| **Trust: source** | "How did you get my email?" / "Are you with the city?" | Caution | Hostility | Name the public source lightly, then continue | `[A09]` |
| **Trust: "who are you?"** | "Who are you with?" | They can't place you | Rejection | Company, a one-line role, congratulations if relevant, then a question back | `[A17]` |
| **Credibility: "are you real?"** | "Have you worked in cannabis before?" | A credibility check | Disqualification | **The truth.** Then why this situation is live, and a question. Never claim experience you don't have | `[A15]` |
| **Skeptic: "what's your angle?"** | "Are you a broker… or selling data? Just tell me your fee" | An engaged skeptic (a good prospect) | That they'll pay | Answer the skepticism in the first line, plainly, then the fee | `[A02]` |
| **Category objection** | "Not interested in purchasing leads… are you lead gen?" | Category confusion, or a firm no-pay policy | Persuadable | Clarify **once**: agree with what's true, reposition using their word, ask about their fit | `[A14]` |
| **Second pushback** | "Not open to incurring costs to acquire clients" | Resistance | Final no | Pull away calmly, door open. Don't argue, discount or chase | `[A14]` |
| **Rule or constraint** | "Approved suppliers only" / "Depends on MSA status" | A buying restriction | That any supplier works | "I don't want to assume. Let me check." Verify publicly where possible | `[A16, A08]` |
| **Information gap** | "Your message is pretty vague" | Missing information | That it's dead | Learn their actual requirements; reply with specifics | `[A01]` |
| **"What if it doesn't work?"** | (deliverable vs outcome) | Risk concern | Unqualified | Separate what we control from what we don't, then the replacement promise | `[A21.6, S23]` |
| **Payment terms** | "Can we split it?" / "Pay on close?" | Cash-flow or risk concern | Overpriced | Change the structure (split, milestone, shorter priority window), **never the price**. For small suppliers: no success-only | `[A21.6, S18]` |
| **References or proposal** | "Send me references" / "Send a proposal for legal" | Due diligence | Stalling (assume good faith) | A written process summary, the delivery standard and the timeline; **respect legal review** | `[A21.6]` |
| **Non-decision-maker** | (licensing team replies) | Interest exists | Authority | "Who's the best person on your team for a quick conversation?" | `[A18]` |
| **Wrong lane** | "Can you also get us [unrelated]?" | Interest | Fit | Decline honestly, or offer the in-lane version | `[S18]` |
| **Not now** | "Not right now" / "Series B isn't on our table yet" | No current window | Permission for endless follow-up | Respect it; one door-open line; ask permission to check back later | `[A02, A21.4]` |
| **Call request** | "Why don't we set up a call…" | High interest | Agreement | Accept; ask when *they're* free, or offer two times | `[A13, A14, A15]` |
| **No / opt-out** | "Remove me" / "Not interested" | A clear stop | — | **Stop all outreach to them.** Note it on the card | Compliance |
| **No reply** | — | Nothing definite | — | Follow the outreach standard's follow-ups; then stop | Doc 2 §3 |

## 4. Reply construction

**Default formula** `[A18, S03]`:

1. **Mirror** their words.
2. **Hold back** names, lists, menus and anything not yet earned, *with a reason that serves them* ("so I only send you the ones that actually fit [their location]").
3. **One next step:** a question, two times, or the close.

**Exception:** price or "how does it work?" gets a straight answer `[A06]`.

**Length:** one to four sentences. "Long explanations are a mistake" `[A17]`.

**Tone:**

- Match theirs: formal gets formal `[A20]`.
- Calm. No pressure, no flattery, no "just checking in."

## 5. When to stay on email, and when to take a call

**Close by email** (Saad's 12 no-call deals) when all of these are true `[Part C §2]`:

- It's a one-off introduction or a defined engagement, at a price a stranger can pay upfront (in his cases, $3,000–12,000).
- Both sides are at E3 in writing.
- We verified the fit ourselves.
- All five buyer questions can be answered in the close email.

**Take a short call (10–15 minutes)** when:

- the prospect asks for one;
- they send a detailed list of questions (regions, scope, direct vs brokered) `[A13]`;
- the buyer is rule-bound or has several locations `[A16]`;
- it's a retainer, or high-ticket and new;
- they hesitate twice by email.

**On the call:**

- **Open honestly** about what we are and aren't `[A13]`: "I'm not a remediation operator myself, but I sit close to a specific category of demand…"
- **Spend about 80% of it asking questions.** Learn their real economics.
- **Re-aim the lane** if those economics point somewhere better `[A13]`.
- **Afterwards,** send the same written close email.

Frankie's alternative for services and retainers is a 5-minute Loom or a Google Doc answering result, will it work for me, how it works and price. Useful subject lines: "Idea For [company]," then "Here's the video we discussed" `[Part B §2]`.

## 6. The close (written)

**Send from your own inbox, in a new thread:** "Taking this over to my direct email so I can send this properly" `[A01, A07]`.

| Part | Answers | Model |
|---|---|---|
| Who we are, in one true line | Who are you? | "I find businesses that need [X] and introduce them directly to companies that can supply it" `[A07]` |
| (If they were skeptical) a plain clarification first | Their doubt | "Quick clarification: I'm not representing a sub and I'm not selling you data…" `[A02]` |
| The opportunity in their words, no names | What am I paying for? | "A facility in [city] consuming over 3.5M kWh a year… looking for a company experienced with [requirements]" `[A01]` |
| Why them, with evidence | Can you deliver? | "From what I found on [company], you've already done [X], which is why I came to you first" `[A02, A10]` |
| Fee and invoice | Price | "My fee for the qualified introduction is $[X]. Invoice attached" `[A02]` |
| Exactly what they get, and the scope boundary | What am I paying for? | "A direct email introduction with full context… you'll have their name, company and direct contact… That's my entire delivery; no meetings needed with me" `[A07, A08]` |
| The replacement clause | What if it doesn't work? | "If the opportunity materially differs from what I've described, I'll replace the introduction at no extra cost" `[A01]` |
| What happens next | After I pay? | "Once payment's taken care of, I'll make the introduction within 24 hours" `[A01]` |
| The transparency line (both sides pay) | Fairness | "For transparency, I'm also compensated independently by [the other side] for [their service]. This is a separate mandate…" `[A04]` |

**Optional additions:**

- **An ROI line,** e.g. "This isn't a one-off clean. It's her ongoing turnover work, week after week" `[A09]`.
- **A one-page thesis brief** for higher-value matches `[A04, A14]`.

**Free side, at the same time:**

> "I have a [type] that looks like a strong fit for what you described. I'm checking them now against [your requirements]. There's no fee or obligation on your side; my arrangement is with [the other side]. If it lines up, I'll introduce you directly [today / within 24 hours]." `[A01, A02, A07]`

Optional additions to the free-side note:

- the 30-second video ask `[A06, A10]`;
- a standing contact for capacity checks `[A12]`;
- the repeat-source line `[A05]`.

**After sending the invoice, don't chase** `[A07]`. At most one gentle note after a few days, carrying anything new.

## 7. After payment

1. "Got it, payment came through. I'm introducing you now" `[A10]`.
2. **The introduction:** both names, each side's needs and requirements in their own words, why it fits, "I'll leave it to you both from here." **No fee mentioned** `[A01, A08]`. Only after both sides have agreed to the handoff.
3. **Optional:** a thesis brief, or the supplier's price sheet attached `[A04, A11]`.
4. **Check in once** after about 24 hours: "How did the conversation with [B] go?" Then leave them alone `[A10]`.
5. **Track lightly:** introduced, met, became a project, rough value, usable as a case study.
6. **Offer continuation:** roll the first payment into a 60–90 day engagement `[A04]`; repeat sale: "Got another one that looks like your kind of thing. Want me to send it over?" `[A10]`.
7. **Replace** if it was clearly not a fit; **never refund-promise** beyond the stated terms.

## 8. Stop and escalate

**Stop:** on a no, an opt-out, or a second refusal on the commercial model; after the follow-up sequence ends; or when the counterpart has disappeared and there's no new information.

**Hand to a human** (when this framework runs inside an AI system):

- legal or regulatory questions;
- a requested contract or MSA;
- any fee above the market brief's range;
- harm-sensitive situations;
- the prospect asks whether they're talking to a person or AI (answer truthfully);
- anything not covered here.

**Never:**

- present the AI as a human, or invent a reason for writing;
- reveal names before payment and permission;
- invent buyers, suppliers, experience, results or urgency;
- argue after a second refusal;
- keep contacting someone who opted out.

## 9. Acceptance tests (for the future skill)

The skill passes when, given the real threads, it reproduces the right **move** (not necessarily the words) and never breaks a rule:

| Test | Input (case) | Expected move |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | A skeptic: "what's your angle, just tell me your fee" `[A02]` | A plain answer first, then the fee; no call push; no names |
| T2 | "Send your fees and facility name" `[A07]` | The fee and deliverable; withhold the name |
| T3 | "Have you worked in cannabis?" `[A15]` | The truth, plus why it's live, plus "When are you free?" |
| T4 | "Not interested in purchasing leads" `[A14]` | Reposition once; on a second pushback, pull away |
| T5 | A buyer listing three technical requirements `[A01]` | Verify each one; quote them back in the close |
| T6 | "Approved suppliers only" `[A16]` | "Don't want to assume, let me check" |
| T7 | An eager "tell me more" `[A17]` | One line plus "What are you looking to [do] right now?" |
| T8 | A licensing staffer replies `[A18]` | Ask for the best person |
| T9 | Both sides pre-sold `[A04]` | Two invoices for named different services, with transparency lines |
| T10 | Any first email for an E1 market | No "I have…" claim about the other side |
